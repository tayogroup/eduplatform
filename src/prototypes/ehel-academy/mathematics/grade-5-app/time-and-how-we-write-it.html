Time and How We Write It

Split a second into tenths, read the same moment in 12-hour and 24-hour clocks, work out an interval that runs past the hour, write a length of time as a decimal or in mixed units, and compare a clock here with a clock somewhere else in the world.

For the grown-up: what to have to hand

A clock with hands is worth more here than a digital one, because the 12-hour and 24-hour step asks the learner to see the same position twice in a day. A stopwatch or a phone timer for the race step - timing each other over a short distance makes hundredths of a second real rather than a printed number.

Support and challenge
An easier way in

For any interval that crosses the hour, always go up to the hour first and write that number down before going on. Two small additions are easier than one subtraction that has to borrow sixty, and the two hops can both be checked on a clock face.

A harder challenge

Step 46 compares Mogadishu with one city at a time. Pick three cities in different zones and find a single hour when it is a reasonable time to call in all three. Is there one? What is the largest gap you can find where there is not?

About

What this lesson is about

By the end of this lesson you will be able to…

  • Understand time intervals less than one second.
  • Compare times between time zones in digital notation (12- and 24-hour) and on analogue clock faces.
  • Find time intervals in seconds, minutes and hours that bridge through 60.
  • Recognise that a time interval can be expressed as a decimal, or in mixed units.
Lecture

Unit lecture

The whole lesson, in three parts.

Part 1 of 3

Sixty, not ten

Time is the one measure that does not carry at ten or at a hundred. Minutes carry at sixty and hours at twenty-four, which is why an interval across the hour is worked in two hops rather than subtracted like ordinary numbers.

Part 2 of 3

The same moment, written differently

A 24-hour clock needs no am or pm because it never repeats itself; after midday you add twelve to the hour. The same length of time can also be written in mixed units or as a decimal, and 0.5 hours is thirty minutes rather than fifty.

Part 3 of 3

Smaller than a second, and further than a country

A race is timed to hundredths, so the gap between two runners is often a fraction of one second. At the other end of the scale, the same moment is a different clock time in another time zone, sometimes on another day.

Step 42

Less than a second

A second is not the smallest piece of time. Races are timed to hundredths, and the gap between two runners is often far less than one second. Line the two finishes up and read the difference.

The finish times

Line up the decimal points, then subtract. 10.4 and 10.35 look as though the second is bigger because it has more digits. Writing 10.4 as 10.40 puts both on the same footing, and the gap is five hundredths of a second.
Step 43

The same time, written twice

A 24-hour clock never repeats itself, so it needs no am or pm. After midday you add 12 to the hour, which is the whole rule. Move the clock and watch both ways of writing it.

Both clocks

Write 16:45 the other way

Midnight and midday are the two that catch people out. 00:30 is half past midnight, not half past midday, and 12:30 is half past midday. Everything between 13:00 and 23:59 is the afternoon or evening with 12 added.
Step 44

Counting past the hour

Minutes do not carry at ten or at a hundred — they carry at sixty. So an interval that crosses the hour is easier in two hops: up to the hour, then on from it.

How long is that?

Do not subtract the digits as if they were ordinary numbers. From 09:50 to 10:20 is not 70 minutes, and it is not 30 either if you take 50 from 20 and ignore the borrow. Ten minutes gets you to 10:00, then twenty more: thirty in total.
Step 45

Half an hour, or 0.5 hours

The same length of time can be written in mixed units, as a fraction of an hour, or as a decimal. They are three ways of saying one thing, and a timetable or a wage slip may use any of them.

Choose a length of time

Three ways to write it

0.5 hours is thirty minutes, not fifty. The decimal is a fraction of SIXTY minutes, so 0.5 means half of sixty and 0.25 means a quarter of it. Reading the digits after the point as minutes is the mistake this step exists to stop.
Step 46

What time is it there?

Clocks around the world are set to different hours, so the same moment is a different time in each place. Pick a city and compare it with Mogadishu.

A time zone can push you into another day. When the difference carries past midnight, the other city is on yesterday or tomorrow. The panel says so when it happens, because a time with no day attached can be read twelve or twenty-four hours wrong.
