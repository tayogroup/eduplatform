# Grade 4 Computing (computing/grade-4-v2) validation

Reviewed 2026-09-11; its fixes are committed and NOT yet deployed. Reviewer: Claude (automated measurement + judgement); no human teacher review yet.  
Against the 27-area framework in 'computing-grades validation.docx'. Scale: 1 Unsatisfactory; 2 Needs major improvement; 3 Acceptable with revisions; 4 Strong; 5 Excellent.  
What was reviewed: the standalone lesson build that ehel-comp-g04 launches (computing/grade-4-app, served as app/computing/grade-4-v2), not the Word-pack course under computing/grade-4, which the shell serves when no override applies and which declares a different framework.  

The Word report beside the owner's other validation reports (OneDrive: `computing-grade-4-v2 validation report.docx`) carries the same content; both are written from one script.

## Summary

The build teaches all 39 Cambridge Primary Computing 0059 Stage 4 objectives across fourteen lessons. A check by hand of every objective against the steps that claim it found each one taught through something the child does, with two qualifications. 4CT.09 asks children to develop algorithms that give different outputs for different inputs; in the app they choose an input and follow the branch it takes, and only a home project has them write one. And lesson 4 teaches that idea by name, IF and otherwise, which Cambridge lists as selection at Stage 5. 95 of the 100 teaching steps can tell a child “not quite”, each of the 140 questions has one key and a reason, and every lesson was played to the end in a browser after this review's fixes, with no errors and nothing wider than a phone screen.

Grade 4 carries every shared fix so far, the Grade 3 validation's larger controls and shake picture included; it was redeployed with them earlier on 2026-09-11, and all fourteen lessons were played to the end on the live pages then: every step stored, every lesson recorded complete, and a reopened lesson opening where it was left.

Reading every learner-facing line found eighteen errors, all corrected. The one that taught something false: lesson 1's traffic-light round asked the child to correct a loop, and the “corrected” loop ran red, green, red and amber, amber, while the reason on screen said red and amber comes after red. Lesson 7's quiz had the lights “red, amber, green”. Others: a reason that did not follow (pack your bag after breakfast because “you pack the lunch you just made”); walking to school as the reason for more coat pegs, in two places; an oven timer offered as a control system, which has no sensor; the same five children with different ages in two tables of one lesson; Maya on a scooter one step after she came by car; driverless cars that will come “one day”; shuttles “at walking pace”; a phone that “has no cable socket”; three counts of steps that disagreed with each other; two explainers describing what was not on the page; “a cable plugged into the wall” as the definition of ethernet; an unplugged instruction that did not parse; and a rule, “never type a password where there is no padlock”, that forbids every password in the most-used browser, which replaced its padlock with a different icon in 2023.

Two things were missing and are added: 4CS.05 names games among the file types and the lesson had none, and the program repeat block, which holds one block, now says so beside the loops of lessons 1 and 4, which hold several. Two steps in lesson 8 shared a title; one is renamed. The home project that has a child follow the router's cables now names a grown-up.

The measurements found two things the reading could not. Five pictures needed 2020 devices, in 18 places (a lift, bubble tea, a bucket, a toothbrush, a pot plant); they are replaced. And the page for teachers and parents printed the 1 = a code as the unplugged version of the Caesar step and nothing for Pigpen; the shared kit now prints each cipher's own. Contrast passes everywhere it was measured, and no control is under 44 px.

What is left is content work and work no measurement can do. 4CT.09 needs an activity in which the child writes the algorithm, and the owner should decide whether Stage 4 keeps the word IF. There is no recap of the lesson before and no warm-up, which Grades 1 and 2 have. Several tasks repeat Grade 3: watering three plants, getting ready for school, bedtime, laying the table. A teacher needs to read the content and the question keys, somebody should listen to the cipher lesson's letter names and watch children use a lesson, and one should be walked with a screen reader. This review's fixes are committed and not deployed: the live Grade 4 pages carry the errors above until Grade 4 is redeployed.

Change status at 2026-09-11, fixes committed and not deployed: 8 items need no change or are done, 9 have their fix committed with a redeploy, a human check or an owner decision still open, 10 are open and not started.

Average score 4.0 / 5 across 27 areas; lowest 3 (Assessment Balance, Multimedia Quality, Duplication / overlap); 4 areas at 5.

## What was fixed during this review

Committed as `d2de1fb1f` on 2026-09-11 and pushed; NOT deployed. Grades 1 to 3 were rebuilt on the fixed kit and their pages are byte-identical to before.

- Traffic lights: lesson 1's correction round now puts the wrong step where fixing it gives red, red and amber, green, amber, which is what its reason says; lesson 7's question runs the lights in that order instead of “red, amber, green”.
- Reasons and facts: BAG comes after BREAKFAST because it “ends with your coat on and your bag in your hand”, not because “you pack the lunch you just made”; most children walking now means a safe crossing, not more coat pegs (lesson 8, two places); a water heater replaces an oven timer among control systems, which need a sensor; the class pets table has the same ages as the tables two steps later (and Amal's record in the demonstration says 9); the child on a scooter is Hana, since Maya came by car; cars park themselves and “in some cities already, drive themselves”; shuttles steer “slowly”; a phone has “no socket for a network cable”.
- Counts: the plant algorithm with a loop is “three steps and a count” and “five steps and a count instead of eleven”; four pancakes written out long are “twenty-two steps, mixing and serving included”; the checked loop is nine steps “with the can fetched once before and put away once after”.
- Explainers that described another page: DRESS now has the socks lesson 3's explainers list; lesson 5's explainer quotes the comment that is on the page, “Greet the player when the jumps are done”, instead of one about saying goodbye.
- Words a child would misread: “a cable plugged into the wall” is now “a network cable from the computer to the router” (lesson 10, a sort and its game); “Cover their eyes with the torch off” is now “Switch the torch on, then off: when it goes dark, they run the loop until you say Stop” (lesson 7).
- Added: a game file among the five file types, “from a few MB to 50 GB” (lesson 13, 4CS.05), with its question asking which of three is biggest; and one sentence in lesson 5 saying that this repeat block holds one block, where lessons 1 and 4's loops held several and many apps allow several.
- Safety: “Never type a password where there is no padlock” is now “Never type a password on a page that says Not secure”, with a note that some browsers show a different picture where the padlock was (lesson 11); the router home project names a grown-up (lesson 10).
- Titles: lesson 8's second “Data, or information?” is “Raw fact, or meaning?”.
- Emoji: five 2020 pictures in 18 places became older ones that say the same thing (a lift became a building, bubble tea a hot drink, a bucket a shower head for the watering can, a toothbrush a tooth, a pot plant a seedling).
- Shared kit (build-hub.py): a cipher step's unplugged version on the teachers' page is chosen by its cipher, so Caesar and Pigpen each get their own and the 1 = a code keeps Grade 3's. Only Grade 4's hub changes; Grades 1 to 3's pages are byte-identical.

## Verdict on Grade 4 Computing

Nothing found stops the children already using Grade 4. Its fourteen lessons were played to the end on the live pages today, and every record was complete. The errors this review found are corrected in the repository and wait for a redeploy; the one that taught something false was a traffic-light loop the child was told they had fixed. The build teaches all 39 Stage 4 objectives through activities.

Two questions go beyond fixing. 4CT.09 is followed more than it is developed, and lesson 4's IF is Stage 5 vocabulary a year early; the second is the owner's call. Beyond that, a recap and a warm-up, fresh tasks where Grade 3's routines return, and the human checks: a teacher's read of the content and the keys, a listen to the ciphers, a child watched, and a screen-reader walk-through.
