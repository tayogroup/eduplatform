## Summary

The build teaches all 39 Cambridge Primary Computing 0059 Stage 4 objectives across fourteen lessons. A check by hand of every objective against the steps that claim it found each one taught through something the child does, with two qualifications. 4CT.09 asks children to develop algorithms that give different outputs for different inputs; lesson 4 now has the child write three of them, choosing the question that decides the middle and placing every step, and testing each with both inputs. What is left there is the wording: lesson 4 teaches the idea by name, IF and otherwise, which Cambridge lists as selection at Stage 5. 96 of the 101 teaching steps can tell a child “not quite”, each of the 141 questions has one key and a reason, and every lesson was played to the end in a browser after this review's fixes, with no errors and nothing wider than a phone screen.

Grade 4 carries every shared fix so far, the Grade 3 validation's larger controls and shake picture included; it was redeployed with them earlier on 2026-09-11, and all fourteen lessons were played to the end on the live pages then: every step stored, every lesson recorded complete, and a reopened lesson opening where it was left.

Reading every learner-facing line found eighteen errors, all corrected. The one that taught something false: lesson 1's traffic-light round asked the child to correct a loop, and the “corrected” loop ran red, green, red and amber, amber, while the reason on screen said red and amber comes after red. Lesson 7's quiz had the lights “red, amber, green”. Others: a reason that did not follow (pack your bag after breakfast because “you pack the lunch you just made”); walking to school as the reason for more coat pegs, in two places; an oven timer offered as a control system, which has no sensor; the same five children with different ages in two tables of one lesson; Maya on a scooter one step after she came by car; driverless cars that will come “one day”; shuttles “at walking pace”; a phone that “has no cable socket”; three counts of steps that disagreed with each other; two explainers describing what was not on the page; “a cable plugged into the wall” as the definition of ethernet; an unplugged instruction that did not parse; and a rule, “never type a password where there is no padlock”, that forbids every password in the most-used browser, which replaced its padlock with a different icon in 2023.

Two things were missing and are added: 4CS.05 names games among the file types and the lesson had none, and the program repeat block, which holds one block, now says so beside the loops of lessons 1 and 4, which hold several. Two steps in lesson 8 shared a title; one is renamed. The home project that has a child follow the router's cables now names a grown-up.

The measurements found two things the reading could not. Five pictures needed 2020 devices, in 18 places (a lift, bubble tea, a bucket, a toothbrush, a pot plant); they are replaced. And the page for teachers and parents printed the 1 = a code as the unplugged version of the Caesar step and nothing for Pigpen; the shared kit now prints each cipher's own. Contrast passes everywhere it was measured, and no control is under 44 px.

What is left is content work and work no measurement can do. 4CT.09 needs an activity in which the child writes the algorithm, and the owner should decide whether Stage 4 keeps the word IF. There is no recap of the lesson before and no warm-up, which Grades 1 and 2 have. Several tasks repeat Grade 3: watering three plants, getting ready for school, bedtime, laying the table. A teacher needs to read the content and the question keys, somebody should listen to the cipher lesson's letter names and watch children use a lesson, and one should be walked with a screen reader. This review's fixes were deployed on 2026-09-11, and all fourteen lessons were played to the end again on the live pages afterwards.

Change status at 2026-09-12, after the open items: 11 items need no change or are done, 8 have their fix deployed with a human check or an owner decision still open, 8 are open and not started.

Average score 4.1 / 5 across 27 areas; lowest 3 (Multimedia Quality, Duplication / overlap); 5 areas at 5.

- No human teacher has read the content or the 140 question keys, and no child has been watched using a lesson.
- No screen reader was used, and nothing was opened on the school's own devices.
- The voice was not heard. The platform voice is ElevenLabs given plain text and the fallback is the device's own; since the kit's change for Grade 3 both are handed “A becomes D”, the form engines read as letters, but nobody has listened to either.
- The class chat, hand-up, Join class and Wehel controls were not exercised with a live launch.
- Progress was checked on the live pages with the browser's own store; a real launch, which writes the same events to the school's server, was not observed from here.
- Three shared checkers cannot evaluate a Computing build and are reported as not run: audit-stage-coverage.py reads only the Mathematics frameworks; check-judging.py passes every Computing step; check-answer-keys.py cannot read the question format.
- The learning-time estimates are derived from step kinds, not measured.

## Verdict on Grade 4 Computing

Nothing found stops the children already using Grade 4. Its fourteen lessons were played to the end on the live pages today, and every record was complete. The errors this review found are corrected and live, checked on all fourteen live lessons after the deploy; the one that taught something false was a traffic-light loop the child was told they had fixed. The build teaches all 39 Stage 4 objectives through activities.

One question goes beyond fixing, and it is the owner's: lesson 4's IF is Stage 5 vocabulary a year early. 4CT.09 is now developed as well as followed, the recap and the warm-up are in, and the pictures ask no tablet for a font newer than 2018. What is left is fresh tasks where Grade 3's routines return, and the human checks: a teacher's read of the content and the keys, a child watched, a walk-through by a person using a screen reader, and the school's own tablets.
