## Overview

Grade 4 English is the year a learner starts using English on someone else's
behalf — interviewing, reporting, explaining, presenting. It follows **Cambridge
Primary English 0058, Stage 4**.

Ten units run across three terms. Unit 1 sets the pattern: twenty words, six
grammar lessons covering verb patterns, adverbs with the present simple, *have
got*, Wh- questions, imperatives and conjunctions, four or five texts to read —
Amal interviewing Omar at the town post counter — and then the learner conducts
an interview of their own. The year moves through daily life and communication,
nature and weather, food and health, community, action and movement, people in
society, emotions and identity, tools and machines, and places and plans.

Across the year a learner meets **212 words**, reads **46 texts**, and works
through **76 learning outcomes**. Unit 10 is an exhibition: a six-page display
board, a two-minute talk given to an audience, a corrected draft carrying a
partner's feedback, and a written reflection naming a goal for Grade 5.

Two habits define the year. Writing is **revised inside the unit** — Unit 1's
interview report is drafted in Writing 1 and revised once in Writing 5 before it
is ever submitted — and every marked piece comes back with **one written next
step**. A learner who treats the first draft as the finished piece is not doing
Grade 4 English.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Grade 4 assumes Grades 1 to 3. There is no foundation unit inside this course; a
learner who has not met the earlier material is pointed back to it.

Before Unit 1 every learner sits the **Grade 4 English Placement Exam**: 30
questions, 30 marks, about 25 minutes, in three sections —

- **Grade 3 skills** (18 questions) — the most important words, reading and
  grammar from Grade 3.
- **Grade 2 skills** (7 questions) — key words, sounds and sentences.
- **Early foundations, Grades 1–2** (5 questions) — first sounds, first words,
  first sentences.

Attempts are unlimited and it is never a fail. Four results:

- **Ready for Grade 4** — 80% overall with at least 60% in every section.
- **Ready, with a little review** — 50% or more; start at Unit 1 after the review
  lessons the report names.
- **Grade 3 skills below 40%** — that section is treated as critical, and the
  report routes to Grade 3 Units 3, 6 and 9 (*Time and Daily Life*, *Describing
  People and Things*, *Thinking, Feelings, and Imagination*) whatever the overall
  score.
- **Let's build strong roots first** — the report recommends **Grade 3 English**
  as the course to take.

The review lessons the report names open directly, in whichever earlier grade
they belong to, even where that grade is otherwise walked one unit at a time.
That is deliberate: the point of a targeted report is that a learner rebuilds the
specific thing they lost, so the links reach it.

The exam can be retaken at any point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- A writing book or folder that keeps **drafts**. Revision happens twice over in
  this course — once inside the unit before submission, once after the teacher's
  next step — and the Unit 10 capstone requires the corrected draft, with a
  partner's feedback notes on it, handed in beside the finished board.
- Materials for a **six-page display board** in Unit 10: card or paper, glue,
  colours. It is displayed and talked to, not filed, so plan for something that
  stands up.
- A way to record audio. Every unit assignment includes a spoken piece.
- A device that plays audio, for the readings and recorded vocabulary.
- An internet connection for live sessions.

### Attendance

The course runs **60 live sessions** — 6 per unit, all 45 minutes.

Sessions expect preparation, and it is specific: each names the vocabulary group
to learn beforehand (Unit 1, session 1: *mail, language, citizen, agree,
continue*). The session is built on the assumption that was done.

Attendance is expected at all sessions; tell the teacher in advance where
possible. Sessions are recorded and a missed one should be watched before the
next. More than two missed in a unit and the teacher will contact the family to
agree how to catch up.

Unit 10 is the exception worth naming: the capstone talk is **given to an
audience**. A learner who misses that session has missed the assessment, not a
lesson, so tell the teacher early if the date is a problem.

### Homework

Each unit carries **six activities** and **one assignment** — **10 assignments**
across the year.

A typical assignment is two pieces: one written, already revised once inside the
unit, and one recording. Unit 1 asks for the interview report from Writing 1
revised using Writing 5, plus a one-minute recorded talk about a routine. The
teacher marks both against the rubrics and returns **one written next step for
each piece**.

So the loop has three beats, not two: draft, self-revise, then marked return.
Expect **45–60 minutes** on an assignment across the unit's two weeks, plus the
revision.

The Unit 10 capstone is four submissions: the finished display board, the
corrected draft carrying **a partner's three feedback notes**, the recording of
the two-minute talk, and a four-sentence reflection naming a Grade 5 goal. It
depends on a partner's work, so it cannot be left to the last week — someone
else is waiting on it.

### Assessment and grading

**Assignments — 352 marks across the year.** Nine unit assignments at 32 marks,
the Unit 10 capstone at 64.

A unit assignment splits **16 writing, 16 speaking**, each on a four-level
rubric.

- **Writing** — Ideas, Organization, Language, Conventions, 4 marks each.
- **Speaking** — Clarity, Vocabulary, Sentence accuracy, Interaction, 4 marks
  each.

What earns full marks is named in the task, not left to impression. Unit 1's
writing marks look for the person named, three duties listed, past simple used
for what they said, and one honest closing sentence; its speaking marks look for
two correctly placed adverbs, complete sentences and clear delivery.

The capstone's 64 marks are four blocks of 16 — the board, the recording, the
reflection, and the corrected draft, where the draft's own 16 are 4 for acting on
peer feedback, 4 for accurate verb forms across present, past and future, 4 for
varied connectives and multi-clause sentences, and 4 for punctuation.

**Unit quizzes — 100 questions across the year**, ten per unit.

**Final course quiz.** After the capstone: 30 questions, 30 marks, about 30
minutes, in three sections — Words and Meaning, Reading and Language,
Communication and Application. Pass mark **80%**.

> **Attempts are set by the school, not by the course.** The course records the
> rule as "teacher or school policy" and does not choose one. State it here so a

> fail}}.

**Self-assessment.** Six *I can…* statements per unit, rated **Not yet / With
help / By myself**. No marks. The middle option is how a learner reports needing
support without reporting failure.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Come prepared** — the named vocabulary group before the session.
- **Revise before you submit.** The unit builds a revision step into the writing;
  handing in the first draft skips work that was set.
- **Take the next step seriously.** Every marked piece returns one written next
  step, and acting on feedback is worth marks outright in the capstone.
- **Give a partner honest, specific feedback.** The capstone requires three
  feedback notes from a partner, and a learner's own mark depends on the notes
  they received being worth acting on.
- **Speak in complete sentences**, and interact — answering a follow-up properly
  is assessed, not just politeness.
- **Present to the room** in Unit 10, to an audience, for two minutes.

### Support and communication

**If a learner is struggling**, the placement exam can be retaken at any point,
and its report names the specific earlier units to revisit — those links open
those units directly. Grade 3 English remains available as a course for a learner
who needs the ground under this one.

**Extra help available:**

- The teacher, at the contact above.
- Wehel, the in-course AI tutor, available while the learner is working.
- Recorded audio for the vocabulary and the readings.

**How the school will contact you:** when marked work is returned, after a missed
assignment, after two missed live sessions in a unit, and at the end of each
term.

**If you have a concern**, contact the teacher first. If it is unresolved after
{{n}} school days, contact {{school administrator role/name}}.

**For English as an additional language, or any additional need**, tell the
teacher at the start of the year. The revision step is the easiest place in this
course to add support without lowering what is expected.
