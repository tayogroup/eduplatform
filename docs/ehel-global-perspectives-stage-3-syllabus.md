## Overview

Stage 3 Global Perspectives is the last guided year, and its questions have edges: fairness, rules, age, welcome. It follows **Cambridge Primary Global Perspectives 0838, Stage 3**.

Five units — *We can build it!*, *Rule Makers*, *Older and Wiser*, *A Fair Life!*, *You are welcome!*

Across the year: **5 units**, **38 learning outcomes**, **41 explainers**,
**12 speaking prompts** and **15 reflection prompts**.

**This is a guided course, and the grown-up is part of the design.** Every unit
carries a **grown-up guide**, kept whole and in its own voice rather than
rewritten into learner prose — 5 of 5 units have one. The learner's own
teaching comes from the activity sheet, which is already written to a child; the
guide stays as a letter to the adult, because that is what it is.

**Assessment is by project, not by quiz.** These units carry no challenge
questions at all — the course records assessment as *project*. What a learner produces is the evidence.

**There are no live sessions, no assignments to hand in and no marks.**

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Stage 3 assumes Stages 1 to 2, though the skills spiral rather than stack — a learner joining here meets Research from the beginning of this stage's treatment of it.

Before Unit 1 a learner sits the **Stage 3 Global Perspectives Placement Exam**: 28 questions, 28 marks, about 25 minutes. Attempts are unlimited and it is never a fail.

Sections:

- **Stage 2 skills** (17 questions)
- **Stage 1 foundations** (11 questions)

The lowest band recommends **Stage 2 Global Perspectives**.
The check can be retaken at any point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- **An adult who can sit with the learner.** This is not optional at this stage — the course is built around it.
- A device that can display the course, with **sound** — the narration is
  complete and is part of the teaching.
- An internet connection.
- Household objects the activity sheets name — nothing needs buying.

### Attendance

**This course has no live sessions.** What replaces attendance is a rhythm the
school sets: {{how often a learner is expected to work, and how progress is
checked}}.

### Homework

There is no homework to submit.

Each unit works through its explainers, its speaking prompts and its activities with an adult, and finishes on a **project**. The project is the assessment, so it is the part that must not be skipped.

### Assessment and grading

**There are no marks in this course.**

These units carry **no challenge questions**. Assessment is the project — the course records it as *project*, judged by the adult who worked through the unit with the learner.

> **A decision for the school.** A project judged at home by a parent is evidence
> of a kind, but it is not moderated. Say what the school does with it:
> {{who sees the project, whether it is reviewed, and what a family is told}}.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Ask before you answer.** This subject is about how a view is formed, not
  which view is held.
- **Disagree properly.** The speaking prompts ask a learner to hear someone else's answer before giving their own.
- **The grown-up guide is for the adult**, not the child. It is written in its own voice on purpose.
- **Say where things came from.** Research is a graded skill here from Stage 4
  onward.
