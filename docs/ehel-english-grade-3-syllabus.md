## Overview

Grade 3 English is the year English stops being about naming things and starts
being about saying something. It follows **Cambridge Primary English 0058,
Stage 3**.

Ten units run across three terms. Each opens with its vocabulary in small
groups, moves through six grammar lessons, and then asks the learner to read
five texts — Unit 1 ends with the story *Amal's Big Day* — before writing and
speaking about them. The subjects widen as the year goes: family and the values
that hold a household together, school and learning, time and daily routine,
the places and people of a community, actions, describing people precisely,
nature and climate, the language of mathematics, and finally the world inside
your head — thinking, feeling and imagining.

Across the year a learner meets **168 words**, reads **50 texts**, and works
through **73 learning outcomes**. Unit 10 gathers the year up: thirty words
revised from Units 1 to 8, a six-page booklet, a showcase presentation, and a
written reflection that names a goal for Grade 4.

The shift from the earlier grades is in how work is finished. A Grade 3 piece is
written, marked against a rubric, returned with **one improvement target**, and
then redrafted. The redraft is the point. A learner is expected to work
independently through the unit and to bring finished work to be marked, rather
than to be walked through it.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Grade 3 assumes Grades 1 and 2. Unlike the earlier grades there is **no
foundation unit inside this course** — a learner who has not met the earlier
material is pointed back to it rather than caught up here.

Before Unit 1 every learner sits the **Grade 3 English Placement Exam**: 28
questions, 28 marks, about 25 minutes, in two sections — **Grade 2 skills** (17
questions) and **Grade 1 foundations** (11). Attempts are unlimited and it is
never a fail; it finds a starting point. It reports one of four results:

- **Ready for Grade 3** — 80% overall with at least 60% in each section. Start
  at Unit 1.
- **Ready, with a little review** — 50% or more. Start at Unit 1 after the
  review lessons the report names.
- **Grade 2 skills below 40%** — the exam treats this section as critical and
  routes to three specific Grade 2 units regardless of the overall score:
  *Welcome and Calendar*, *Let's Measure*, and *Home, Sweet Home*.
- **Let's build strong roots first** — the report recommends **Grade 2 English**
  as the course to take, and says plainly that a grown-up or teacher should help
  choose the path.

That last result is a course change, not a study plan, so it needs a
conversation with the family rather than a link. The exam can be retaken at any
point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- A writing book or folder that holds **drafts as well as finished work**. Marked
  drafts are resubmitted in this course, and the Unit 10 capstone requires the
  marked revision draft to be handed in alongside the final booklet.
- Pens or pencils, and something to make a six-page booklet with in Unit 10 —
  paper, scissors, glue.
- A way to record audio. Every unit assignment includes a spoken piece, and the
  recording must be audible throughout to earn full speaking marks.
- A device that plays audio, for the readings and the recorded vocabulary.
- An internet connection for live sessions.
- A dictionary is not required. The course carries its own for Grade 3.

### Attendance

The course runs **60 live sessions** — 6 per unit, every one of them 45 minutes.
Sessions are evenly spread, so a missed session is a sixth of a unit's teaching.

Sessions expect preparation. Each carries a *before the session* instruction —
typically to read the unit overview and learn the vocabulary group — and the
session is built on the assumption it was done. A learner who arrives without
the words has a different lesson from the one that was planned.

Attendance is expected at all sessions. Tell the teacher in advance where
possible. Sessions are recorded, and a missed one should be watched before the
next session rather than left to accumulate. If a learner misses more than two
sessions in a unit, the teacher will contact the family to agree how to catch
up.

### Homework

Each unit carries **six activities** and **one assignment**, and across the year
that is **10 assignments**.

An assignment is not a worksheet. A typical one asks the learner to submit two
written pieces and one recording — for Unit 1, two pieces of writing and a
recording of *Introduce Your Family*. The teacher marks it against the rubrics,
returns **one improvement target per piece**, and the learner redrafts.

Plan for that loop rather than for a single deadline: draft, marked return,
redraft. Expect **45–60 minutes** on an assignment across the two weeks of a
unit, plus the redraft.

The Unit 10 capstone is four submissions in one: the finished six-page booklet,
the **marked revision draft**, the showcase presentation recording, and a
four-sentence reflection naming a Grade 4 goal. It also draws on peer feedback,
so it cannot be left to the last week.

Keep every marked draft. The capstone asks for one back.

### Assessment and grading

**Assignments — 352 marks across the year.** Nine unit assignments at 32 marks
each, and the Unit 10 capstone at 64.

A unit assignment splits evenly: **16 marks on writing, 16 on speaking**, each
against a four-level rubric.

- **Writing** is judged on Ideas, Organization, Language and Conventions — 4
  marks each. Level 1 is *ideas are not yet clear*; level 4 is *clear, developed
  and engaging ideas*.
- **Speaking** is judged on Clarity, Vocabulary, Sentence accuracy and
  Interaction — 4 marks each.

Full marks are specific rather than impressionistic. Writing earns them only
when every sentence is punctuated, at least five unit words are used correctly,
and the piece explains a reason with *because*. Speaking earns them only when
the recording is audible throughout and the learner answers a follow-up question
in a complete sentence.

The capstone's 64 marks are four blocks of 16: the booklet, the recording, the
revision draft (acting on peer feedback, verb forms, joining words, punctuation
including speech marks), and the reflection.

**Unit quizzes — 100 questions across the year**, ten per unit.

**Final course quiz.** After the Unit 10 capstone: 30 questions, 30 marks, about
30 minutes, in three sections — Words and Meaning, Reading and Language, and
Communication and Application. The pass mark is **80%**.

> **Attempts are set by the school, not by the course.** The course records the
> attempt rule as "teacher or school policy" and does not choose one, unlike the
> earlier grades where the final is unlimited. State the rule here so a learner
> knows it before sitting: {{attempts allowed, and what happens on a first fail}}.

**Self-assessment.** Each unit ends with six *I can…* statements rated **Not yet
/ With help / By myself**. They carry no marks. The middle option matters: it is
how a learner reports needing support without reporting failure.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Come prepared.** The vocabulary group before the session, as the session
  instructions ask.
- **Speak in full sentences.** The speaking rubric marks sentence accuracy and
  interaction, so answering a follow-up question properly is part of the work,
  not politeness.
- **Take feedback as instruction.** Every marked piece comes back with one
  improvement target. Acting on it is explicitly worth marks in the capstone.
- **Give useful peer feedback.** The capstone revision draft is assessed partly
  on acting on feedback from another learner, which only works if the feedback
  given is honest and specific.
- **Take turns and listen** in live sessions.

This year's vocabulary includes respect, honour, duty, kindness and behaving
well in public and in private. The course teaches those words; the class is
where they are practised.

### Support and communication

**If a learner is struggling**, the placement exam can be retaken at any point
and its report names the specific earlier units to revisit. Grade 2 English
remains available as a course for a learner who needs the ground under this one.

**Extra help available:**

- The teacher, at the contact above.
- Wehel, the in-course AI tutor, available while the learner is working.
- Recorded audio for the vocabulary and the readings.

**How the school will contact you:** when marked work is returned, after a
missed assignment, after two missed live sessions in a unit, and at the end of
each term.

**If you have a concern**, contact the teacher first. If it is unresolved after
{{n}} school days, contact {{school administrator role/name}}.

**For English as an additional language, or any additional need**, tell the
teacher at the start of the year. The redraft loop is the easiest place in this
course to adjust support without changing what is expected.

## Before submitting for approval

1. Fill every `{{...}}`, including the final-quiz attempt rule — that one is a
   school decision the course deliberately leaves open, not an oversight.
