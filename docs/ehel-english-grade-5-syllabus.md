## Overview

Grade 5 English is the year the subject becomes about craft. Where earlier years
asked a learner to say something, this one asks how a thing is made and then has
them make one. It follows **Cambridge Primary English 0058, Stage 5**.

Ten units run across three terms, each built on a form of writing. Unit 1 asks
what stories are for, through the fable *The Camel and the Sparrow* and *The
Silence After the Rumble*, in which Amal sweeps the steps of a courthouse in a
town rebuilding after an earthquake. The year then moves through non-fiction
about space, reflective writing, myths and legends, instructions, the features
that separate adventure from mystery from realistic fiction, retelling a story
from another character's viewpoint, persuasive writing, and finally scripts —
*The Forest Guardians* read first as a play and then as a film, stage directions
against camera directions.

Across the year a learner meets **268 words**, reads **49 texts**, and works
through **76 learning outcomes**. Unit 10 gathers it up: thirty words revised
from Units 1 to 8, six grammar workshops, and a multi-section product built for
**a real audience**, presented and reflected on.

The habit that defines Grade 5 is self-marking. The writing and speaking rubrics
are printed inside each unit, and the assignment asks the learner to **revise
against them before submitting** — the same four criteria, in the same words,
that the teacher will then use. A learner finishes the year able to judge their
own work against a standard rather than waiting to be told.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Grade 5 assumes Grades 1 to 4. There is no foundation unit inside this course.

Before Unit 1 every learner sits the **Grade 5 English Placement Exam**: 32
questions, 32 marks, about 30 minutes — the longest of the primary placements,
because there are four prior years to place against. Three sections:

- **Grade 4 skills** (19 questions)
- **Grade 3 skills** (7 questions)
- **Foundations, Grades 1–2** (6 questions)

Attempts are unlimited and it is never a fail. Four results:

- **Ready for Grade 5** — 80% overall with at least 60% in every section.
- **Ready, with a little review** — 50% or more; start at Unit 1 after the review
  lessons the report names.
- **Grade 4 skills below 40%** — treated as critical, and the report routes to
  Grade 4 Units 3, 5 and 7 (*Food and Health*, *Action and Movement*, *Emotions,
  Behaviour, and Identity*) whatever the overall score.
- **Let's build strong roots first** — the report recommends **Grade 4 English**
  as the course to take.

The review lessons open directly in whichever earlier grade they belong to, even
where that grade is otherwise walked one unit at a time — including the Grade 1
and Grade 2 units the foundations section points at.

The exam can be retaken at any point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- A writing folder that keeps **drafts and planning frames**. Unit 7 requires the
  planning frame to be submitted alongside the finished retelling, not just the
  final piece.
- Materials for the Unit 10 product. It is multi-section and made for a real
  audience, so what it needs depends on what the learner chooses to make —
  settle that with the teacher early rather than in the final week.
- A way to record audio. Every unit assignment includes a spoken piece, and
  several run to three minutes.
- A device that plays audio, for the readings and recorded vocabulary.
- An internet connection for live sessions.

### Attendance

The course runs **60 live sessions** — 6 per unit, all 45 minutes.

Preparation before a session is now reading, not just vocabulary. Unit 1's first
session asks the learner to skim *What Makes a Fable?* and **bring a fable they
already know**. Arriving without it changes what the session can do, because the
learner's own example is the material.

Attendance is expected at all sessions; tell the teacher in advance where
possible. Sessions are recorded and a missed one should be watched before the
next. More than two missed in a unit and the teacher will contact the family to
agree how to catch up.

### Homework

Each unit carries **six activities** and **one assignment** — **10 assignments**
across the year.

Most units ask for two pieces, one written and one recorded; four ask for three.
The written work is **revised against the printed rubrics before it is
submitted**, which is set work, not a suggestion.

Unit 1 is the fullest example: the learner's own fable, eight adjective-and-
preposition sentences, and a recording of a three-minute talk with three
signposted sections. Unit 7 asks for a retelling in another character's voice
plus the planning frame behind it, and a recording answering at least four
questions **in character**.

Expect **45–60 minutes** on an assignment across the unit's two weeks, plus the
revision pass.

### Assessment and grading

**Assignments — 352 marks across the year.** Nine unit assignments at 32 marks,
the Unit 10 capstone at 64.

The usual shape is **16 writing, 16 speaking**, each on a four-level rubric —
Writing judged on Ideas, Organization, Language and Conventions; Speaking on
Clarity, Vocabulary, Sentence accuracy and Interaction, four marks per criterion.

**Unit 1 is the exception, and it is the only one:** 16 marks for the fable, 8
for the eight adjective-and-preposition sentences at one mark each, and 8 for the
talk. If a learner or family compares Unit 1's marks against a later unit's,
that is why.

The standards are named rather than left to impression. A level-four blog in Unit
2 keeps a friendly informal voice throughout, underlines five unit words used
accurately, marks four indefinite pronouns with matching verbs, and closes by
encouraging the reader. Unit 1's fable needs animal characters, one clear
problem, dialogue in speech marks, and a moral that matches the plot.

One marking rule is worth stating to families outright: where a task invites
personal content — Unit 1's sentences about how the learner feels — **marks are
not deducted for what the learner chose to write about**. The mark is for the
English.

**Unit quizzes — 100 questions across the year**, ten per unit.

**Final course quiz.** After the capstone: 30 questions, 30 marks, about 30
minutes, in three sections — Words and Meaning, Reading and Language,
Communication and Application. Pass mark **80%**.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Come prepared**, including anything the session asks you to bring.
- **Revise against the rubric before submitting.** It is printed in the unit for
  that purpose, and skipping it means handing in work the learner has not yet
  judged.
- **Stay in role where the task asks for it.** Unit 7's recording is answered in
  character, and marks depend on staying inside it.
- **Take turns and listen**; answering a follow-up properly is assessed.
- **An adult must be present whenever a learner records audio, presents online,
  or uses the Wehel AI tutor.** This applies in every unit of Grade 5. It is a
  supervision rule, not a comment on independence — the work itself is the
  learner's own.

### Support and communication

**If a learner is struggling**, the placement exam can be retaken at any point
and its report names the specific earlier units to revisit; those links open
those units directly. Grade 4 English remains available as a course for a learner
who needs the ground under this one.

**Extra help available:**

- The teacher, at the contact above.
- Wehel, the in-course AI tutor — with an adult present, as above.
- Recorded audio for the vocabulary and the readings.
- For a learner who writes slowly, the course allows a spoken response in place
  of a written one on some tasks. Ask the teacher rather than assuming it.

**How the school will contact you:** when marked work is returned, after a
missed assignment, after two missed live sessions in a unit, and at the end of
each term. The capstone is conferenced at each milestone rather than collected
at the end.

**If you have a concern**, contact the teacher first. If it is unresolved after
{{n}} school days, contact {{school administrator role/name}}.

**For English as an additional language, or any additional need**, tell the
teacher at the start of the year.
