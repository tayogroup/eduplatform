# Grade 1 Computing (computing/grade-1-v2) validation

Reviewed 2026-09-11. Reviewer: Claude (automated measurement + judgement); no human teacher review yet.  
Against the 27-area framework in 'computing-grades validation.docx', which is unchanged and remains the criteria source for Grades 2 to 4. Scale: 1 Unsatisfactory; 2 Needs major improvement; 3 Acceptable with revisions; 4 Strong; 5 Excellent.  
What was reviewed: the standalone lesson build that ehel-comp-g01 launches (computing/grade-1-app, served as app/computing/grade-1-v2), not the Word-pack course under computing/grade-1, which the shell serves when no override applies and which declares a different framework.  

The Word report beside the owner's other validation reports (OneDrive: `computing-grade-1-v2 validation report.docx`) carries the same content; both are written from one script.

## Summary

The build teaches all 28 Cambridge Primary Computing 0059 Stage 1 objectives across eight lessons. A check by hand of every objective against the steps that claim it found each one taught through something the child does, and no step claiming an objective it does not teach. 55 of its 64 teaching steps can tell a child “not quite”, each of its 81 questions has one key and a reason, and every lesson was played to the end in a browser twice today with no errors and nothing wider than a phone screen.

This review found one defect that was costing every learner something. It is fixed in the repository and not yet deployed. The lesson overview and the “Computing world” placeholder ticked themselves off while the page was still loading, before the progress module existed. Measured on the live bundle: a lesson finished to 100% on screen was stored as 14 of 16 steps and never recorded as complete, a reopened lesson always started again at step 1, and a fresh lesson opened at 13%. Each Grade 1 lesson is estimated at 40 to 45 minutes against a 20-minute app block, so every lesson takes at least two sittings, and the second one always began from the start. The fix is in the shared Computing kit, so it also covers Grades 2 to 4, which had the same defect. It was verified on a local copy of the deployed layout: all eight Grade 1 lessons store every step and are recorded complete, and a reopened lesson opens where it was left.

Reading every learner-facing line found eight factual errors, all corrected. The worst was the popular myth that the word “bug” comes from the 1947 moth, told in six places and keyed as the right answer to a quiz question. The others were a treasure map called a program against the course's own definition, “a computer cannot read our sentences”, a rocket bug that “cannot be fixed later”, and three smaller overstatements. Two text colours below the 4.5:1 contrast minimum and three emoji that older tablets draw as empty boxes were also fixed, and the same measurement found and fixed an almost invisible loop label in Grade 4.

What is left is mostly work no measurement can do: a teacher reading the content and the 81 question keys, a screen-reader walk-through, watching children use it, and opening it on the school's own tablets. There is also one decision. All of today's fixes are committed and not deployed, and the live pages of all four Computing grades carry the progress defect until they are redeployed.

Change status at 2026-09-11: 11 need no change or are done, 6 have their fix committed with a deploy or a human check still open, 10 are open and not started.

Average score 4.1 / 5 across 27 areas; lowest 3 (Assessment Balance, Multimedia Quality); five areas at 5. Before this review's fixes, Learner Progress and Completion would have scored 2 and Content Accuracy 3.

## What the earlier verification could not see

Every earlier verification of this build played each lesson to 100% on a local server. That server deliberately does not serve the progress module, so no earlier check ever looked at what a finished lesson leaves in the school's record. “100% in the browser” was true of the page and false of the record, in every Computing lesson, from the first release on 2026-09-10 until this review.

## Required changes, by priority

- **High.** Redeploy Grades 1 to 4 (area 22). The progress fix is in the shared kit and committed; the live pages of every Computing grade still lose completion and resume until they are redeployed.
- **Medium.** A teacher's read of the eight content modules and the 81 question keys (areas 1, 4, 10, 19); a screen-reader walk-through (15); two lessons opened on the school's own tablets, including the nine 2019 glyphs (18, 21); watching two or three children use a lesson (13); committing the browser driver with a check on the stored record (27).
- **Low.** A second activity for 1CS.02 and 1MD.03 and a judging step for 1DC.02 (3); a one-frame recap of the previous lesson (9); a diagnostic opener and a decision on whether a score gates completion (11); timing two children (17); a proofread (19); self-hosted fonts (20); splitting the kit's JavaScript by stage (21); a decision on the placeholder (23); 44 px targets and a 13 px minimum text size (15).

## What was fixed during this review

Committed as `04a1ff89f` on 2026-09-11 and pushed; NOT deployed. Grades 2 to 4 were rebuilt on the fixed kit with their lesson data byte-identical to before.

- Progress: the overview and Computing world no longer tick themselves during page load. A finished lesson is now stored complete, a reopened lesson opens where it was left, and a fresh lesson opens at 0%. The fix is in the shared Computing kit (lib/computing.js), so it covers all four grades.
- The “bug” story, told as it happened in six places: people already called a machine's mistakes bugs, then found a real moth and taped it into their notebook, which made the word famous. The quiz question now asks what was found, not where the word came from.
- A treasure map is “an algorithm”, not a program; a computer “needs the steps written as code” rather than “cannot read our sentences” (four places); a rocket bug “cannot be fixed while the rocket is flying”; the web-search, spinning-circle and toaster explanations no longer overstate.
- Contrast: the sorting machine's caption (4.04:1) and the phone-screen hint (4.21:1) now pass at 5.0:1 and 5.1:1; Grade 4's loop label (1.29:1) now inherits the page's ink.
- Emoji: toothbrush (2020) became a tooth, a potted plant (2020) a jar for the step “get a pot”, an X-ray (2021) a hospital.

## What this review could not establish

- No human teacher has read the content or the 81 question keys, and no child has been watched using a lesson.
- No screen reader was used, and nothing was opened on the school's own devices.
- The class chat, hand-up, Join class and Wehel controls were not exercised with a live launch.
- The progress fix was verified on a local copy of the deployed layout (the built pages plus the five platform modules the deploy puts beside them), not on the CDN, because it is not deployed.
- Three shared checkers cannot evaluate a Computing build, and are reported as not run rather than as passes: audit-stage-coverage.py reads only the Mathematics frameworks (it reported “0 of 36” against 0096); check-judging.py passes every Computing step because every step carries an element it takes for a question; check-answer-keys.py cannot read the question format and refuses.
- The learning-time estimates are derived from step kinds, not measured.

Outside this review: Science Grade 1's live pages have the same mechanism. On alive-or-never-alive.html today, the record said step 4 and the reload opened step 1, and a fresh open already had a step ticked. That session's uncommitted change moves the overview to finish “on arrival”, which by this review's measurement still ticks step 1 during page load. Named here; Science's files were not touched.

## Verdict on Grade 1 Computing

Nothing found stops the children already using Grade 1, and the one defect that was costing each of them something is fixed in the repository. Once redeployed, finished lessons reach their teacher and a reopened lesson opens where it was left. The build teaches all 28 Stage 1 objectives through activities, holds the Stage 2 line, and now carries no factual error that a careful read found.

Two things should happen before this is called validated rather than measured, and neither is engineering: a teacher reads the content and the 81 question keys, and somebody watches a child use a lesson and walks one with a screen reader. The redeploy is the one decision that changes what a learner gets today.
