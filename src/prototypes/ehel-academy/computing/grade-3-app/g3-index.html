Do it unplugged, for real

  • Follow it exactly. Write the steps on cards, one per card. The child reads them out and the grown-up does EXACTLY what each card says, silly mistakes and all - a computer follows its algorithm the same way.
  • Correct the algorithm. Say the steps of a job in the wrong order, or with one silly step in it, and ask the child to spot the bug and say what it should be.
  • Edit the algorithm. Take a recipe or a routine and change one step together. Say out loud what the change will do BEFORE you try it.
  • Follow it exactly. You need: A grown-up, a simple job (make toast, water a plant). Write the steps on cards, one per card. The grown-up follows them EXACTLY, silly mistakes and all. Did the job get done? If not, which step was wrong? Look for: A computer follows an algorithm the way your grown-up did.
  • Say why. You need: The same cards. For each card, say why it comes where it does. Try swapping two cards. What goes wrong? That is understanding the algorithm. Look for: Every step has a reason.
  • Edit it. You need: The same cards, one blank card. Change one card to make something different: toast with honey, a taller sandwich. Say before you do it what will change. Then follow the edited algorithm. Look for: Edit is a change you want; correct is a fix you need.

Answer keys

  • Follow it exactly Turn the tap on &rarr; Put soap on your hands &rarr; Rub your hands together &rarr; Rinse the soap off &rarr; Dry your hands
  • Why is each step where it is? Why does rinse come AFTER rub? → You rinse off the soap you have already rubbed in
  • Correct the algorithm
    • grow a plant from a seed the bug is step 3, Eat the seed; it should be Plant the seed in the soil
    • grow a plant from a seed the bug is step 2, Plant the seed; it belongs after Fill it with soil
    • grow a plant from a seed the bug is step 5, Put it in the fridge; it should be Put it in the sun
  • Edit the algorithm
    • a tower with a red top brick → Add the red brick
    • a taller tower → Add the purple brick
    • a tower that starts on a blue brick → Put down the blue brick
  • Follow, understand, edit or correct? Follow: doing the steps of a recipe, one by one, Robo doing forward, forward, turn, exactly as written; Understand: explaining why the oven goes on before the cake goes in, saying that soap needs wet hands, so the tap comes first; Edit: swapping jam for honey because you like honey, adding a brick so the tower is taller; Correct: spotting that 'bake for 3 hours' should be 30 minutes and fixing it, changing 'shoes, then socks' to 'socks, then shoes'
  • Check: linear algorithms
    1. What makes an algorithm linear? → the steps go in a line, one after another
    2. In the hand-washing algorithm, why does the tap go on first? → every other step needs water
    3. You change a step because you WANT a different outcome. That is... → editing
  • Show what you know
    1. Robo does exactly the steps written down, in order. Robo is... → following the algorithm
    2. The seed algorithm said 'eat the seed'. Changing it to 'plant the seed' is... → correcting
    3. Changing the small top brick to a red brick because you want a red top is... → editing
    4. Which is a linear algorithm? → tap on, soap, rub, rinse, dry
    5. Why does soap come after the tap? → soap needs wet hands to lather
    6. Understanding an algorithm means... → knowing why each step is where it is
    7. In the seed algorithm the seed was planted BEFORE the soil went in. The fix is to... → move the seed step after the soil step

Do it unplugged, for real

  • Cut the waste: a cup of tea. Write the steps of a job on cards, and slip in two cards that do nothing for it or say the same thing twice. Can the child find and remove them, and does the job still get done?
  • Find the steps that repeat: three plants. Write out a job with a part that happens again and again (brush, rinse, brush, rinse). Ask which cards you could write once with 'repeat 3 times' in front.
  • Spot the waste. You need: A grown-up, cards, a simple job. Write the steps of a job on cards, and slip in two that do nothing or say the same thing twice. Can the child find them and take them out? Does the job still get done with the short set? Look for: If the job still gets done, the cut steps were waste.
  • Find the repeat. You need: A job with a part that comes round again (setting the table, brushing every tooth). Write out every step, long. Find the run of steps that happens again and again. Write it once with 'repeat N times' in front. How many cards did you save? Look for: Repeated steps are written once.

Answer keys

  • Long or concise? What does a CONCISE algorithm have? → Only the steps the task needs, and no more
  • Cut the waste: a cup of tea cut: Sing a song, Stir again, Wave at the cat; keep: Get a cup &rarr; Put a teabag in &rarr; Pour in hot water &rarr; Add milk &rarr; Stir
  • Cut the waste: getting dressed cut: Take your coat off, Put your coat on again, Look in the mirror for a long time; keep: Put on socks &rarr; Put on shoes &rarr; Put on your coat &rarr; Put on your hat
  • Find the steps that repeat: three plants repeat 3 times: Fill the can, Pour on a plant, Walk to the next plant; How many times do the repeated steps happen? → 3
  • Find the steps that repeat: laying the table repeat 4 times: Put down a plate, Put a fork on the left, Put a knife on the right, Put a cup at the top; How many times does the run of four steps repeat? → 4
  • Waste, or needed? Needed: get two slices of bread, spread butter on one slice, spread jam, put the top slice on, cut it in half; Waste: spread butter on the same slice again, watch a cartoon, take the top slice off, then put it back
  • Check: concise
    1. An efficient algorithm is... → concise: only the steps the task needs
    2. Fill, pour, walk, fill, pour, walk, fill, pour, walk. What is a shorter way to write it? → repeat 3 times: fill, pour, walk
    3. Why is a concise algorithm easier to EDIT? → there are fewer steps to change, and a repeat is changed once
  • Show what you know
    1. 'Open the door, open the door, walk in.' Which step is waste? → the second open the door
    2. Which algorithm is concise? → boil, bag, pour, wait, stir
    3. Laying four places: plate, fork, knife, cup, four times. The concise way is... → repeat 4 times: plate, fork, knife, cup
    4. Coat on, coat off, coat on. How many of those steps are needed? → one
    5. What does 'efficient' mean for an algorithm? → does the whole job with no wasted steps
    6. If you cut a step and the tea does NOT get made, that step was... → needed
    7. Steps that happen again and again, back to back, in a task are called... → repeated steps

Do it unplugged, for real

  • What will the change do?. Before changing one step of a recipe or a routine, say out loud what will happen. Then change it and see.
  • Say the reason. You need: A grown-up, a routine. Say the steps of a routine (making toast, going to bed). For every step, say 'because': it comes here because... Find one step whose order does not matter. Most do. Look for: Logic is the 'because'.
  • Predict, then try. You need: A recipe or routine you can change safely. Pick one change: swap two steps, or leave one out. Say out loud what will happen. Try it. Were you right? Look for: Predicting before trying is what programmers do.
  • Sections on the wall. You need: Paper, a pen. Write 'getting ready for school' as four sections: wash, dress, breakfast, bag. Put it on the wall and follow it for a week. Change one section (a new breakfast). Did the others need changing? Look for: A change stays in its section.

Answer keys

  • Logical thinking What is logical thinking, when you make an algorithm? → Putting each step where it is for a reason
  • Order it with logic Get a bowl &rarr; Pour in cereal &rarr; Pour on milk &rarr; Get a spoon &rarr; Eat; not needed: Put the bowl in the bath, Boil the milk, Phone a friend
  • What will the change do?
    1. What will the sandwich be like? → The jam ends up on TOP of the sandwich
    2. What will the sandwich be like? → A jam sandwich with no butter
    3. What will the sandwich be like? → A cheese and jam sandwich
    4. What will the sandwich be like? → A banana sandwich instead of a jam one
  • Predict again: bedtime
    1. What happens at story time? → The lights are off, so the story is read in the dark
    2. What is different? → Bed with unbrushed teeth
    3. What is different? → A drink of water before the story
  • Divide the task into sections Wash: brush your teeth, wash your face, brush your hair; Dress: put on your shirt, put on your shoes, put on socks; Breakfast: pour the cereal, pour on the milk; Bag: pack your reading book, pack your lunch box
  • Why sections help You want to change breakfast from cereal to toast. Which sections do you have to change? → Only the breakfast section
  • Check: think it through
    1. Why does toast come before butter? → butter melts on hot toast, so the toast must be made first
    2. Predicting a change means... → saying what will happen before you try it
    3. Dividing a big task into sections makes it... → easier to follow and easier to edit
  • Show what you know
    1. Which is a LOGICAL reason for an order? → unlock the door before opening it, because a locked door will not open
    2. Swap 'spread jam' with 'put the top on'. What happens? → the jam ends up on top of the sandwich
    3. Take 'brush teeth' out of the bedtime algorithm. What is different? → bed with unbrushed teeth
    4. 'Getting ready for school' divided into wash, dress, breakfast, bag is... → a big task divided into sections
    5. To change cereal to toast you change... → only the breakfast section
    6. Cereal goes in the bowl before the milk because... → cereal needs a dry bowl to land in and milk would splash
    7. Before you change a step, the sensible thing to do is... → predict what the change will do

Do it unplugged, for real

  • Make it shorter. Lay out a long program of cards with a card that does nothing and the same card three times. Make it shorter without changing what it does.
  • Start from the same place. Draw the blocks on paper cards (move right, jump, say hello). The child lays out a program; the grown-up is the computer and acts it out block by block.
  • Change the number. Lay out 'forward 2, forward 1' in cards on a floor grid. Change ONLY the numbers to land a toy on a target square.
  • Test and debug. Write a four-card program with one wrong card. Act it out, spot which card went wrong, swap it, and act it out again.
  • Tidy the cards. You need: Cards with a block on each. Lay out a long program: jump, jump, jump, wait, say hello, wait. Take out the cards that do nothing. Fold the three jumps into 'repeat 3 times, jump'. Act out both versions. Are they the same? Look for: Same job, fewer cards.
  • Start from the same square. You need: A floor grid, a toy. Run a program of forward, forward, turn with the toy, and leave it where it stops. Run it again from THERE. Where does it end now? Add a 'go to the start square' card at the top and run twice more. Look for: A reset at the top makes every run the same.

Answer keys

  • Make it shorter
    • jump 3 times, then say hello jump, jump, jump, wait, say → repeat3, jump, say
    • move right twice, then spin twice right, wait, right, spin, spin, wait → repeat2, right, repeat2, spin
    • say hello, then grow 4 times wait, say, grow, grow, grow, grow, wait → say, repeat4, grow
  • Start from the same place
    • Go home, Move right, Jump → blocks home, right, jump
    • Go home, Spin, Move left → blocks home, spin, left
    • Go home, Grow, Say hello → blocks home, grow, say
  • Change the number
    • stop on the flower, 3 squares along → right x2, jump x1, right x1
    • stop on the flower, 2 squares along → right x3, left x1
    • stop on the flower, 1 square back → left x2, jump x1, right x1
  • Test and debug
    • jump 3 times, then say hello the bug is block 2 (spin); it should be jump
    • go home, move right twice, grow the bug is block 3 (left); it should be right
    • say hello, then shrink twice the bug is block 3 (grow); it should be shrink
  • Check: tidy programs
    1. Jump, jump, jump, jump can be combined into... → repeat 4 times, jump
    2. Why put a go home block FIRST? → so the cat starts from the same place every time
    3. The cat stops at square 2 and the flower is at 3. What do you change? → a number inside a move block, to go one further
  • Show what you know
    1. A wait block that does nothing for the job is... → an unused command: delete it
    2. Removing a wait and folding three jumps into a repeat makes the program... → shorter, and it still does the same thing
    3. The cat was left two squares along by the last program. To start from the beginning you... → put a go home block first
    4. What does 'initialisation' mean? → putting things back to their starting state at the start of a program
    5. Move right 1, jump, move right 1 stops at square 2. To stop at 3, change... → one move number from 1 to 2
    6. After you fix a bug you should... → run the program again to test the fix
    7. Which program is clear and concise? → repeat 3 times jump, say hello
