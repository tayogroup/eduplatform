# Ehel Academy syllabuses — index and standing decisions

Forty syllabus drafts, covering **370 units** and **3,052 learning
outcomes** across five subjects. Each is narrative copy for the authoring form
at `local/hubredirect/syllabus.php`, laid out field for field so it pastes box
for box, with the schedule half deliberately left to `pqsyl_generated()`.

Every figure in every draft is read from that course's own data. Unit tables in
the four generated subjects are produced from the data and verified back against
it row by row — 289 of 289 exact.

## Decide once, not forty times

Four things recur across the set. Settling each one resolves many syllabuses at
a stroke.

### 1. Curriculum review is outstanding everywhere except English

All **289 units** of Mathematics, Science, Computing and Global Perspectives
carry a review-pending status, as does every capstone. Science and Computing
additionally record a *self-teaching content pass* as complete, so their labels
and their unit statuses disagree about how far the content has come — neither
claims a sign-off.

**Decision:** publish with the status disclosed, or hold the generated subjects
back until review completes.

### 2. No lecture video exists in any generated subject

All 289 units report `Video pending`, with no video and no poster. Science and
Global Perspectives have *complete* audio narration beside entirely absent
video, which is an odd pair to leave unexplained in a catalogue.

**Decision:** correct any catalogue copy that implies video lessons.

### 3. The capstone is one template repeated eight times, in two subjects

Mathematics and Science each ship a capstone whose final product is **verbatim
identical from Stage 1 to Stage 8**, along with its four project stages and its
driving question bar the stage number. Mathematics asks a five-year-old whose
mathematics is *Numbers to 10* for a budget and a visitor survey; Science asks
the same child for a safety checklist and a visitor survey.

**Decision:** age-scale the capstones, or accept and say so.

### 4. Self-paced courses need a rhythm and a reporting format

Four of the five subjects have **no live sessions, no assignments and no marks**
— a pass or fail per unit and nothing else. Neither how often a learner is
expected to work nor what a family is told at the end of term exists in the
course data, and neither can.

**Decision:** set both, once, at school level.

## Per-subject items that do not generalise

### English — the Lower Secondary marks, now itemised

**Resolved 2026-08-09.** Grades 7 and 8 supplied two rubrics worth **32 marks**
and awarded assignments out of **40** and a capstone out of **72**, leaving
**112 of 432 marks — 26% of each year — with no stated basis**. All twenty
assignments across the two grades now publish their division: a unit assignment
as writing 16 + speaking 16 + third piece 8, the capstone as 16 + 16 + 16 + 16 +
8. Both builders emit it too, so a rebuild cannot reintroduce the gap.

**The division is authored, not Cambridge-derived** — the source packs carry no
mark scheme at all — so every touched assignment records that curriculum
confirmation is pending. Confirming it is the remaining step.

Related, and smaller: the Grade 5 and Grade 6 capstones are unitemised too, and
per-task marking guidance thins out up the grades — 10 of 10 units in Grade 3,
7 in Grades 4 and 5, **none in Grades 6 and 7**, 2 in Grade 8.

And: **there is no Grade 9**, but the Grade 8 capstone asks the learner to write
a *Grade 9 transition reflection*. Families will ask what comes next.

### Computing — the narration is not in the repository

**7,854 clips exist on one machine and cover every stage; zero are tracked.**
`computing/media/audio/tts/` is gitignored. Science commits 6,479 clips and
Global Perspectives 2,684; Computing commits none. A fresh checkout has no
Computing audio, so whether a learner hears anything depends on which machine a
deploy runs from.

### Global Perspectives — Stage 5 is HELD

**Held from publication 2026-08-09** until the full content is located. It holds
**Research and Analysis and nothing else**.

A re-export does not fix it, and that has been tested rather than assumed: a
fresh Google Drive export taken on 2026-08-09 is the right subject and contains
the same two units, byte for byte. The folder it exports from holds two units,
so either the other four live elsewhere in Drive or they were never authored.

The later placement exams used to route here. Stages 6, 7 and 8 each named Stage
5 units as the place to go and rebuild, which after the withdrawal sent the
lowest-scoring learner to a withdrawal notice. All now point at **Stage 4**,
which teaches the same six skills one per unit, and a build gate fails on any
placement exam that routes into a withdrawn stage.

*An earlier version of this index said the Stage 6 exam tested 20 of 34
questions on skills Stage 5 never taught. That was wrong: those 20 questions are
Research and Analysis, which Stage 5 does teach, and the other four skills are
tested separately against Stage 4.*

`check:global-perspectives` now reports the shortfall in its own block rather
than passing silently, and fails outright on any missing skill the source
manifest does not document.

Separately: **Cambridge prints no objective codes for this subject.** The codes
shown against each unit are assigned by Ehel's extractor and must not be
presented to a family as Cambridge references.

## What each subject is, in one line

- **English** — taught. Live sessions, assignments, rubrics, marks. Grade 1
  assumes an adult in the room; by Grade 7 the learner sources and attributes
  everything and defends it aloud.
- **Mathematics** — self-paced, the largest subject at 133 units. A grown-up
  guide on every concept, and one hands-on activity per unit.
- **Science** — self-paced, the most practical: **six hands-on activities per
  unit**, 318 across the subject. Narration complete and committed. No grown-up
  guide; written to the learner.
- **Computing** — self-paced, and the only subject that teaches **debugging** and
  **e-safety** inside the lessons. Stages 1–4 rewritten from teacher guides into
  learner prose; Stages 5–8 are student books carried across.
- **Global Perspectives** — self-paced, and really two courses: Stages 1–3
  guided, with a grown-up guide kept whole and assessment by project; Stages 4–8
  one unit per transferable skill, assessed by written challenge.
