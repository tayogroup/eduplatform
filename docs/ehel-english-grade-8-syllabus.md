## Overview

Grade 8 English is the year of argument and evidence. It follows **Cambridge
Lower Secondary English 0861, Stage 8**, and it opens on the most contemporary
material in the whole series: Unit 1 is about **how rumours outrun facts**, and
how a careful reader tells the two apart. The first assignment is an opinion
essay on spotting misinformation, an annotated comparison of two information
texts, and a recorded contrast debate.

Ten units run across three terms — languages and communication, design and
packaging, community and environment, advertising and persuasion, natural
resources, natural wonders and conservation, historical figures and legacy,
global storytelling, and music as a universal language.

Across the year a learner meets **310 words** — the most of any year at Ehel
Academy — reads **44 texts**, and works through **74 learning outcomes**. The
vocabulary is the vocabulary of judgement: *concise*, *zeal*, *articulate*,
*benign*, *disposition*, *clarify*.

**Every unit, without exception, submits a source annotation.** Grade 7
introduced that habit from its second unit; Grade 8 assumes it from the first
day. Unit 10 closes the course with an evidence-led inquiry, an attributed
source record, four annotated portfolio pieces and a **six-minute oral defence**
— the longest piece of sustained speaking a learner does here.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Grade 8 assumes Grades 1 to 7. There is no foundation unit inside this course.

Before Unit 1 every learner sits the **Grade 8 English Placement Exam**: 36
questions, 36 marks, about 35 minutes. Three sections:

- **Grade 7 skills** (22 questions)
- **Grade 6 skills** (8 questions)
- **Foundations, Grades 4–5** (6 questions)

Attempts are unlimited and it is never a fail. Four results:

- **Ready for Grade 8** — 80% overall with at least 60% in every section.
- **Ready, with a little review** — 50% or more; start at Unit 1 after the review
  lessons the report names.
- **Grade 7 skills below 40%** — treated as critical, and the report routes to
  Grade 7 Units 1, 4 and 8 (*Family, Identity, and History*; *Transport, Cities,
  and Urban Solutions*; *Media, Technology, and News*) whatever the overall
  score.
- **Let's build strong roots first** — the report recommends **Grade 7 English**
  as the course to take.

The review lessons open directly in whichever earlier grade they belong to, even
where that grade is walked one unit at a time. The foundations section reaches
back four years, naming two Grade 4 units and two Grade 5 units.

The exam can be retaken at any point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- A writing folder that keeps drafts.
- **A source record kept from Unit 1.** Every assignment in this course requires
  a source annotation — there is no unit where it is optional — and the capstone
  requires an attributed source record covering the year.
- Materials for the Unit 10 inquiry product. Settle what it will be with the
  teacher early.
- A way to record audio. Every unit assignment includes a spoken piece, and the
  capstone defence runs to six minutes.
- A device that plays audio, and an internet connection for live sessions and
  research.

### Attendance

The course runs **60 live sessions** — 6 per unit, all 45 minutes.

Each session names its preparation: review the unit overview and the vocabulary
group beforehand.

Attendance is expected at all sessions; tell the teacher in advance where
possible. Sessions are recorded and a missed one should be watched before the
next. More than two missed in a unit and the teacher will contact the family to
agree how to catch up.

### Homework

Each unit carries **six activities** and **one assignment** — **10 assignments**
across the year.

Every assignment is three pieces in the same shape: **a written piece, a source
annotation, and a recording**, all improved against the Grade 8 rubrics before
submission. Unit 1 sets the pattern — an opinion essay on spotting
misinformation, an annotated comparison of two information texts, and a recorded
contrast debate.

Expect **60–75 minutes** on an assignment across the unit's two weeks, plus the
revision pass.

The Unit 10 capstone is five submissions: the evidence-led inquiry product, the
attributed source record, four annotated portfolio pieces, the six-minute oral
defence recording, and the transition reflection.

### Assessment and grading

**Assignments — 432 marks across the year.** Nine unit assignments at 40 marks,
the Unit 10 capstone at 72.

The course supplies two rubrics: **Writing** on Ideas, Organization, Language
and Conventions, and **Speaking** on Clarity, Vocabulary, Sentence accuracy and
Interaction — four criteria each, four marks per criterion. **That is 32
marks.**

help / By myself**. No marks.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Come prepared** — the unit overview and vocabulary group before the session.
- **Attribute everything, from the first unit.** In a course that opens on
  misinformation, using a source without saying where it came from is not a
  paperwork failure; it is the thing the year is about.
- **Argue, and be argued with.** Unit 1's recording is a contrast debate, and
  interaction is assessed all year.
- **Improve against the rubric before submitting**, on every piece.
- **Be ready to be questioned.** The capstone is a six-minute oral defence: the
  learner answers for the work rather than presenting it.
- **A parent or another adult must be present whenever a learner uses a device
  or the Wehel AI tutor.** This holds in all ten units. The tutor is for
  practising language, not for producing the work.

### Support and communication

**If a learner is struggling**, the placement exam can be retaken at any point
and its report names the specific earlier units to revisit; those links open
those units directly. Grade 7 English remains available as a course for a
learner who needs the ground under this one.

> **What comes after Grade 8.** This is the last English course Ehel Academy
> offers — there is no Grade 9 — yet the capstone asks the learner to write a
> *Grade 9 transition reflection* and set a goal for it. Families will ask, and
> the course cannot answer: {{what a learner moves on to after Grade 8, and what
> the school provides or recommends}}.

**Extra help available:**

- The teacher, at the contact above.
- Wehel, the in-course AI tutor — with an adult present, for practice not
  production.
- Recorded audio for the vocabulary and the readings.

**How the school will contact you:** when marked work is returned, after a
missed assignment, after two missed live sessions in a unit, and at the end of
each term. The capstone is conferenced at its milestones.

**If you have a concern**, contact the teacher first. If it is unresolved after
{{n}} school days, contact {{school administrator role/name}}.

**For English as an additional language, or any additional need**, tell the
teacher at the start of the year.
