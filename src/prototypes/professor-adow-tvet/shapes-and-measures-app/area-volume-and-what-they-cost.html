What this lesson is about

Work out the perimeter and area of a floor, split an awkward room into rectangles, take the openings out of a wall, and calculate the volume of a trench, a drum and the mass of what fills them.

By the end of this lesson you can:

  1. Calculate the perimeter of a rectangle and of an L-shaped room.
  2. Calculate the area of a rectangle, a triangle and a circle.
  3. Split an irregular shape into rectangles and check the split accounts for all of it.
  4. Subtract doors and windows from a wall area before ordering.
  5. Calculate the volume of a rectangular solid and of a cylinder.
  6. Convert cubic metres to litres, and find a mass from a volume and a density.
Where this leadsThis module serves all six departments. Measurement is the same skill in construction, mechanical engineering, electronics, agriculture, livestock and food processing — only the units and the tolerances change. Where a criterion maps onto the Kenyan Carpentry & Joinery Level 4 units it says so; the other five departments' frameworks are not yet in hand, and a criterion that plainly serves them carries no mapping rather than a guessed one.

What goes wrong hereReaching for the area formula because the shape is a rectangle. Perimeter and area answer different questions. Skirting is bought by the metre and tiles by the square metre, and ordering one by the other's figure is a mistake nobody notices until the material arrives.

Area: the surface of the job

Assessed here

What goes wrong hereUsing the diameter where the formula asks for the radius. It gives an answer four times too big, because the radius is squared. If a circular answer looks wildly large, this is the first thing to check.

Splitting an awkward shape

Assessed here

What goes wrong hereSplitting so the pieces overlap, and counting the overlap twice. Draw the split on the sketch before calculating anything. If two rectangles share a strip, that strip is in the total twice and the order comes in over.

Taking the openings out

Assessed here · and at the bench

What goes wrong hereDeducting the openings AND then adding a waste allowance on the gross figure. The allowance goes on the NET area — the surface you are actually covering. Applied to the gross it quietly pays for the door twice.

Volume: the space the job fills

Assessed here · and at the bench

What goes wrong hereMixing units inside a volume calculation. Depth in centimetres and length in metres gives an answer out by a hundred. Put all three dimensions in metres first, then multiply.
