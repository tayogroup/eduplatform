Carpentry Tools & Joints

The families of wood joint, how a joint is marked out and cut, and how members are fastened and assembled. Theory, drawings and assessment are done here; every cut is made and signed off at the bench.

Where this leadsThis is foundation work. It is not a unit of the Kenyan Carpentry & Joinery Level 4 qualification — all eight of those units assume you already have tools and joints. What you learn here is what they are built on: marking out, sawing to a line and fitting a joint feed directly into Doors and Windows (0732 351 05D), Timber Furniture Items (0732 351 06D) and Cabinets (0732 351 08D).

Lessons

Marking Out and Cutting a Halving Joint
Name the three tools the job needs, make the workshop safe, put the stages in order, mark out a halving joint one line at a time, decide which side of the line to saw, and close the joint. · 10 stepsThe Five Families of Joint
Meet the five families of wood joint, see two from each, sort ten joints into the family they belong to, and choose a joint for a job by asking which way the load pulls. · 5 stepsSawing and Paring to the Line
Start a saw cut without it jumping, keep it square in both planes by watching the face you are not looking at, and pare waste with a chisel so the edges do not break out. · 5 stepsNails, Screws and Glue
Tell nails apart by head and section, choose a screw by its head, drive a fastener deep enough to hold, pre-drill where the timber would split, glue a joint without flooding it, and inspect the finished work against the drawing. · 7 steps
