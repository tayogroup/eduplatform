Telling the Time

1

How long is that?

Every unit of time is made of a fixed number of smaller ones. Group them and you can swap between the two.

2

Swapping units, both ways

Going to the smaller unit you multiply, because there are more of them. Going back to the bigger one you divide. It is the same fact used two ways.

3

Three ways to say one time

A clock face, a 12-hour time with am or pm, and a 24-hour time. One moment, three ways of writing it.
12-hour—
24-hour—

4

Read the clock

No numbers written out this time. The short hand gives the hour — the one it has passed, not the one it is heading for — and the long hand gives the minutes, five for every number.

5

Writing it in 24-hour

In 24-hour time nothing is written twice, so no am or pm is needed. Morning hours stay as they are and afternoon hours have 12 added — and midnight and midday are the two you have to learn.

6

Reading a timetable

Read down a column to follow one bus, and across a row to compare the same stop. Times are in 24-hour.

7

Which one do you catch?

Find your stop’s row, then look along it for the last bus that still gets you there in time. The latest one is the answer, because any earlier one means waiting about.

8

How long between?

Count on from the start to the end. Do the whole units first, then the rest.

9

Over the end of the month

Adding days is easy until you run off the end of the month. Use up the days that are left in this month first, and only what is over goes into the next one.

10

Months and years apart

Gaps are not only hours and minutes. Months and years are counted the same way — and to cross from one year into another, count the whole years first and the leftover months after.
