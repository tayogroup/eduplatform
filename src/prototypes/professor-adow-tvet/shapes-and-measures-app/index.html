Shapes and Measurements

Reading a measurement, converting between units, working out area and volume, proving a corner square and a surface level, estimating what a job needs, and marking a solid out flat before it is bent. Every worked example comes from a real trade — none of it is arithmetic for its own sake.

Read a measurement

Reading a Measurement

Find a measurement on a rule to the nearest millimetre, learn why a tape's hook is meant to move, convert between millimetres and metres without changing the quantity, and decide whether a measurement is inside the tolerance a job allows.

Area, Volume and What They Cost

Work out the perimeter and area of a floor, split an awkward room into rectangles, take the openings out of a wall, and calculate the volume of a trench, a drum and the mass of what fills them.

Square, Level and Setting Out

Tell square, level and plumb apart and know which tool proves each, read a spirit level and say which way the work moves, prove a frame square by its diagonals, set out a right angle with 3-4-5, and produce an estimate somebody else can check.

Solids, Nets and Angles

Name the faces, edges and vertices of a solid, read the flat development that folds into one, allow for the thickness of the material, tell an acute angle from an obtuse one, transfer an angle with a bevel, and work out a mitre.

Where this leadsThis module serves all six departments. Measurement is the same skill in construction, mechanical engineering, electronics, agriculture, livestock and food processing — only the units and the tolerances change. Where a criterion maps onto the Kenyan Carpentry & Joinery Level 4 units it says so; the other five departments' frameworks are not yet in hand, and a criterion that plainly serves them carries no mapping rather than a guessed one.
