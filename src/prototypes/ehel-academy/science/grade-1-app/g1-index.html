What Stage 1 science covers

Thinking and Working ScientificallyAsk questions, predict, sort, use equipment safely, measure in hands and cubes, record in tables, say whether it matched.
BiologyLiving and never alive; what animals and plants need; parts of a plant; the body and the five senses; how people are alike and different.
ChemistryWood, metal, plastic, glass, rock, paper and fabric; object versus material; properties; squashing, bending, twisting and stretching.
PhysicsHow things move; pushes and pulls; floating and sinking; sources of sound and sound fading with distance; electricity and magnets.
Earth and SpaceEarth is our planet and mostly water; land is rock and soil; the Sun gives light and heat and is one of many stars.
Science in ContextHow thinking has changed, how everyday things work, who uses science at work, and how what we do affects the world.

Lesson 1: Alive or Never Alive10 steps · about 25 minutes · 10 objectives

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 1)

  • 1Bp.01 Identify living things and things that have never been alive.
  • 1TWSc.01 Sort and group objects, materials and living things based on observations of the similarities and differences between them.
  • 1TWSp.01 Ask questions about the world around us and talk about how to find answers.
  • 1Bp.02 Know that animals, including humans, need air, water and suitable food to survive.
  • 1Bp.03 Know that plants need light and water to survive.
  • 1TWSp.02 Make predictions about what they think will happen.
  • 1TWSc.04 Follow instructions safely when doing practical work.
  • 1TWSa.01 Describe what happened during an enquiry and if it matched predictions.
  • 1TWSc.05 Collect and record observations and/or measurements by annotating images and completing simple tables.
  • 1SIC.04 Talk about how science helps us understand our effect on the world around us.

The steps

  1. What makes something alive? · Press Next and watch the goat. Living things do four things.
  2. Alive, or never alive? · Tap each picture and hear whether it is alive.
  3. Sort them into two bins · Is it alive , or was it never alive ? Tap the right bin.
  4. Ask a science question · Look at the puppy. Scientists start with a question. Tap one you would like to ask.
  5. What animals need · Press Next . Every animal, and you, needs three things to stay alive.
  6. What does the cat need? · Does the cat need this to stay alive? Tap the right bin.
  7. Does a plant need water? · Two plants. We will water one and give the other no water . First, predict!
  8. Record what you saw · Scientists write down what happened. Fill in the table for %s .
  9. Caring for living things · Living things need us to look after them. Tap each picture to hear how.
  10. Show what you know · Time to show what you know. Tap the answer.

Do the experiment for real

  • Does a plant need water?. Two small plants or two cuttings in cups. Water one every day and not the other. Look every day for a week and talk about what changes.

Answer keys

  • Sort them into two bins Alive: hen, fish, palm tree, bee, grass; Never alive: cup, river, brick, cloud, toy bear
  • What does the cat need? Needs it: water, air, cat food; Does not need it: ball of wool, cat bed, ribbon, television
  • Does a plant need water? prediction: It will droop and go yellow; what happened: It drooped and went yellow
  • Show what you know
    1. Which one is alive? → a camel
    2. Which one was never alive? → a stone
    3. A car moves along the road. Is it alive? → No. Moving is not enough. It never eats or grows.
    4. What does a cat need to stay alive? → air, water and food
    5. You feel thirsty. What is your body asking for? → water
    6. A plant gets no water for five days. What happens? → it droops and goes yellow
    7. A mango tree does not walk about. Is it alive? → Yes. It grows, drinks water and makes seeds.
    8. A hen has chicks. What does that tell you? → The hen is alive, because living things can have babies.
Lesson 2: Parts of a Plant11 steps · about 30 minutes · 10 objectives

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 1)

  • 1Bs.01 Recognise and name the major parts of familiar flowering plants (limited to roots, leaves, stems and flowers).
  • 1Bp.03 Know that plants need light and water to survive.
  • 1TWSp.02 Make predictions about what they think will happen.
  • 1TWSc.04 Follow instructions safely when doing practical work.
  • 1TWSa.01 Describe what happened during an enquiry and if it matched predictions.
  • 1TWSc.05 Collect and record observations and/or measurements by annotating images and completing simple tables.
  • 1TWSc.03 Take measurements in non-standard units.
  • 1TWSc.02 Use given equipment appropriately.
  • 1SIC.03 Know that everyone uses science and identify people who use science professionally.
  • 1TWSp.01 Ask questions about the world around us and talk about how to find answers.

The steps

  1. A seed grows · Press Next day and watch the seed. Which part grows first?
  2. Tap the part · Find each part of the plant. Tap the %s .
  3. What does each part do? · Every part has a job. Tap each one to hear it.
  4. Which part? · Which part of the plant does the job? Tap it.
  5. Does a plant need light? · Two plants. One by the window , one in a dark cupboard . Both get water. Predict first!
  6. My plant diary · A plant diary is a table. Fill in what you saw on %s .
  7. How tall is the plant? · Measure %s in cubes. Press to stack a cube each time.
  8. Use the tools safely · Scientists use tools carefully. Tap each tool to hear how to use it.
  9. People who use science · Some people use plant science every day at work. Tap each one.
  10. Ask a question about a plant · Look at the flower. Tap a question you would like to ask about it.
  11. Show what you know · Time to show what you know. Tap the answer.

Do the experiment for real

  • Does a plant need light?. Two small plants. Put one on a sunny windowsill and one inside a cupboard, and water both. Look after a few days: the one in the dark goes pale.

Answer keys

  • Which part?
    1. Which part drinks water from the soil? → roots
    2. Which part catches sunlight and makes food? → leaves
    3. Which part makes the seeds? → flower
    4. Which part holds the plant up tall? → stem
    5. Which part is hidden under the soil? → roots
    6. Which part is usually the most colourful? → flower
  • Does a plant need light? prediction: It will go pale and droopy; what happened: It went pale, thin and droopy
  • Show what you know
    1. Which part holds the plant in the soil? → roots
    2. Which part makes food using sunlight? → leaves
    3. A new plant grows from a... → seed
    4. What do plants need to stay alive? → light and water
    5. Which part carries water up to the leaves? → the stem
    6. A plant is kept in a dark cupboard with water. What happens? → it goes pale and droopy
    7. Which grew first from the seed? → the root
    8. A seedling is 3 cubes tall and a sunflower is 8 cubes tall. Which is taller? → the sunflower

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 1)

  • 1Bs.03 Recognise and name the major external parts of the human body.
  • 1Bs.02 Identify the senses (limited to sight, hearing, taste, smell and touch) and what they detect, linking each to the correct body part.

  • 1TWSc.01 Sort and group objects, materials and living things based on observations of the similarities and differences between them.
  • 1TWSc.03 Take measurements in non-standard units.
  • 1TWSc.05 Collect and record observations and/or measurements by annotating images and completing simple tables.

The steps

  1. Tap the body part · Find each part on the child. Tap the %s .
  2. Move your body · Press Next and do what the picture says. Really do it!
  3. Five senses · You have five senses . Each one uses a body part. Press Next .
  4. Which sense is it? · Something is happening. Which body part tells you about it? Tap it.
  5. The feely bag · Your hand is in the bag. You cannot see. What is inside? Tap it.
  6. Same and different · Look at the friends. Tap each one. What is the same about them? What is different?
  7. Everyone, or not everyone? · Does everyone have this, or only some people ? Tap the right bin.
  8. How tall? Measure in hands · Measure %s in hand spans. Press to lay down a hand each time.
  9. Write down the heights · Put each friend's height into the table. How tall was %s ?
  10. How does that work? · Science explains how things we use every day work. Tap each one.
  11. Ask a question about your body · Tap a question you would like to ask about your body.
  12. Show what you know · Time to show what you know. Tap the answer.

Answer keys

  • Which sense is it?
    1. You know bread is baking before you see it. Which part told you? → nose
    2. A dog barks behind you. Which part told you? → ears
    3. The sand is hot under your feet. Which part told you? → skin
    4. You see a red bird in the tree. Which part told you? → eyes
    5. The lemon is sour! Which part told you? → tongue
    6. The blanket feels soft. Which part told you? → skin
    7. Thunder rumbles far away. Which part told you? → ears
    8. Smoke! Which sense warns you first, before you see the fire? → smell, with your nose
  • The feely bag
    1. It feels soft, warm and furry. It wriggles. → a kitten
    2. It feels hard, cold, small and round. → a marble
    3. It feels rough and bumpy, like sandpaper. → a rock
    4. It feels light, soft and tickly. → a feather
    5. It feels smooth, cold and hard, and it has a handle. → a metal spoon
  • Everyone, or not everyone? Everyone: a nose, two ears, two eyes, hands; Some people: curly hair, glasses, brown eyes, a gap tooth
  • Show what you know
    1. Which body part do we use to see? → eyes
    2. Which body part do we use to hear? → ears
    3. We smell a flower with our... → nose
    4. How many senses do we have? → five
    5. We taste an orange with our... → tongue
    6. Which is at the end of your leg? → your foot
    7. What does every person have? → two eyes and a nose
    8. Sami is 6 hand spans tall and Nora is 4. Who is shorter? → Nora
Lesson 4: What Is It Made Of?10 steps · about 30 minutes · 12 objectives

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 1)

  • 1Cm.01 Identify, name, describe, sort and group common materials, including wood, plastic, metal, glass, rock, paper and fabric.
  • 1Cm.02 Understand the difference between an object and a material.
  • 1TWSc.01 Sort and group objects, materials and living things based on observations of the similarities and differences between them.
  • 1Cp.01 Understand that all materials have a variety of properties.
  • 1Cp.02 Describe common materials in terms of their properties.
  • 1TWSc.02 Use given equipment appropriately.
  • 1Cc.01 Describe how materials can be changed by physical action, e.g. stretching, compressing, bending and twisting.
  • 1TWSp.02 Make predictions about what they think will happen.
  • 1TWSc.04 Follow instructions safely when doing practical work.
  • 1TWSa.01 Describe what happened during an enquiry and if it matched predictions.
  • 1TWSc.05 Collect and record observations and/or measurements by annotating images and completing simple tables.
  • 1SIC.02 Talk about how science explains how objects they use, or know about, work.

The steps

  1. What is it made of? · Tap each object. Hear what material it is made from.
  2. Object or material? · An object is a thing. A material is what it is made from. Press Next .
  3. Object, or material? · Is this an object , or a material ? Tap the right bin.
  4. Sort by material · Which material is this object made from? Tap the right bin.
  5. Test the materials · Choose a material, then press each test. Test at least three materials fully.
  6. Describe it · Which word describes the material? Tap it.
  7. Squash, bend, twist, stretch · Can we change a material's shape with our hands? Predict first, then try clay, an elastic band and a stone.
  8. Did it change shape? · Record what happened. Did %s change shape when you squashed and bent it?
  9. The right material for the job · Why is a window made of glass and a raincoat of plastic? Tap each one.
  10. Show what you know · Time to show what you know. Tap the answer.

Do the experiment for real

  • Squash, bend, twist, stretch. A ball of play dough, an elastic band and a stone. Squash, bend, twist and stretch each one. Which changed shape? Which did not?

Answer keys

  • Object, or material? Object: cup, shoe, key, book; Material: metal, wood, fabric, glass
  • Sort by material Wood: pencil, chair; Metal: coin, nut and bolt; Plastic: shampoo bottle, toy bricks; Glass: drinking glass; Paper: newspaper; Fabric: scarf, socks
  • Describe it
    1. A pillow feels... → soft
    2. A stone feels... → hard
    3. Sandpaper feels... → rough
    4. A glass window is... → smooth and see-through
    5. A rubber band is... → bendy and stretchy
    6. A metal spoon is... → hard and shiny
    7. A wooden ruler is... → stiff
  • Squash, bend, twist, stretch prediction: Nothing. The stone will keep its shape; what happened: Nothing. It kept its shape
  • Show what you know
    1. A metal spoon. Which word is the MATERIAL? → metal
    2. What is a wooden chair made from? → wood
    3. Which material can you see through? → glass
    4. Which of these feels rough? → sandpaper
    5. Which material is bendy? → a rubber band
    6. You squash a ball of clay. What happens? → it changes shape
    7. You squash a stone as hard as you can. What happens? → nothing, it keeps its shape
    8. Which is the best material for a raincoat? → plastic, because it is waterproof
Lesson 5: Pushes, Pulls and Floating9 steps · about 25 minutes · 11 objectives

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 1)

  • 1Pf.01 Explore, talk about and describe the movement of familiar objects.
  • 1Pf.02 Describe pushes and pulls as forces.
  • 1TWSc.01 Sort and group objects, materials and living things based on observations of the similarities and differences between them.
  • 1TWSp.02 Make predictions about what they think will happen.
  • 1TWSc.03 Take measurements in non-standard units.
  • 1TWSa.01 Describe what happened during an enquiry and if it matched predictions.
  • 1Pf.03 Explore that some objects float and some sink.
  • 1TWSc.04 Follow instructions safely when doing practical work.
  • 1TWSc.05 Collect and record observations and/or measurements by annotating images and completing simple tables.
  • 1SIC.02 Talk about how science explains how objects they use, or know about, work.
  • 1TWSp.01 Ask questions about the world around us and talk about how to find answers.

The steps

  1. How does it move? · Things move in different ways. Tap each one and hear the word for how it moves.
  2. Push or pull? · A push moves a thing away from you. A pull brings it towards you. Which is this?
  3. Push gently, push hard · Does a bigger push move the ball further? Predict, then push the ball two ways and count the steps.
  4. Stop it, turn it · Forces can also stop a moving thing or change its direction . Press Next .
  5. Float or sink? · Will the %s float or sink? Predict, then drop it in the tank and see.
  6. Record the results · Write your results in the table. Did %s float or sink?
  7. Pushes and pulls all around us · Science explains how the things we use work. Tap each one.
  8. Ask a question about the sea · Look at the boat. Tap a question you would like to ask.
  9. Show what you know · Time to show what you know. Tap the answer.

Do the experiment for real

  • Push gently, push hard. A ball on a smooth floor and some tape marks a step apart. Push gently, then hard, and count how many marks the ball passes each time.
  • Float or sink?. A bowl of water and eight things from the kitchen (an apple, a stone, a coin, a cork, a leaf, a spoon). Predict out loud before each one goes in.

Answer keys

  • Push or pull? Push: kicking a ball, pressing a doorbell, pushing a trolley, pushing a swing; Pull: opening a drawer, tug of war, walking a dog on a lead, pulling a zip up
  • Push gently, push hard prediction: The ball will roll further; what happened: The ball rolled further, nine steps instead of three
  • Float or sink? apple: Float; stone: Sink; wooden log: Float; metal key: Sink; leaf: Float; coin: Sink; empty plastic bottle: Float; brick: Sink
  • Show what you know
    1. Kicking a ball away from you is a... → push
    2. Opening a drawer towards you is a... → pull
    3. A push or a pull is called a... → force
    4. You push the ball harder. It goes... → further
    5. Which of these sinks in water? → a stone
    6. Which of these floats? → an apple
    7. A ball rolls towards you. You put your hand in front. What happens? → it stops
    8. How does a spinning top move? → it spins round and round
Lesson 6: Sounds Near and Far8 steps · about 20 minutes · 12 objectives

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 1)

  • 1Ps.01 Identify different sources of sound.
  • 1Bs.02 Identify the senses (limited to sight, hearing, taste, smell and touch) and what they detect, linking each to the correct body part.
  • 1TWSc.01 Sort and group objects, materials and living things based on observations of the similarities and differences between them.
  • 1Ps.02 Explore that as sound travels from a source it becomes quieter.
  • 1TWSp.02 Make predictions about what they think will happen.
  • 1TWSc.03 Take measurements in non-standard units.
  • 1TWSc.04 Follow instructions safely when doing practical work.
  • 1TWSa.01 Describe what happened during an enquiry and if it matched predictions.
  • 1TWSc.05 Collect and record observations and/or measurements by annotating images and completing simple tables.
  • 1TWSc.02 Use given equipment appropriately.
  • 1SIC.02 Talk about how science explains how objects they use, or know about, work.
  • 1SIC.03 Know that everyone uses science and identify people who use science professionally.

The steps

  1. Where does the sound come from? · Tap each picture and listen . The thing making the sound is called the source .
  2. Who or what made it? · Was that sound made by an animal or person , or by a thing ? Tap the right bin.
  3. What makes a sound? · A sound is made when something shakes very fast. Press Next and listen.
  4. Near and far · What happens to a sound as you walk away from it? Predict, then ring the bell and step back.
  5. How loud was it? · Record how loud the bell was at each distance. How loud was it %s ?
  6. Use the sound makers safely · Sound makers are tools. Tap each one to hear how to use it safely.
  7. Sound at work · Science explains how sound things work, and some people use sound science all day. Tap each one.
  8. Show what you know · Time to show what you know. Tap the answer.

Do the experiment for real

  • Near and far. One person rings a bell or taps a spoon on a cup while the other walks away, one step at a time, saying loud, medium or quiet.

Answer keys

  • Who or what made it? Animal or person: moo, waaah, woof, singing; A thing or machine: ding, honk, boom, ring ring
  • Near and far prediction: Quieter than when I am next to it; what happened: It got quieter with every step
  • Show what you know
    1. We hear with our... → ears
    2. A drum booms. What is the source of the sound? → the drum
    3. A sound is made when something... → shakes very fast
    4. You walk away from a ringing bell. The sound gets... → quieter
    5. Which of these is a source of sound? → a barking dog
    6. You hum and feel your throat. What do you feel? → shaking
    7. A sound hurts your ears. What should you do? → cover your ears and move away
    8. Stop a rubber band shaking with your finger. What happens to the sound? → it stops
Lesson 7: Electricity and Magnets9 steps · about 25 minutes · 9 objectives

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 1)

The steps

  2. Electricity, or not? · Does this need electricity to work? Tap the right bin.
  3. Where does it come from? Stay safe · Electricity comes from the wall or from batteries. It is useful and it can hurt. Press Next .
  4. Before electricity · Long ago there was no electricity in houses. Tap each picture to see what people did instead.

Do the experiment for real

Answer keys

  • Will it stick? paperclip: Sticks; wooden block: Does not stick; steel key: Sticks; plastic cup: Does not stick; steel spoon: Sticks; paper: Does not stick; kitchen foil: Does not stick; iron nail: Sticks
  • Show what you know
    1. Which of these needs electricity to work? → a lamp
    2. Which of these works with no electricity? → a bicycle
    3. What is a battery? → electricity stored in a little box
    4. What goes into a wall socket? → only a plug
    5. A magnet is held near a paperclip. What happens? → the paperclip jumps to the magnet
    6. A magnet is held near a wooden block. What happens? → nothing
    7. Long ago, with no electricity, people lit their homes with... → candles and oil lamps
    8. You leave a room for the day. To use less electricity you should... → switch the light off
Lesson 8: Our Earth, Our Sun11 steps · about 25 minutes · 12 objectives

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 1)

  • 1ESs.01 Know that Earth is the planet on which we live.
  • 1ESp.01 Know that Earth is mostly covered in water.
  • 1TWSp.02 Make predictions about what they think will happen.
  • 1TWSc.04 Follow instructions safely when doing practical work.
  • 1TWSa.01 Describe what happened during an enquiry and if it matched predictions.
  • 1TWSc.05 Collect and record observations and/or measurements by annotating images and completing simple tables.
  • 1ESp.02 Describe land as being made of rock and soil.
  • 1TWSc.01 Sort and group objects, materials and living things based on observations of the similarities and differences between them.
  • 1ESs.02 Describe the Sun as a source of heat and light, and as one of many stars.
  • 1SIC.01 Talk about how some of the scientific knowledge and thinking now was different in the past.
  • 1SIC.03 Know that everyone uses science and identify people who use science professionally.
  • 1TWSp.01 Ask questions about the world around us and talk about how to find answers.

The steps

  1. Where do we live? · Press Next and zoom out, from your house to the whole planet.
  2. Mostly water? · Is Earth mostly water or mostly land? Predict, then catch the globe ten times and see where your finger lands.
  3. Count the catches · Put your catches into the table. How many landed on %s ?
  4. Dig into the land · What is land made of? Press Dig and go down.
  5. Rock, soil or water? · Is this rock , soil or water ? Tap the right bin.
  6. The Sun brings light and heat · Press Next and watch the Sun come up. What does it bring?
  7. Sun or shade? · Two cups of water: one in the sun , one in the shade . Which gets warmer? Predict first.
  8. The Sun is a star · At night you can see many stars. Press Next to find out what the Sun really is.
  9. Long ago, and the people who look up · Long ago people thought different things about the Earth and the Sun. Tap each picture.
  10. Ask a question about the sky · Look up. Tap a question you would like to ask about the sky.
  11. Show what you know · Time to show what you know. Tap the answer.

Do the experiment for real

  • Mostly water?. Any ball with blue and green paper stuck on (or a real globe). Throw and catch it ten times and see whether a fingertip lands on water or land.
  • Sun or shade?. Two cups of water, one in the sun and one in the shade. Feel them with a fingertip after an hour. Never look straight at the Sun.

Answer keys

  • Mostly water? prediction: More water than land; what happened: More landed on water than on land
  • Rock, soil or water? Rock: pebble, mountain, sand; Water: the sea, river, lake; Soil: garden soil, mud
  • Sun or shade? prediction: The cup in the sun; what happened: The cup in the sun got much warmer than the cup in the shade
  • Show what you know
    1. What is the name of the planet we live on? → Earth
    2. Most of Earth is covered in... → water
    3. Dig down through the soil. What do you find underneath? → rock
    4. What is soil? → the dark, crumbly top layer of the land
    5. The Sun gives us... → light and heat
    6. Which cup of water got warmer? → the one in the sun
    7. What is the Sun? → a star, the nearest one to Earth
    8. Long ago some people thought the Earth was flat. How do we know it is round? → photographs from space show it
