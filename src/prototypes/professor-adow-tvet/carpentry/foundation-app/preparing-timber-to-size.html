Preparing Timber to Size

0

What this lesson is about

Group the hand tools by what they do, name the parts of a smoothing plane, take four damaged tools out of service, choose timber for a job, and take a rough-sawn board to a straight, square, finished size.

By the end of this lesson you can:

  1. Group hand tools by what they do, not by what they are made of.
  2. Name the parts of a smoothing plane.
  3. Inspect a tool and decide whether it is fit to use, repaired or replaced.
  4. Choose timber for a job and mark it with an allowance for sawing and planing.
  5. Plane a face side and a face edge square to it, and mark both.
  6. Set a marking gauge to a dimension and plane to the gauge lines.
  7. State the rules that make a power planer safe to stand at.
Where this leadsThis is foundation work. It is not a unit of the Kenyan Carpentry & Joinery Level 4 qualification — all eight of those units assume you already have tools and joints. Preparing stock to size is behind every one of them, and most directly behind Door and Window Frames (0732 251 02D), Timber Furniture Items (0732 351 06D) and Timber Floors and Framed Structures (0732 351 07D).

The smoothing plane

Assessed here

What goes wrong hereKeeping even pressure from one end of the stroke to the other. The pressure moves: on the front knob as the plane goes on, on the rear tote as it comes off. Even pressure rounds both ends of the board, and a board rounded at the ends is not straight however many shavings you take.

What goes wrong herePlaning all four faces true and then wondering why nothing fits. Only two surfaces are planed true on their own: the face side and the face edge. 7

Setting the gauge

Assessed here · and at the bench

What goes wrong hereSetting the gauge against the rule by eye and locking it without checking. The thumbscrew often drags the stem a fraction as it tightens. Set it, lock it, and THEN measure it again — a gauge one millimetre out puts every line one millimetre out, invisibly and consistently.
