What Stage 2 computing covers

Computational ThinkingAlgorithms that are precise: following and understanding them, finding and correcting the one step that is wrong, working out which steps a task needs, predicting what an algorithm will produce, and giving instructions exact enough to draw a picture or build a tower.
ProgrammingA program tells the computer how to run an algorithm; building programs with the repeat block; planning the instructions for each object; testing as you go; debugging with a partner; and steering a floor robot to a destination.
Managing DataWhy data is kept on computers; collecting categorical data for a purpose, and the ways of doing it; which questions give statistical data and which do not; presenting data as a graph; and using data to solve a problem.
Networks and Digital CommunicationThe devices that can join a network and the internet; two devices doing together what neither can alone; wired and wireless networks; telling when a network is there and when it is not; and what sharing over a network means, including its risks.
Computer SystemsHardware and software, by their proper names; what makes a device easy to use; input devices and output devices; the jobs computers do better than people; choosing a device for the place and the purpose; and robots in stories against robots that work.

Do it unplugged, for real

  • Tell Robo how to draw a house. One person gives instructions for drawing a house; the other draws EXACTLY what is said and nothing more. 'Draw a shape' gets a shape. Then swap, and try to be precise enough that the drawing comes out right first time.
  • Follow the algorithm: a cup of tea. Write the steps on cards, one per card. The child reads them out and the grown-up does EXACTLY what each card says, silly mistakes and all - a computer follows its algorithm the same way.
  • Draw what I say. You need: Paper, a pencil, a grown-up. You give instructions for drawing a house. Your grown-up draws EXACTLY what you say and nothing more. 'Draw a shape' gets a shape. 'Draw a roof' with no place gets a roof anywhere. Swap over. Look for: How many tries until the drawing came out right? Precise instructions get it right first time.
  • Tea for a robot. You need: A grown-up in the kitchen. Say the tea algorithm, one step at a time. Your grown-up is the robot. If you say 'pour the water' before 'get a cup', watch what the robot does. Say it again, precisely, in order. Look for: A robot needs the cup BEFORE the water.
  • Needed or not. You need: Paper and a pencil. Write eight steps for a job, and hide two steps in it that are not needed. Give it to a grown-up to cross out the ones that do not belong. Swap over. Look for: A step the task does not need is a step that should not be there.

Do it unplugged, for real

  • Find the bug and fix it: a cup of tea. Say the steps of a job in the wrong order, or with one silly step in it, and ask the child to spot the bug and say what it should be.
  • Follow it exactly: a cheese sandwich. Write the steps on cards, one per card. The child reads them out and the grown-up does EXACTLY what each card says, silly mistakes and all - a computer follows its algorithm the same way.
  • Predict, then do. You need: Counters, buttons or sweets. A grown-up writes a three-step algorithm: start with 6, take away 2, add 3. Say the output BEFORE you touch the counters. Then do the steps and check. Look for: Did your prediction match? Try a four-step one.
  • Bug in the routine. You need: A grown-up. Your grown-up says a routine with one bug in it: cup, water, tea bag, milk. Say which step is the bug and whether it is wrong or just too early. Say the corrected routine. Look for: Some bugs are wrong steps; some are right steps in the wrong place.
  • Precise or vague hunt. You need: A day at home. Listen out for instructions people give: 'put it there', 'tidy up a bit', 'put the cup on the table'. Say which ones are precise and which are vague. Make a vague one precise. Look for: 'Tidy up a bit' becomes 'put the books on the shelf'.

    4. Say hop. Say hop. Say hop. Say stop. What is the LAST word you say? → stop
    5. Put on one sock. Put on the other sock. Take one sock off. How many socks are on? → 1

    • Get ready for bed the bug is step 4, Lights off; it belongs after Get into bed
  • Precise, or vague? Precise: turn left at the shop, take three steps forward, put the cup on the table, add two spoons of sugar; Vague: go somewhere, walk a bit, put it there, add some stuff

Do it unplugged, for real

  • Build the program. Draw the blocks on paper cards (move right, jump, say hello). The child lays out a program; the grown-up is the computer and acts it out block by block.
  • Repeat cards. You need: Paper cards, a pen, a grown-up. Write 'jump' on a card and 'repeat 3 times' on another. Put the repeat card BEFORE the jump card. Your grown-up is the computer and does the cards. Try repeat 3 times with nothing after it. What happens? Look for: A repeat repeats the card after it. With nothing after it, it repeats nothing.
  • Same output?. You need: Paper cards. Lay out: jump, jump, jump, jump. Lay out: repeat 4 times, jump. A grown-up runs both. Do they do the same thing? Look for: Same output, fewer cards.
  • Test as you go. You need: Paper cards, a grown-up. Build a program two cards at a time. After every two, your grown-up runs it. Hide one wrong card somewhere. See how fast the test finds it. Try building all eight cards first, then testing once. Which was faster to fix? Look for: The bug is always in the newest cards.

Answer keys

  • Build the program
    • Jump, Jump, Move left → blocks jump, jump, left
    • Say hello, Grow, Move right, Move right → blocks say, grow, right, right
    • Spin, Shrink, Jump, Say hello, Move left → blocks spin, shrink, jump, say, left
  • The repeat block Repeat 3 times, then jump. What does the cat do? → Jumps three times
  • Use the repeat block
    • Jump 3 times → blocks repeat3, jump (a repeat block is required)
    • Move right 4 times, Say hello → blocks repeat4, right, say (a repeat block is required)
    • Spin 2 times, Grow → blocks repeat2, spin, grow (a repeat block is required)
    • What will the cat do when this program runs? → Move right 3 times, then jump once
  • Same output, or different? Same output: repeat 3 times, jump / jump, jump, jump, repeat 4 times, spin / spin, spin, spin, spin, repeat 2 times, move left / move left, move left; Different output: repeat 2 times, move right / move right, move right, move right, repeat 2 times, say hello / say hello, repeat 3 times, grow / grow, grow
  • Test as you go
    1. You have built 2 blocks of a 6-block program. What is the best thing to do next? → run it now and check, then add more
    2. Sami builds all 10 blocks, runs it once, and it goes wrong. Why is that harder to fix? → the bug could be in any of the 10 blocks
    3. When should you test a program? → regularly, while you are building it
    4. Your program worked after 2 blocks. You add 2 more and now it goes wrong. Where is the bug? → in the 2 new blocks
  • Show what you know
    1. What does a program do? → tells the computer how to run an algorithm
    2. The algorithm says: jump, say hello. Which program is right? → jump block, say hello block
    3. What does 'repeat 3 times, jump' do? → jumps three times
    4. Which program has the SAME output as 'move right, move right, move right, move right'? → repeat 4 times, move right
    5. Where does the repeat block go? → before the block it repeats
    6. Why test a program regularly while building it? → a bug can only be in the newest blocks
    7. A repeat block with nothing after it... → repeats nothing
    8. You want the cat to spin twice using the FEWEST blocks. Which? → repeat 2 times, spin
