1

What each digit is worth

A digit is worth different amounts in different places. The same 8 can mean 8, or 80, or 80,000 — the column it sits in decides.

2

The same number, regrouped

Swap one thousand for ten hundreds and you still have the same number — it is only stored differently. That swap is called regrouping, and it is what you do when you subtract.

3

Counting on in steps

Counting on in steps of the same size works from any starting number. Find the step first — take one term away from the next — then fill the gap.

4

Smallest to largest

Put them in order, smallest first. Watch the negatives: −9 looks bigger than −2, but it is further left, so it is smaller.

5

Which one is it nearer?

To round a number, find the two neighbours it sits between and ask which is nearer. Exactly halfway is the one rule you have to be told: it goes up.

6

One number, five answers

The same number rounds to a different answer for every place you are asked about. So the question is never just “round it” — it is round it to the nearest what.
