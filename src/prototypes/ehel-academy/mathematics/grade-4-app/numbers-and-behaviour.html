Numbers and How They Behave

Saying it in words

Split the number into thousands, hundreds, tens and ones, and the name reads itself.

Odd, even, and what happens

Pair the counters. One left over means odd. Now add two numbers and watch what the leftovers do.

3

The shape that hides a number

The shape stands for a number nobody has told you. The rest of the sum tells you what it has to be.

4

What comes next?

Look at the step from each term to the next. Same step every time is linear. Changing step is non-linear.

5

Numbers that make squares

A square number is one whose dots make a perfect square. Watch what has to be added each time.

6

Estimate before you work

Round to something easy first. The estimate tells you roughly what to expect, so a silly answer stands out.

Tables, and a shortcut

Every table from 1 to 10 — and a trick: in a chain of multiplications you may regroup and do the easy pair first.

8

Does it divide exactly?

You can tell without dividing. Nearly every test only asks you to look at the end of the number.

9

Ten times, a hundred times

Multiplying by 10 does not “add a zero”. Every digit moves one place to the left, and the zero only fills the gap.

10

Below zero, in order

Further right on the line always means bigger — and that keeps working below zero, where −2 is bigger than −9.
