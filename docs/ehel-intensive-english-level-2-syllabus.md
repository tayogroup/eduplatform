## Overview

Intensive English Level 2 takes an adult from a secure A2 to a secure **B1
reaching towards B2** — an independent user, able to follow the main points of
clear standard input and take part in extended discussion on familiar and
unfamiliar topics.

Twenty units run in two bands: **B1, units 1 to 10**, and **B1+, units 11 to
20**.

Where Level 1 was organised by situation — the clinic, the market, the working
week — Level 2 is organised by **what English can do**. *Telling My Story*,
*Saying It Precisely*, *Experience and Change*, *Packing More Into a Sentence*,
*If and Unless*, *Joining Ideas*, *Opinions and Disagreement*, *Building Words*,
*Reporting What People Said*, *The Passive Voice*. The subject of the course has
become the language itself.

Unit 1 sets the tone by refusing an easy start: it opens on an account of the
learner's own life, and says plainly that this is harder than it sounds, because
a life is not a sequence of events but a set of turning points.

Across the level: **571 vocabulary items**, **61 readings**, **240 quiz
questions**, **80 learning outcomes**, **120 speaking tasks**, **80 writing
tasks**, **69 grammar lessons**, **120 activities** and **80 self-assessment
statements**.

The level ends in a **B1 exit test across all four skills**.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Level 2 assumes Level 1, and the entry condition is explicit: **passing Level
1's A2 exit test**.

A learner arriving from outside takes the **Level 2 Placement Exam** first: 30
questions, 30 marks, about 30 minutes, in three sections — everyday words and
phrases (11), grammar patterns (10), and reading and communication (9). Attempts
are unlimited and it is never a fail.

Its lowest band does not soften the answer, and the wording is worth keeping:
*"Your English will grow fastest on a strong foundation. Start with Level 1 — it
moves at your pace, and this exam will be here whenever you are."*

### Homework

Every unit carries **one assignment**, **20 across the level**.

Each is a recording plus written work. Unit 1 is representative and shows the
level's character: four minutes of your own story, spending at least half the
time on one thing, using at least four adjective-and-preposition pairs — then
the written half. The instruction to spend half the time on one thing is the
level in miniature: it is training depth, not coverage.

Alongside each assignment: 6 speaking tasks, 4 writing tasks, roughly 3–4
grammar lessons, 6 activities and 12 quiz questions per unit.

The capstone in Unit 20, *My English Voice*, is four parts and assesses the
level: a two-minute benchmark recording on a genuinely difficult subject, kept
and dated, with a separate list of every avoidance found on a second listening;
a portfolio; three recordings in total; a plan; and a written assessment.

That first instruction — record yourself, then listen again and write down every
place you avoided something — is the most demanding thing either level asks, and
it is worth telling a learner in advance that it is coming.

### Assessment and grading

**Assignments — 620 marks across the level.** Units 1 and 2 at 25 marks, Units 3
to 19 at 30, and the Unit 20 capstone at 60.

Work is marked on **four skills**, eight criteria at four marks each — the same
rubric as Level 1:

- **Speaking** — Pronunciation, Fluency, Interaction
- **Writing** — Accuracy, Range, Purpose
- **Listening** — Understanding
- **Reading** — Real-world documents

> **The rubrics and the marks do not agree.** Eight criteria total **32 marks**;
> a unit assignment is worth **25 or 30**. That holds in all 20 units, and none
> carries marking guidance reconciling them.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Record, listen back, and write down what you avoided.** The capstone
  formalises it; the habit should start in Unit 1.
- **Go deep rather than wide.** Half your speaking time on one thing is a rule
  from the first assignment onward.
- **Disagree in English.** *Opinions and Disagreement* is a whole unit, and
  Interaction is a marked criterion.
- **Expect to be uncomfortable.** The level opens by asking for your own life
  story and closes by asking you to catalogue your own avoidances.
