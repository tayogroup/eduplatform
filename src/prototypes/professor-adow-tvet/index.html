What the app assesses, and what it does not Every step states whether the platform can assess it or whether an assessor signs it off, and the three modules differ honestly. Digital Skills is assessed here most — 71% — because naming a part, choosing wired over wireless and ordering a diagnosis are judgements rather than hand skills. Shapes and Measurements is 63%: a reading or a calculation is right or it is not. Carpentry is only 29% — sawing to a line is judged on the work, not on a screen. No module claims to deliver a Kenyan Level 4 unit. Carpentry names the units it feeds; Digital Skills names none, because the only Kenyan framework in hand is the carpentry one and an invented code would be worse than an honest blank.
Not yet trade-reviewed The performance criteria and the teaching were written from published curricula and have not been read by a practising tradesperson or a TVET assessor. That review comes before any of this is put in front of a learner.
