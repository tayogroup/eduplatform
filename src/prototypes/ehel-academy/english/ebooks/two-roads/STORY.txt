TWO ROADS
Grade 3 - Level 3
Amal series, book 3 of 4, from the reading of the same name (Term 2, Unit 6: Describing People and Things)

1. Two Roads. Written by Ehel Academy. Illustrated by Ehel Academy Learning Studio.
2. There are two roads near my home. They both lead to the same market.
3. The first road is smooth and easy to walk on. It was paved a few years ago.
4. Cyclists use it every morning, because their wheels glide over it without a single bump.
5. The second road is much older. It is rough, so I watch my feet the whole way.
6. Some of the stones are as big as a fist, and puddles collect between them when it rains.
7. My father takes the rough road every day, because it is shorter than the smooth one.
8. "My old boots are tough," he says, and he laughs at the wet stones.
9. My little sister prefers the smooth road, because she can ride without stopping.
10. I like to explore both: one on quiet mornings, and one when I want an adventure.
11. They are not similar at all, and that is exactly what I like about them.
12. Whichever road I choose, I remember what my father told me. Both of them arrive.

Unit vocabulary reinforced: rough, tough, smooth, similar, favourite, extra, careless, able.
Themes: comparing two things properly instead of ranking them
Cameos: the paved road, the stony one, the father's tough boots and the sister's scooter are all the Unit 6 reading's own.
