Grade 1 Global Perspectives

Eight lessons, in the order they are meant to be done. Every one is about finding things out and talking about them: a question to build, a classmate to ask, a picture to read, a problem to solve, a garden to plant together. About 345 minutes in all, one lesson a week.

Lesson 1

Ask Away

Find out what a question is, meet the six question words, build your own questions about pets, and read two pictures to find out what they tell us.

What We Know

Everybody knows something. Put what you know about keeping healthy on the class board, say one thing out loud, and answer questions with the information that was actually asked for.

15 steps · about 40 min · stickersStart Lesson 4

What Happens Next

Every action has a next thing. Predict what will happen to you, watch it happen, then choose the action that would really fix a problem.

15 steps · about 40 min · stickersStart Lesson 5

Which One Helps?

A book, a photo, a map, a person, a real thing: which one would help you find out about food? Choose it and say why, then say what YOU think about the food we eat.

15 steps · about 40 min · stickersStart Lesson 6

Better Together

Plant a garden and paint a mural with three friends. Share what you have so everyone can finish, be kind when things go wrong, and then say what you did and what each friend did.

16 steps · about 50 min · stickersStart Lesson 7

Talk and Listen

Listen to three classmates talk about their families, ask each one a question about what they said, answer questions about your own family with the information that was asked for, and build questions of your own.

15 steps · about 45 min · stickersStart Lesson 8

Look Back

Seven lessons of finding out, thinking, choosing, working together and talking. Look back over all of them: what did you learn, what did you like, and what do you know now?

What Stage 1 Global Perspectives covers

ResearchAsking questions about a topic; talking about what a picture or a book tells us; asking classmates to find things out; and recording what we found in a pictogram or a simple chart.
AnalysisSaying what we already know about a topic; talking about what a pictogram shows; thinking about what happens to us because of what we do; and choosing an action that could fix a problem.
EvaluationChoosing the source that would help us find out about a topic, and saying why; and saying what we think about a topic.
ReflectionSaying what we did to help the team, and what a friend did; saying what we learned in an activity; and saying what we liked.
CollaborationSharing what we have with a partner so that both of us can finish; and working kindly and positively with others.
CommunicationAnswering a question with the information that was asked for; and listening to others, then asking them a simple question about what they said.

Do it together, for real

  • Build a question about pets. Pick a topic (pets, the park, dinner). Take turns asking a question about it that starts with What, Where, Who, When, Why or How. Count how many different questions you can ask.
  • Read the picture: at the vet. Look at one photo or picture book page together. Ask: what does this picture tell us? Find three things in it and say a fact about each.
  • Look back. At the end of the day ask: what did you learn today? What did you like best, and why?
  • Three questions. You need: A grown-up who has a pet, or a favourite animal. Ask your grown-up three questions about their pet or favourite animal. Use a different question word each time: What, Where, Who, When, Why or How. Listen to each answer and say it back. Look for: Did each question word get a different kind of answer?
  • Question walk. You need: A grown-up and a walk outside. On a walk, point at something and ask a question about it. Your grown-up asks one back. Count how many questions you asked together. Look for: Which question word did you use most?
  • Read a picture together. You need: A picture book with a busy picture in it. Open a page with lots happening. Take turns to tap a thing in the picture and say what it tells you. Find something you did not notice the first time. Look for: What did the picture tell you that the words did not?

  • Read the picture: at the vet find: The vet is checking the dog's ears.; A cat is waiting for its turn.; The vet weighs every animal to see if it is growing well.; Medicine helps a poorly pet get better.; The vet is a doctor for animals.. What does this picture tell us? → A vet is a doctor who helps animals get better
  • Read the picture: at the park find: Ducks live on the pond. They eat plants and seeds from the water.; Dogs in the park stay on a lead so they do not chase the ducks.; A bird made a nest in the tree to keep its eggs safe.; The sign says: please do not feed the ducks bread. Bread is bad for them.; A squirrel is looking for nuts to hide and eat later.. What does this picture tell us about the ducks? → They live on the pond and should not be fed bread
  • Ask the right question
    1. You want to know the PLACE the rabbit sleeps. Which question? → Where does the rabbit sleep?
    2. You want to know the PERSON who feeds the cat. Which question? → Who feeds the cat?
    3. You want to know the REASON the cat hides. Which question? → Why does the cat hide?
    4. A picture shows a vet holding a rabbit. What does it tell us? → The vet is looking after the rabbit

  • Show what you know
    1. What is a question for? → finding something out
    2. Which word asks about a PERSON? → Who
    3. Which word asks about a TIME? → When
    4. Which of these is a question? → Where do birds sleep?
    5. What is a source? → something you can find information in
    6. The vet picture showed medicine on a shelf. What did that tell us? → medicine helps poorly pets get better
    7. The park sign said: do not feed the ducks bread. Why? → bread is bad for ducks

Do it together, for real

  • Ask the class: how do you get to school?. Ask everyone at home one question with a few answers (how do you get to work or school?). Draw one small picture per person in a row for each answer.
  • Talk about our pictogram. Look at the picture rows you made. Ask: which row is the longest? How many chose this? Did anyone choose that?
  • Record it on a chart. Draw two big circles or two columns on paper and sort what you found out into them, one fact at a time, saying why each goes where it goes.
  • Look back. At the end of the day ask: what did you learn today? What did you like best, and why?
  • Family survey. You need: Paper, a pencil, everyone at home. Ask everyone the same question: how do you get to work or school? Draw one small picture for each person, in a row for each answer. Look at your pictogram. Which row is longest? Look for: Did anyone give an answer you did not expect?
  • Count the cars. You need: A window that looks onto a road, and a grown-up. Watch the road for five minutes. Make a mark for each car, each bus and each bike that goes past. Count the marks. Which went past most? Look for: How many went past altogether?
  • Breakfast pictogram. You need: Paper and crayons. Ask everyone at home what they had for breakfast. Draw a row for each kind of breakfast. Tell somebody what your pictogram shows. Look for: Which breakfast was the most? Did anyone have something nobody else had?

Answer keys

  • Ways to find out We want to know how OUR class gets to school. What is the best way to find out? → Ask everyone in the class
  • Ask the class: how do you get to school? Amal: Walk; Sami: Bus; Nora: Car; Omar: Walk; Hana: Bike; Tariq: Walk. Pictogram: Walk 3; Bus 1; Car 1; Bike 1
  • Talk about our pictogram
    1. How do MOST of the class get to school? → Walk
    2. How many children walk? → 3
    3. Does anyone come by car? → Yes
    4. How many children ride a bike? → 1
  • Record it on a chart On foot: Amal walks, Omar walks, Tariq walks; On wheels: Sami's bus, Nora's car, Hana's bike
  • Ask again: what do you play at playtime? Amal: Skipping; Sami: Tag; Nora: Skipping; Omar: Football; Hana: Skipping; Tariq: Tag. Pictogram: Skipping 3; Tag 2; Football 1; Sandpit 0
  • Talk about the playtime pictogram
    1. Which game do MOST children play? → Skipping
    2. Does anyone play in the sandpit? → No
    3. How many children play tag? → 2
    4. Which game did the FEWEST children choose? → Sandpit
  • Finding out
    1. We wanted to know how the class gets to school. What did we do? → asked everyone and recorded the answers
    2. In a pictogram, one picture is… → one person
    3. The longest row in a pictogram shows… → the answer most people gave
    4. A row with no pictures means… → nobody gave that answer
  • Look back no key for what was liked. Things learned today: Find out about our class by asking everyone the same question.; Record each answer in a pictogram, one picture for one person.; Record what we found on a chart with two sides.; Talk about what a pictogram shows: the most, how many, and did anyone.; not today: how to swim; the names of the planets; how to tie a shoelace
  • Show what you know
    1. What is a survey? → asking everyone the same question and recording the answers
    2. Why do we record each answer? → so we do not forget it
    3. What is a pictogram? → a picture for every person, in rows
    4. Which way did the most children get to school? → walk
    5. Who should you ask to find out about YOUR class? → the people in your class
    6. On our chart, where does Hana's bike go? → on wheels
    7. Nobody chose the sandpit. What does the sandpit row look like? → empty, no pictures
    8. What did we record the playtime answers in? → a pictogram

Do it together, for real

  • What do we know about keeping healthy?. Name a topic and take turns saying one thing you already know about it. Stop and laugh when somebody says something that is about a different topic.
  • Answer what was asked. Ask the child a question and listen for whether the answer is ABOUT the question. Swap: they ask, you answer with something off the point, and they catch you.
  • Look back. At the end of the day ask: what did you learn today? What did you like best, and why?
  • What do we know?. You need: Everyone at home, and a topic. Pick a topic: the sea, or birds, or bread. Everyone says one thing they know about it. If somebody says something about a different topic, laugh and say: that is about something else! Look for: Did everyone know a different thing?
  • Answer the question. You need: A grown-up. Your grown-up asks you a question: what did you do at school today? Answer with something that is really about it. Now you ask them a question, and listen to whether their answer is about it. Look for: Was every answer about the question?
  • Healthy day. You need: Paper and crayons. Draw five things you did today that kept you healthy. Tell somebody what each one is. Say which one you know the most about. Look for: Which healthy helper did you forget? Add it tomorrow.

Answer keys

  • What do we know about keeping healthy? about keeping healthy: We wash our hands before we eat, Fruit and vegetables help us grow, Running and playing make our hearts strong, We need sleep every night, Brushing our teeth keeps them clean; not about it: Lions live in Africa, A bus has big wheels, The sea is salty
  • Answer what was asked
    1. What do you eat to stay healthy? → I eat fruit and vegetables.
    2. How do you keep your teeth clean? → I brush them in the morning and at night.
    3. What do you do to keep your body strong? → I run and play outside every day.
    4. When do you wash your hands? → Before I eat, and after the toilet.
    5. Why do we drink water? → Because our bodies need water to work.
  • About our topic? About keeping healthy: eating vegetables, going to bed on time, washing your hands; About something else: a red car, flying a kite, a teddy bear's name
  • Healthy helpers Sami asks: why do we need sleep? Which answer is ABOUT sleep? → Our bodies grow and rest while we sleep
  • Knowing and answering
    1. Our topic is keeping healthy. Which of these belongs on the board? → We need sleep every night
    2. Nora asks: what do you eat for breakfast? Which answer is about the question? → I eat porridge and a banana.
    3. Omar said his cat is called Tiger. Was that about keeping healthy? → No, it was about cats
    4. When everybody says what they know, the class… → knows a lot together
  • Look back no key for what was liked. Things learned today: Say something you know about a topic.; Keep what you say about the topic, not about something else.; Answer a question with the information that was asked for.; Say why water, fruit, sleep, exercise and washing keep us healthy.; not today: how to bake bread; how to ride a bike; the names of the planets
  • Show what you know
    1. What was our topic today? → keeping healthy
    2. Which of these is ABOUT keeping healthy? → washing your hands before eating
    3. Teacher Yasmin asks what you eat to stay healthy. Which answer is about the question? → I eat fruit and vegetables.
    4. A good answer is… → about the question that was asked
    5. Why do we drink water? → our bodies need it to work
    6. What does sleep do for us? → our bodies grow and rest
    7. Sami asks how you keep your teeth clean. Which answer? → I brush them morning and night.
    8. Omar knows about cats. Amal knows about washing hands. Do they know the same things? → No, people know different things

Do it together, for real

  • What happens to you?. Talk about a small choice and what happens next: if you leave your coat, if you go to bed late, if you share your snack. What happens to YOU?
  • Fix it!. Name a small problem at home (toys everywhere, a thirsty plant) and offer three things you could do. Which one actually fixes it? Try it.
  • Look back. At the end of the day ask: what did you learn today? What did you like best, and why?
  • If, then. You need: A grown-up and a quiet minute. Say something you might do: leave my shoes by the door, or eat all the sweets. Your grown-up says: and then what happens to you? Swap over. They say an action, you say what happens next. Look for: Was every next thing a bad one? Find a happy one.
  • Water watch. You need: Everyone at home, for one day. Watch for taps left running, all day. Every time you find one, turn it off and say: that fixes it! Count how many you turned off. Look for: How many taps did you catch?
  • Fix it at home. You need: A grown-up and a small problem. Find a small problem at home: toys everywhere, or a thirsty plant. Say three things you could do about it. Choose the one that really fixes it, and do it. Look for: Did your action fix the problem, or just move it?

Answer keys

    • You share your crayons with Nora. → Nora is happy, and we draw together
    • You leave your toys all over the stairs. → I will trip over them in the dark
  • Good for me, or not? Good for me: drinking water, wearing a hat in the hot sun, sharing with a friend; Not good for me: running into the road, forgetting your lunch, pushing in the line
  • Fix it!
    • The class plants are droopy → Give them some water
    • The playground is covered in litter → Pick it up with a grown-up and put it in the bin
    • The tap is dripping and wasting water → Turn it off properly, and tell a grown-up if it still drips
    • Sami has no crayons and cannot draw → Share some of your crayons
  • What is water for? If we leave taps running and waste water, what happens? → There is less water for drinking, washing and plants
  • Next things and fixes
    1. You leave your coat at home in the rain. What happens to you? → you get wet and cold
    2. The plants are droopy. Which action fixes it? → give them water
    3. What is a consequence? → what happens next because of what you did
    4. The playground has litter. Which action fixes it? → pick it up and bin it
  • Look back no key for what was liked. Things learned today: Say what happens to you next because of something you do.; Predict a consequence before it happens.; Tell a good next thing from a bad one.; Choose the action that would really fix a problem.; not today: how to swim; how to bake bread; the names of the planets
  • Show what you know
    1. Sami left the tap on and ran off. What happened next? → the sink overflowed and the floor got wet
    2. You stay up very late. What happens to you tomorrow? → you are tired at school
    3. You share your crayons with Nora. What happens? → Nora is happy, and you draw together
    4. Which of these is GOOD for you? → wearing a hat in the hot sun
    5. The tap is dripping. Which action fixes it? → turn it off properly, and tell a grown-up if it still drips
    6. Sami has no crayons. Which action fixes it? → share some of yours
    7. What is water for? → drinking, washing, cooking and plants
    8. Before you do something, what is a good question to ask yourself? → what will happen to me next?

Do it together, for real

  • Choose a source. Put a book, a photo, a map and an object on the table. Name a topic and ask which one would help you find out about it, and why.
  • What do you think?. Ask: what do you think about ___? Listen for an opinion AND a reason, and make sure the reason is about the topic.
  • Look back. At the end of the day ask: what did you learn today? What did you like best, and why?
  • Sources on the table. You need: A book, a photo, a map and a real thing, and a grown-up. Put four sources on the table. Your grown-up names a topic: birds, or the sea, or bread. Point to the source that would help, and say why. Look for: Was there a topic that none of the four could help with?
  • Opinion time. You need: Everyone at dinner. Ask everyone: what do you think about tonight's dinner? Everyone says what they think, and why. Check every reason is about the dinner. Look for: Did everybody have the same opinion? Did everybody have a reason?
  • Ask a person who knows. You need: A grown-up who cooks. Ask: how do you make my favourite food? Listen to all the steps. Say why that person was a good source. Look for: Could a map have told you that? Could a football?

    • how bread is made → the baker next door, because the baker knows how bread is made
