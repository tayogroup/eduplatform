For the grown-up. These seven lessons cover the Cambridge Primary Mathematics Stage 1 strands: number, fractions, pattern, geometry and measure, time, and statistics. They are ordered so that each lesson only needs what the ones above it have already taught — halving comes straight after doubling, and the data lesson comes last because it needs counting and sorting. Fractions at Stage 1 is halves only — quarters, thirds and equivalence are Stage 2, so nothing here is ever cut into more than two parts.

For teachers and parents

What each lesson teaches, in Cambridge's own words; what every step asks the child to do; the answer keys for the end-of-lesson check; and one thing to try at home with things you already have. Open a lesson to work through it, or print this page for the lot. The minutes are an estimate of about two and a half minutes a step, not yet timed against a real class, so treat them as a guide to the length of a sitting rather than a plan.

Lesson 1: Counting to Twenty 20 steps · about 50 min · 10 objectives · 15 check questions

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Mathematics 0096, Stage 1)

  • 1Nc.01 Count objects from 0 to 20, recognising conservation of number and one-to-one correspondence.
  • 1Nc.02 Recognise the number of objects presented in familiar patterns up to 10, without counting.
  • 1Nc.03 Estimate the number of objects or people (up to 20), and check by counting.
  • 1Nc.04 Count on in ones, twos or tens, and count back in ones and tens, starting from any number (from 0 to 20).
  • 1Nc.05 Understand even and odd numbers as ‘every other number’ when counting (from 0 to 20).
  • 1Ni.01 Recite, read and write number names and whole numbers (from 0 to 20).
  • 1Np.01 Understand that zero represents none of something.
  • 1Np.02 Compose, decompose and regroup numbers from 10 to 20.
  • 1Np.03 Understand the relative size of quantities to compare and order numbers from 0 to 20.
  • 1Np.04 Recognise and use ordinal numbers from 1st to 10th.

The steps

  • Count to 10 · Press the plus to put a counter in the frame. Count out loud with me. Then press Mix them up. Is it still the same number?
  • See how many · Do not count! Press look, see the pattern, then tap how many you saw.
  • Then to 20 · Fill the first frame. That is ten. Then keep going. Ten and one more is eleven.
  • Read the number · Look at the counters. Which number is it? Tap the number.
  • Write the number · Now write the number. Press the digit keys to build it.
  • Zero means none · Take the counters away one at a time. When there are none left, that is zero.
  • Counting in tens · A full frame is one ten. Press next ten and count in tens: ten, twenty.
  • Counting in twos · Socks come in pairs. Press next pair and count in twos with me: two, four, six.
  • Odd and even · Tap a number. If every counter has a partner, the number is even. If one is left alone, it is odd.
  • More or fewer? · Which group has more? Tap it.
  • Put them in order · Tap the cards from the smallest number to the biggest.
  • Numbers to 20 · Two numbers. Which one is bigger? Tap it. The number line will help you.
  • Compare to 20 · Two pictures. Does the first have more, fewer, or the same? Look at the tens first, then the ones.
  • First, second, third · Ten runners in a race. The one nearest the flag is first. Press next runner: first, second, third.
  • Guess, then count · Do not count yet! How many stars do you think there are? Tap your guess.
  • Estimate to 20 · How many apples? Slide your guess on the number line and lock it. Then tap each apple to count it into the frames.
  • Patterns you know · Press look. The picture shows for a moment. Then tap how many you saw, without counting.
  • Zero means empty · Zero means none at all. Look at each picture and tap the answer.
  • Every other number · Count, and say every other number. The numbers you land on are all even, or all odd.
  • Places in a line · Count from the front of the line: first, second, third. Tap the answer.

Check answers

  • How many counters? 7
  • A dice shows ⚄. How many dots, without counting? 5
  • Which word says 13? thirteen
  • Which digits do you press to write fourteen? 1 then 4
  • There are no apples left on the plate. Which number is that? 0
  • Which is more, 9 or 6? 9
  • And how many counters now? 14
  • Which number comes just after 16? 17
  • 2, 4, 6, 8, ? 10
  • 17 is 1 ten and ? ones 7
  • Count on in tens: 0, 10, ? 20
  • Which is more, 14 or 17? 17
  • Is 15 odd or even? odd
  • In the line A, B, C, D, who is 3rd? C
  • 20, 19, 18, 17, ? 16

Try at home

Gather up to 20 small things, such as beans or bottle tops. Ask your child to guess how many, then count them by touching each one once. Then put them in pairs: if every one has a partner the number is even, and if one is left alone it is odd.

Lesson 2: Adding and Taking Away 11 steps · about 30 min · 6 objectives · 11 check questions

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Mathematics 0096, Stage 1)

  • 1Ni.02 Understand addition as: counting on combining two sets.
  • 1Ni.03 Understand subtraction as: counting back take away difference.
  • 1Ni.04 Recognise complements of 10.
  • 1Ni.05 Estimate, add and subtract whole numbers (where the answer is from 0 to 20).
  • 1Ni.06 Know doubles up to double 10.
  • 1Nm.01 Recognise money used in local currency.

The steps

  • Adding · Put the two groups together. How many altogether? Tap the answer.
  • Taking away · Take some away. Tap the counters to cross them out.
  • How many more? · Two rows. Match them up in pairs, and the ones left over tell you how many more.
  • Making 10 · Ten friends in a row. Tap where to split them. Two numbers that make ten are a number bond.
  • Through 10 · Adding and taking away with bigger numbers. First tap the answer. Then we will go through ten together.
  • Doubles · A ladybird has the same spots on both sides. Double means the same again.
  • Doubles to 20 · Tap a double to see it in the frames. Double seven is fourteen: that is ten and four.
  • Money to 20 · Coins! Tap coins from the purse to pay the price. Watch the total.
  • Counting on · Put the bigger number in your head, then count on. Tap the answer.
  • Pairs that make 10 · Ten is a full frame. How many more make ten? Look at the empty spaces.
  • Know your coins · Look at the coins. The number on each coin says how many shillings it is worth. Tap the answer.

Check answers

  • 6 + 4 = ? 10
  • 8 red and 5 blue. How many more red than blue? 3
  • 10 − 3 = ? 7
  • 4 and ? make 10 6
  • Double 5 is ? 10
  • 8 + 5 = ? 13
  • 14 − 6 = ? 8
  • 6 and ? make 10 4
  • Double 8 is ? 16
  • 6 + 7 = ? (double 6 and 1 more) 13
  • 10 sh + 5 sh + 2 sh = ? 17 sh

Try at home

Put 10 beans on the table and hide some under a cup. Ask how many are hidden: if 6 are showing, 4 are hidden, because 6 and 4 make 10. For doubles, put the same number in each hand and ask how many there are altogether.

Lesson 3: Halves and Wholes 8 steps · about 20 min · 4 objectives · 10 check questions

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Mathematics 0096, Stage 1)

  • 1Nf.01 Understand that an object or shape can be split into two equal parts or two unequal parts.
  • 1Nf.02 Understand that a half can describe one of two equal parts of a quantity or set of objects.
  • 1Nf.03 Understand that a half can act as an operator (whole number answers).
  • 1Nf.04 Understand and visualise that halves can be combined to make wholes.

The steps

  • Two equal parts · A half means two parts that are exactly the same size. Look at each shape and say whether the two parts are equal.
  • Cut it in half · Only one of these cuts makes two equal parts. Tap the shape that is cut fairly.
  • Colour one half · This shape is cut into two equal parts. Tap one part to colour it. One of two equal parts is one half.
  • Half of a group · Half works on a group of things too. Share them onto the two plates so both plates have the same.
  • Half of a number · Half of a number means sharing it into two equal rows. Press share, then read how many are in one row.
  • Halve it in your head · Now try without the counters. Halving is sharing into two equal parts.
  • Two halves make a whole · Two halves fit back together to make one whole. Tap the half that finishes the shape.
  • How many halves? · If two halves make one whole, how many halves make more wholes? Count them.

Check answers

  • Are these two parts equal? Yes
  • Are these two parts equal? No
  • How much of this shape is coloured? one half
  • How much of this shape is coloured? the whole
  • What is half of 6? 3
  • What is half of 10? 5
  • What is half of 20? 10
  • Amina shares 8 mangoes fairly with her brother. How many does each get? 4
  • How many halves make one whole? 2
  • How many halves make 3 wholes? 6

Try at home

At snack time, cut a piece of fruit or bread into two parts and ask whether they are the same size: only two equal parts are halves. Then share 6 beans fairly between two people, so each person has half.

Lesson 4: What Comes Next 14 steps · about 40 min · 2 objectives · 15 check questions

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Mathematics 0096, Stage 1)

  • 1Nc.04 Count on in ones, twos or tens, and count back in ones and tens, starting from any number (from 0 to 20).
  • 1Nc.06 Use familiar language to describe sequences of objects.

The steps

  • What comes next? · A pattern repeats. Say the pattern out loud, then tap the bead that comes next.
  • The part that repeats · Every pattern has a part that repeats. How many beads are in that part? Tap the number.
  • Missing shapes · A shape has fallen out of the middle of the pattern. Look at the bead before it and the bead after it. Which one belongs in the hole?
  • Numbers that repeat · Numbers can repeat too, just like the beads. Say the pattern out loud, then tap the number that belongs.
  • Missing numbers · Some numbers have fallen off the track. Tap a gap, then tap the number that belongs there.
  • Make your own · Make your own pattern. Tap two or three beads for the part that repeats, then press repeat it.
  • Growing shapes · These patterns grow. Each one adds another row of shapes. Count them, then work out how many are in the next one.
  • Jump patterns · These patterns jump. Find the jump, then fill the gaps in order.
  • Find the missing numbers · This time nobody tells you the jump. Look at two numbers that are next to each other to work it out, then fill the holes.
  • My own number pattern · Now you make a number pattern. Choose a jump pattern or a repeating one, then build it.
  • 3 + ? = 7 · Three add something makes seven. Tap the plus to add counters until you reach seven, and count how many you added.
  • 9 − ? = 4 · Nine take away something leaves four. Cross out counters until four are left, and count the crossed ones.
  • The same on both sides · The equals sign means the same on both sides. Add counters to the light side until the scale is level.
  • The number machine · A number machine does the same thing to every number. Look at what goes in and what comes out. What is the machine doing?

Check answers

  • What comes next? teal
  • How many beads repeat? 3
  • 2, 4, 6, ? 8
  • 10, 9, 8, ? 7
  • What is missing: 13, 14, ?, 16 15
  • 3 + 4 = ? 7
  • 9 − 5 = ? 4
  • 4 + 5 = ? 9
  • 12 − 4 = ? 8
  • The machine does +2. 6 goes in. What comes out? 8
  • 12, 14, 16, ? 18
  • Pattern 1 has 2 squares, pattern 2 has 4, pattern 3 has 6. How many in pattern 4? 8
  • 3, 8, 3, 8, 3, ? 8
  • ?, 8, 10, 12, 14 6
  • Which shape is missing in the middle? teal square

Try at home

Make a pattern with things to hand, such as spoon, cup, spoon, cup, and ask what comes next. Then ask your child to make a pattern for you to carry on, and to point to the part that repeats.

Lesson 5: Shapes and Sizes 18 steps · about 50 min · 9 objectives · 13 check questions

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Mathematics 0096, Stage 1)

  • 1Gg.01 Identify, describe and sort 2D shapes by their characteristics or properties, including reference to number of sides and whether the sides are curved or straight.
  • 1Gg.02 Use familiar language to describe length, including long, longer, longest, thin, thinner, thinnest, short, shorter, shortest, tall, taller and tallest.
  • 1Gg.03 Identify, describe and sort 3D shapes by their properties, including reference to the number of faces, edges and whether faces are flat or curved.
  • 1Gg.04 Use familiar language to describe mass, including heavy, light, less and more.
  • 1Gg.05 Use familiar language to describe capacity, including full, empty, less and more.
  • 1Gg.06 Differentiate between 2D and 3D shapes.
  • 1Gg.07 Identify when a shape looks identical as it rotates.
  • 1Gg.08 Explore instruments that have numbered scales, and select the most appropriate instrument to measure length, mass, capacity and temperature.
  • 1Gp.01 Use familiar language to describe position and direction.

The steps

  • Flat shapes · Here is a flat shape. Press count the sides and I will count them with you. Then press next shape.
  • Sort the shapes · Read the rule, then tap every shape that follows it.
  • Solid shapes · A solid shape is not flat. You can hold it. Press next shape to meet them all.
  • Flat or solid? · Look at the shape. Is it flat, or is it solid? Tap your answer.
  • Turn it round · Press turn it. The shape makes a quarter turn. Does it look the same as before?
  • Long, longer, longest · Tap the pictures in order, from the shortest to the longest.
  • Heavy and light · Two things on a balance. The heavier one goes down. Tap the answer.
  • Full and empty · These jugs hold water. Full, empty, more or less. Tap the answer.
  • Which tool measures it? · Every measuring tool has numbers on it. Which one would you use for this job? Tap it.
  • Where is it? · Where is the ball? Look at the box, then tap the word that says where the ball is.
  • Faces and edges · Look at each solid shape. Think about its faces and its edges, then tap the answer.
  • Flat shape, solid shape · A flat shape lies on paper. A solid shape can be picked up and held. Tap the answer.
  • Will it look the same? · Imagine each shape turning. Which one would look exactly the same after the turn?
  • Longer or shorter? · Compare them. Which is longer, taller or thinner? Tap the answer.
  • Heavier or lighter? · Heavy, light, more and less. Think about how heavy each thing is, then tap the answer.
  • Pour and fill · Full, empty, more and less. Think about how much water each one holds, then tap the answer.
  • Read the numbers · Every measuring tool has numbers on it. Read the numbers, then tap the answer.
  • Between, above, behind · Look at the picture. Tap the words that say where it is, or which way it is going.

Check answers

  • How many sides does a triangle have? 3
  • Which shape has no corners? circle
  • How many sides does a hexagon have? 6
  • How many faces does a cube have? 6
  • Which solid shape has no edges at all? sphere
  • Is a cylinder flat or solid? solid
  • A square is turned a quarter turn. Does it look the same? Yes
  • Amina is taller than Musa. Musa is taller than Kiki. Who is the tallest? Amina
  • On a balance, the side that goes down holds the… heavier thing
  • A jug with no water in it is… empty
  • Which tool would you use to see how heavy a bag is? scales
  • Which tool would you use to see how hot the water is? a thermometer
  • The cat is below the table. So the cat is… under the table

Try at home

Go on a shape hunt at home: find something round, something with four straight sides, and something shaped like a box. Then compare two objects and ask which is longer, which is heavier, and which holds more water.

Lesson 6: Days, Months and Clocks 6 steps · about 20 min · 3 objectives · 5 check questions

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Mathematics 0096, Stage 1)

  • 1Gt.01 Use familiar language to describe units of time.
  • 1Gt.02 Know the days of the week and the months of the year.
  • 1Gt.03 Recognise time to the hour and half hour.

The steps

  • Days of the week · Seven days make a week. Tap the days in order, starting with Monday.
  • Months of the year · Twelve months make a year. One month is missing. Tap the one that belongs in the gap.
  • Short times and long times · A second is very short. A year is very long. Tap them in order, from the shortest to the longest.
  • Telling the time · A clock. The short hand tells the hour. When the long hand points to twelve, it is something o'clock. Press next hour.
  • How long does it take? · Seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, months and years. Think about how long each thing takes, then tap the answer.
  • Find the clock · Read each clock. The short hand tells the hour, and the long hand says o'clock or half past. Tap the answer.

Check answers

  • Which day comes after Sunday? Monday
  • How many months are there in a year? 12
  • Which is the shortest? a second
  • The long hand is on 12 and the short hand is on 3. What time is it? 3 o'clock
  • The long hand is on 6 and the short hand is just past 4. What time is it? half past 4

Try at home

Each morning, say the day of the week together and ask what day comes next. Look at a clock with hands, or draw one, and ask where the short hand and the long hand point at an o'clock time and at half past.

Lesson 7: Asking and Sorting 11 steps · about 30 min · 3 objectives · 16 check questions

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Mathematics 0096, Stage 1)

  • 1Ss.01 Answer non-statistical questions (categorical data).
  • 1Ss.02 Record, organise and represent categorical data using: practical resources and drawings lists and tables Venn and Carroll diagrams block graphs and pictograms.
  • 1Ss.03 Describe data, using familiar language including reference to more, less, most or least to answer non-statistical questions and discuss conclusions.

The steps

  • Ask everyone · Ask everyone the question. Tap each child to hear the answer, and watch the answers line up in rows.
  • Write a list · Here are the answers written as a list. Read down the list and count.
  • Make a table · A table is shorter than a list. It says each answer once, with how many children chose it. One number is missing.
  • Build a block graph · Build the block graph. Press the plus under a fruit to add one block for one child.
  • Read the block graph · Now read the graph. Which is the tallest? Which is the shortest? Tap the answer.
  • Make a pictogram · A pictogram uses a little picture instead of a block. One picture stands for one child.
  • Sort into a hoop · A hoop holds the things that follow the rule. Tap a card to put it in the hoop.
  • A Venn diagram · This is a Venn diagram: two hoops that cross over. A card that follows both rules goes in the middle, where the hoops overlap.
  • A Carroll diagram · A Carroll diagram has boxes instead of hoops. Every card has a box of its own. Where does this one go?
  • Answer the question · Here is a graph about pets. Every question has one right answer, and the graph tells you what it is.
  • What can we say? · What can we really say about this graph? Only one of these is true. Tap it.

Check answers

  • What is the first thing to do when you want to collect data? ask a question
  • In a block graph, one block stands for… one child
  • Which fruit column is the tallest? 🍍 pineapple
  • Which fruit did the fewest children choose? 🍎 apple
  • How many more chose pineapple 🍍 than orange 🍊? 3
  • In a pictogram the key says one 🍌 stands for one child. There are 4 bananas. How many children? 4
  • A table is better than a list because… it shows how many chose each answer
  • A hoop holds the things that… follow the rule
  • Two hoops cross over. A card in the middle… follows both rules
  • A red circle is sorted into hoops labelled red and circles. Where does it go? in the middle
  • In a Carroll diagram, how many cards are left out? none, every card has a box
  • A blue square goes in the Carroll diagram with rows red / not red and columns circle / not a circle. Which box? not red, not a circle
  • 6 children have a dog and 2 have a bird. Which is true? more have a dog
  • Our class graph says most chose pineapple. Can we say everybody likes pineapple? no
  • We asked our class. Does the graph tell us about the whole school? no, only the class we asked
  • Which word means the smallest number in a graph? least

Try at home

Ask everyone at home one question, such as their favourite fruit, and record the answers together as a list or with tally marks. Then sort some clothes or toys into two groups, such as red and not red, and count each group.
