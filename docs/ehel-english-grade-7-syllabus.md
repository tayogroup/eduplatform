**Grade 7 leaves Cambridge Primary.** It is the first year on **Cambridge Lower
Secondary English 0861**, and the first year since Grade 3 whose mark scheme
changes: 40 marks per unit assignment instead of 32, a 72-mark capstone instead
of 64, **432 marks** across the year instead of 352.

**The marks now reconcile.** This year awards 40 for a unit assignment and 72
for the capstone against two rubrics worth 32 — and until 2026-08-09 the extra
112 marks, 26% of the year, had no stated basis anywhere. Every assignment now
itemises its division. What remains is a confirmation rather than a gap; see
Assessment.

## Overview

Grade 7 English is the evidence year. It follows **Cambridge Lower Secondary
English 0861, Stage 7**, and the change from primary is visible in what a
learner is asked to hand in: from Unit 2 onward, **every single assignment
includes a source annotation**. Saying what you think stops being enough; you
show where it came from.

Ten units run across three terms. Unit 1 asks where a learner comes from —
family, identity and the stories passed down — and the year moves through
celebrations, clothing and sustainability, transport and cities, the outdoors
and environmental protection, sport and achievement, careers and personal
growth, media and news, and the digital world and future innovation.

The vocabulary moves with it: **297 words**, the most of any year, and words of
a different kind — *belligerent*, *alliteration*, *melancholy*, *bias*,
*perseverance*, *claim*. A learner reads **43 texts** and works through **77
learning outcomes**.

Unit 10 is an **evidence-led inquiry**, and it ends in an **oral defence**: a
five-minute recording in which the learner answers for the work rather than
presenting it. Alongside it go the inquiry product, an attributed source record,
four annotated portfolio pieces, and a reflection that sets a Grade 8
transition goal.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Grade 7 assumes Grades 1 to 6 — the whole of Cambridge Primary. There is no
foundation unit inside this course.

Before Unit 1 every learner sits the **Grade 7 English Placement Exam**: 36
questions, 36 marks, about 35 minutes — the longest and largest in the series.
Three sections:

- **Grade 6 skills** (22 questions)
- **Grade 5 skills** (8 questions)
- **Foundations, Grades 3–4** (6 questions)

Attempts are unlimited and it is never a fail. Four results:

- **Ready for Grade 7** — 80% overall with at least 60% in every section.
- **Ready, with a little review** — 50% or more; start at Unit 1 after the review
  lessons the report names.
- **Grade 6 skills below 40%** — treated as critical, and the report routes to
  four Grade 6 units — *There's a Lesson in That*, *Money Matters*, *Nature's
  Power* and *Entertainment and Media* — whatever the overall score. This is the
  only placement in the series that names four rather than three, which is a
  fair measure of how much Grade 6 carries into this year.
- **Let's build strong roots first** — the report recommends **Grade 6 English**
  as the course to take.

The review lessons open directly in whichever earlier grade they belong to, even
where that grade is walked one unit at a time.

The exam can be retaken at any point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- A writing folder that keeps drafts.
- **A source record kept from Unit 2 onward.** Every assignment from Unit 2 to
  Unit 9 requires a source annotation, and the capstone requires an *attributed*
  source record. A learner who does not keep sources as they go will be
  reconstructing nine units of them in Term 3.
- Materials for the Unit 10 inquiry product. Settle what it will be with the
  teacher early.
- A way to record audio. Every unit assignment includes a spoken piece, and the
  capstone defence runs to five minutes.
- A device that plays audio, and an internet connection for live sessions and
  research.

### Attendance

The course runs **60 live sessions** — 6 per unit, all 45 minutes.

Each session names its preparation: review the unit overview and the vocabulary
group beforehand.

Attendance is expected at all sessions; tell the teacher in advance where
possible. Sessions are recorded and a missed one should be watched before the
next. More than two missed in a unit and the teacher will contact the family to
agree how to catch up.

### Homework

Each unit carries **six activities** and **one assignment** — **10 assignments**
across the year.

An assignment is three pieces, not two. Unit 1 asks for a family biography, a
letter of advice, and a recording presenting the biography. **Units 2 to 9 all
follow the same shape: a written piece, a source annotation, and a recording.**
Every piece is improved against the Grade 7 rubrics before submission.

Expect **60–75 minutes** on an assignment across the unit's two weeks — more
than the primary years, because the sourcing is real work.

The Unit 10 capstone is five submissions: the evidence-led inquiry product, the
attributed source record, four annotated portfolio pieces, the five-minute oral
defence recording, and the Grade 8 transition reflection.

### Assessment and grading

**Assignments — 432 marks across the year.** Nine unit assignments at 40 marks,
the Unit 10 capstone at 72.

The course supplies two rubrics, unchanged from the primary years: **Writing**
on Ideas, Organization, Language and Conventions, and **Speaking** on Clarity,
Vocabulary, Sentence accuracy and Interaction — four criteria each, four marks
per criterion. **That is 32 marks.**

> **Every mark is now accounted for.** Until 2026-08-09 this year awarded 40 and
> 72 against two rubrics worth 32, leaving 112 of 432 marks — 26% — with no
> stated basis. The assignments now itemise it:
>
> | | Marks | Divided as |
> |---|---|---|
> | Each of Units 1–9 | 40 | Writing 16 · Speaking 16 · third piece 8 |
> | Unit 10 capstone | 72 | Inquiry product 16 · Oral defence 16 · Source record 16 · Portfolio 16 · Reflection 8 |
> | **Across the year** | **432** | **fully itemised** |
>
> The writing and speaking blocks are the two rubrics, four criteria at four
> marks each. The third 8 covers the piece Grades 3–6 never asked for — the
> source annotation, or the letter in Grade 7 Unit 1 — which is why this year
> awards 40 where they award 32.
>
> **The division is authored, not Cambridge-derived.** It is not in the source
> packs, which carry no mark scheme at all; it was written to match the work
> each task actually asks for, and every assignment's `reviewStatus` records
> that curriculum confirmation is still pending. Confirm it before the year
> starts: {{confirmed as published, or amended to}}.

**What earns full marks** is also unstated. Grade 3 prints marking guidance in
all ten units and Grades 4 and 5 in seven each; Grades 6 and 7 print none. The
rubric descriptors are all a teacher is given: {{how full marks are judged, and
who sets it}}.

**Unit quizzes — 100 questions across the year**, ten per unit.

**Final course quiz.** After the capstone: 30 questions, 30 marks, about 30
minutes, in three sections — Words and Meaning, Reading and Language,
Communication and Application. Pass mark **80%**. Attempts are recorded as
"teacher or school policy": {{attempts allowed, and what happens on a first
fail}}.

**Self-assessment.** Six *I can…* statements per unit, rated **Not yet / With
help / By myself**. No marks.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Come prepared** — the unit overview and vocabulary group before the session.
- **Attribute everything.** From Unit 2 the source annotation is part of the
  submission, and in the capstone the source record is *attributed* by name.
  Using a source without saying so is the one habit this year is built against.
- **Improve against the rubric before submitting**, on every piece.
- **Be ready to be questioned.** The capstone is an oral defence: the learner
  answers for the choices made, and a five-minute recording gives nowhere to
  hide an unread source.
- **Take turns and listen**; interaction is assessed.
- **A parent or another adult must be present whenever a learner uses a device
  or the Wehel AI tutor.** This holds in all ten units. The tutor is for
  practising language, not for producing the work — which matters more in a year
  where the work must be attributed.

### Support and communication

**If a learner is struggling**, the placement exam can be retaken at any point
and its report names the specific earlier units to revisit; those links open
those units directly. Grade 6 English remains available as a course for a
learner who needs the ground under this one.

**Extra help available:**

- The teacher, at the contact above.
- Wehel, the in-course AI tutor — with an adult present, for practice not
  production.
- Recorded audio for the vocabulary and the readings.

**How the school will contact you:** when marked work is returned, after a
missed assignment, after two missed live sessions in a unit, and at the end of
each term. The capstone is conferenced at its milestones.

**If you have a concern**, contact the teacher first. If it is unresolved after
{{n}} school days, contact {{school administrator role/name}}.

**For English as an additional language, or any additional need**, tell the
teacher at the start of the year.
