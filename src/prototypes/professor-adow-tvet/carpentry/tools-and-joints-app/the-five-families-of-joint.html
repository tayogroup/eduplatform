The Five Families of Joint

0

What this lesson is about

Meet the five families of wood joint, see two from each, sort ten joints into the family they belong to, and choose a joint for a job by asking which way the load pulls.

By the end of this lesson you can:

  1. Name the five families: corner, frame, widening, lengthening and crossing.
  2. Say what each family is FOR, not just what it looks like.
  3. Sort a joint into its family from the job it does.
  4. Choose a joint for a job, giving a reason from strength, appearance and load.
  5. Say why a table top needs a widening joint and a truss member a lengthening one.
Where this leadsThis is foundation work. It is not a unit of the Kenyan Carpentry & Joinery Level 4 qualification — all eight of those units assume you already have tools and joints. What you learn here is what they are built on: marking out, sawing to a line and fitting a joint feed directly into Doors and Windows (0732 351 05D), Timber Furniture Items (0732 351 06D) and Cabinets (0732 351 08D).

Corner and frame joints

Assessed here

What goes wrong hereReaching for a dovetail because it looks like good work. A dovetail resists being pulled apart in ONE direction. On a joint that is never pulled that way it is hours of work buying nothing, and a mortise and tenon would have been both quicker and stronger.

Widening, lengthening and crossing

Assessed here

What goes wrong hereTrying to make a wide board by lengthening, or a long one by widening. The names describe the DIMENSION they add. A widening joint runs along the grain and makes a board wider; a lengthening joint runs across it and makes a member longer. They are not interchangeable in any direction.
