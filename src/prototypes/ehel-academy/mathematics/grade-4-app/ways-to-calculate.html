Ways to Calculate

Warm up

1

Saying it in words

Split the number into thousands, hundreds, tens and ones, and the name reads itself.

2

Estimate before you work

Round to something easy first. The estimate tells you roughly what to expect, so a silly answer stands out.

Roughly, the answer will be about…

3

Adding it exactly

The estimate told you roughly. Now do it exactly: add the ones first, and when a column makes ten or more, the ten is carried into the next column.

4

Taking away exactly

When a column has too little to take from, exchange one from next door — a ten becomes ten ones. What you must never do is turn the column round and take the smaller digit from the bigger.

5

Tables, and a shortcut

Every table from 1 to 10 — and a trick: in a chain of multiplications you may regroup and do the easy pair first.

6

Double one, halve the other

Because you may regroup a multiplication, doubling one number while halving the other cannot change the answer — and it often turns an awkward sum into an easy one.

7

Multiplying a big number

Break the big number into its parts — hundreds, tens and ones — multiply each part on its own, then add them back. Every part has to be used, and forgetting the middle one is the usual slip.

8

Sharing out, and what is left

Take away whole groups until you cannot take another. The number of groups is the answer, and what will not make one more group is the remainder.

9

6s, 7s, 9s and factor pairs

Every rectangle you can make from a number shows a factor pair. Pick a number to see all of them.

10

Multiples and factors, both ways

One fact, two names. In 6 × 4 = 24 the small numbers are factors of 24, and 24 is a multiple of each of them. Which word you use depends only on which end you are standing at.

11

Does it divide exactly?

You can tell without dividing. Nearly every test only asks you to look at the end of the number.
