Marking Out and Cutting a Halving Joint

0

What this lesson is about

Name the three tools the job needs, make the workshop safe, put the stages in order, mark out a halving joint one line at a time, decide which side of the line to saw, and close the joint.

By the end of this lesson you can:

  1. Name the parts of a try square, a marking gauge and a tenon saw.
  2. Take every measurement from the face side and the face edge.
  3. Square a shoulder line and score a gauge line to half the thickness.
  4. Mark the waste before any cut is made.
  5. Saw on the waste side of the line, and say what happens if you do not.
  6. Fit a halving joint and check the assembly is square.
Where this leadsThis is foundation work. It is not a unit of the Kenyan Carpentry & Joinery Level 4 qualification — all eight of those units assume you already have tools and joints. What you learn here is what they are built on: marking out, sawing to a line and fitting a joint feed directly into Doors and Windows (0732 351 05D), Timber Furniture Items (0732 351 06D) and Cabinets (0732 351 08D).

The marking gauge

Assessed here · and at the bench

What goes wrong hereSetting the gauge by eye against the rule and never checking it. A gauge that is a millimetre out puts every cheek a millimetre out, on both members, in the same direction. Set it, lock it, then measure it again before you touch the wood.

The tenon saw

Assessed here · and at the bench

What goes wrong hereReaching for a rip saw because it is the one on the bench. A rip saw is filed to cut along the grain and will tear a shoulder to pieces. The tenon saw's fine teeth and stiff back are what make an accurate cut across the grain possible.

Which side of the line?

Practised here · bench sign-off

What goes wrong hereSawing down the middle of the line, because that looks like accuracy. It is the commonest way to make a slack joint. Half the kerf comes out of the work, so the joint finishes half a saw-blade undersize — and no amount of care afterwards puts wood back.

ADOW-CJ-TJ.03.1

8

Close the joint

Practised here · bench sign-off

What goes wrong hereForcing a tight joint together with the mallet. If it needs force it is too tight, and driving it home splits the member along the grain. Pare a shaving off the cheek and try it again — a joint should go together by hand and stay there.
