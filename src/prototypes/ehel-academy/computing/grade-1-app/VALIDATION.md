# Grade 1 Computing (computing/grade-1-v2) validation

Reviewed 2026-09-11, and revised the same day after the remaining fixes. Reviewer: Claude (automated measurement + judgement); no human teacher review yet.  
Against the 27-area framework in 'computing-grades validation.docx', which is unchanged and remains the criteria source for Grades 2 to 4. Scale: 1 Unsatisfactory; 2 Needs major improvement; 3 Acceptable with revisions; 4 Strong; 5 Excellent.  
What was reviewed: the standalone lesson build that ehel-comp-g01 launches (computing/grade-1-app, served as app/computing/grade-1-v2), not the Word-pack course under computing/grade-1, which the shell serves when no override applies and which declares a different framework.  

The Word report beside the owner's other validation reports (OneDrive: `computing-grade-1-v2 validation report.docx`) carries the same content; both are written from one script.

## Summary

The build teaches all 28 Cambridge Primary Computing 0059 Stage 1 objectives across eight lessons. A check by hand of every objective against the steps that claim it found each one taught through something the child does, and no step claiming an objective it does not teach. After this review's two rounds of fixes no objective rests on a single teaching step, 58 of the 67 teaching steps can tell a child “not quite”, each of the 81 questions has one key and a reason, and every lesson of all four grades was played to the end in a browser after the last change, with no errors and nothing wider than a phone screen.

The first round found one defect that was costing every learner something. The lesson overview and the “Computing world” placeholder ticked themselves off while the page was still loading, before the progress module existed. Measured on the live bundle: a lesson finished to 100% on screen was stored as 14 of 16 steps and never recorded as complete, a reopened lesson always started again at step 1, and a fresh lesson opened at 13%. Each Grade 1 lesson is estimated at 40 to 50 minutes against a 20-minute app block, so every lesson takes at least two sittings, and the second one always began from the start. The fix is in the shared Computing kit, so it also covers Grades 2 to 4, which had the same defect. It is committed and not yet deployed. After the second round it was verified again by the browser driver, now committed: on a copy of the deployed layout all eight Grade 1 lessons store every step and are recorded complete, and a reopened lesson opens where it was left.

The first round also corrected eight factual errors. The worst was the popular myth that the word “bug” comes from the 1947 moth, told in six places and keyed as the right answer to a quiz question. Two text colours below the 4.5:1 contrast minimum and three emoji that older tablets draw as empty boxes were fixed too.

The second round did the remaining work that needed no person: three activities for the objectives that had been thin, a recap of the lesson before and a two-question warm-up in every lesson, controls at least 44 px tall and a 13 px minimum text size, a network map that no longer cuts off its labels, a game question that read as broken English, and the browser driver committed with a check on the stored record. It also corrected two figures the first version of this report got wrong (see below).

What is left is work no measurement can do: a teacher reading the content and the question keys, a screen-reader walk-through, watching children use it and timing them, and opening it on the school's own tablets. There are also three owner decisions. The first is the redeploy: the live pages of all four Computing grades carry the progress defect until they are redeployed. The others are whether a quiz score should gate completion and whether to hide the “Computing world” placeholder. Self-hosting the fonts waits on a shared tool another session is building.

Change status at 2026-09-11, revised: 12 need no change or are done, 9 have their fix committed with a deploy or a human check still open, 6 are open and not started.

Average score 4.3 / 5 across 27 areas (4.1 in the first version); lowest 3 (Multimedia Quality); 8 areas at 5. Before this review's fixes, Learner Progress and Completion would have scored 2 and Content Accuracy 3.

## What changed since the first version of this report

- Four scores rose with the remaining fixes: Content Depth and Coverage 4 to 5 (area 3), Lesson Structure 4 to 5 (9), Assessment Balance 3 to 4 (11) and Bugs, syntax and errors 4 to 5 (27).
- Reading level (area 6) was misreported. The first version quoted Flesch–Kincaid grade 2.0 to 3.9, and 5.0 to 5.2 for lessons 7 and 8. The tool used for every other figure in this report does not reproduce those numbers on the pages as they were then: it gives 3.8 to 5.0 and 5.6 to 5.7. The correction is to the report, not to the text. The score stays at 4, because every line is spoken and much of the level is the vocabulary the objectives require.
- The depth comparison across grades (area 8) was wrong. The first version called Grade 1 the deepest per objective, at 3.0 teaching steps against 2.8 and 2.3 for Grades 3 and 4. Those two figures left out the in-lesson checks, which the Grade 1 figure counted. Measured the same way, the four grades are 3.2, 2.9, 3.9 and 3.4 today, so Grade 3 is the deepest.

## What the earlier verification could not see

Every earlier verification of this build played each lesson to 100% on a local server. That server deliberately does not serve the progress module, so no earlier check ever looked at what a finished lesson leaves in the school's record. “100% in the browser” was true of the page and false of the record, in every Computing lesson, from the first release on 2026-09-10 until this review. The committed driver's --record mode now looks.

## What was fixed during this review

Two commits on 2026-09-11, both pushed and NOT deployed. Grades 2 to 4 were rebuilt on the fixed kit each time; their lessons are unchanged apart from the kit's sizes, the network map and the Sort race wording.

First round, `04a1ff89f`:

- Progress: the overview and Computing world no longer tick themselves during page load. A finished lesson is now stored complete, a reopened lesson opens where it was left, and a fresh lesson opens at 0%. The fix is in the shared Computing kit (lib/computing.js), so it covers all four grades.
- The “bug” story, told as it happened in six places: people already called a machine's mistakes bugs, then found a real moth and taped it into their notebook, which made the word famous. The quiz question now asks what was found, not where the word came from.
- A treasure map is “an algorithm”, not a program; a computer “needs the steps written as code” rather than “cannot read our sentences” (four places); a rocket bug “cannot be fixed while the rocket is flying”; the web-search, spinning-circle and toaster explanations no longer overstate.
- Contrast: the sorting machine's caption (4.04:1) and the phone-screen hint (4.21:1) now pass at 5.0:1 and 5.1:1; Grade 4's loop label (1.29:1) now inherits the page's ink.
- Emoji: toothbrush (2020) became a tooth, a potted plant (2020) a jar for the step “get a pot”, an X-ray (2021) a hospital.

Second round, the remaining fixes, `ac37c2a2f`:

- Depth: three activities for the three objectives that rested on one teaching step or on demonstration: lesson 6 “Record the weather for a week” (1MD.03), lesson 7 “Joined to the internet, or not?” (1DC.02) and lesson 8 “Which program would you open?” (1CS.02).
- Structure and assessment: a “Last time” recap on lessons 2 to 8 and a two-question warm-up on all eight, both carried by the overview. The builder and the gate hold the warm-up to one key and a reason; the hub lists it for teachers and counts its time.
- Accessibility: the blocks, the wi-fi toggle, the listen buttons and the “We did this” buttons are 44 px tall, and the “You need” labels are 13 px. The network map is as tall as its lowest label needs; Grade 2's “smart watch” label had been cut off.
- Wording: the Sort race game shows the item and asks the sort step's own question, where it used to ask “Where does how many children chose apple? go?” (all four grades).
- Tooling: the browser driver is committed as computing/lesson-kit/drive-lessons.mjs, with --record checking the stored record and the resume on a copy of the deployed layout.

## Verdict on Grade 1 Computing

Nothing found stops the children already using Grade 1, and every defect this review found is fixed in the repository. Once redeployed, finished lessons reach their teacher and a reopened lesson opens where it was left. The build teaches all 28 Stage 1 objectives through activities, none now on a single step, holds the Stage 2 line, and carries no factual error that a careful read found.

Two things should happen before this is called validated rather than measured, and neither is engineering: a teacher reads the content and the question keys, and somebody watches a child use a lesson and walks one with a screen reader. The redeploy is the one decision that changes what a learner gets today.
