The eight gaps, and what closed them

One objective had no step at all. The other seven were taught in part: the step existed but stopped short of something the objective names in its own words. The quoted phrase is the framework's.

3Gt.01 Choosing a unit of time was never taught

“Choose the appropriate unit of time for familiar activities.”

The only objective with no step anywhere in the build. Time was taught as clock-reading and as intervals, but a learner was never asked which unit a thing should be measured in.

Filled — Sides, Sizes and Seconds, step 15 now asks it directly: blinking in seconds, a night’s sleep in hours, the school holidays in weeks, growing up in years. Eight familiar activities, each with two plausible wrong units.

3Gg.04 Nothing asked the learner to draw

“Draw lines, rectangles and squares. Estimate, measure and calculate the perimeter of a shape… and area on a square grid.”

Perimeter and area were both taught and both calculated, but every shape was drawn for the learner. The measuring half was covered and the drawing half was not.

Filled — a new step, Sides, Sizes and Seconds, step 8. The learner is asked for a rectangle of a given width and height (or a square of a given side), taps two opposite corners on the grid to draw it, and the page reads back its perimeter and its area from what was actually drawn.

3Nc.06 Spatial patterns only ever grew

“Extend spatial patterns formed from adding and subtracting a constant.”

The growing-shapes step added a constant every time. A pattern that shrinks is named in the objective and never appeared.

Filled — Rows and Rules, step 10 now shrinks half the time, starting high enough that the fourth picture is still positive. The check requires both directions to appear before it will pass.

3Gt.04 Intervals stopped at hours

“Find time intervals between the same units in days, weeks, months and years.”

The step handled minutes and hours between two clock times, and nothing longer.

Filled — Sides, Sizes and Seconds, step 15 now also asks days in a number of weeks, months in a number of years, and the months from one named month to another in the same year.

3Gp.02 The mirror was always vertical

“Sketch the reflection of a 2D shape in a horizontal or vertical mirror line.”

The reflection grid drew a vertical mirror on every round, so a learner never met the horizontal case — which is the one that catches people, because the shape flips top-to-bottom rather than left-to-right.

Filled — Sides, Sizes and Seconds, step 4 now draws either, says which it is, and the check fails unless both appear.

3Nf.08 Fractions were compared but never ordered

“Use knowledge of equivalence to compare and order unit fractions and fractions with the same denominator.”

The step put two fractions side by side and asked for a sign. Ordering three is a different act, and it is named.

Filled — Equal Parts, step 9 now alternates: half the rounds compare a pair, half put three fractions up with their bars and ask for them smallest first. Each set is either same-denominator or all unit fractions, which is exactly the pair of cases the objective names.

3Ss.02 Six ways to show data, and no step that chose between them

“Record, organise and represent categorical and discrete data. Choose and explain which representation to use in a given situation…”

All six named forms — tally, frequency table, pictogram, bar chart, Venn, Carroll — had a step each, so the first pass marked this covered. It read only the objective’s first sentence. The second sentence asks for a judgement between them, and nothing in the lesson asked for one.

This is the gap the TWM pass found rather than the coverage pass: Critiquing is defined as comparing representations to identify advantages and disadvantages, and having nowhere to put it is what exposed the missing sentence.

Filled — a new step, Ask, Count and Chart, step 8. Six situations, each with a form that genuinely suits it and a reason that names the trade-off: a tally is the only one you can keep up with while cars go past; a bar chart is for seeing which is biggest at a glance; only a Venn has an overlap; a Carroll gives “neither” a box of its own.

3Ni.09 Division without the estimate

“Estimate and divide whole numbers up to 100 by 2, 3, 4 and 5.”

The sharing step went straight to the exact answer. Its sibling objective for multiplication (3Ni.08) had an estimating step; this one did not.

Filled — Rows and Rules, step 7 now states the estimate before the learner answers, against the nearest multiple of the divisor, and the check refuses to pass without it.

Thinking and Working Mathematically

The framework lists eight TWM characteristics at the head of Stage 3 and says they are not a separate strand: they “should not be taught as a separate special activity but instead should be embedded throughout the curriculum”. So they are not objectives to tick off, and there are no TWM rows in the table above. This is a quality read on whether the lessons actually give a learner room to do these things.

The wrong reasons are either the misconception the lesson teaches against or something perfectly true that explains nothing — and the second kind is the point. Recorded as an approximation: the learner recognises a justification rather than producing oneapproximated
What was done about the last two, and what is still open. Every answer in this build is a tap — there is no free-text input anywhere in the five lessons, which was checked rather than assumed. A deck of tap-to-answer steps can ask a child to choose, sort, test and extend; it cannot ask them to say why in their own words, so a faithful Convincing step is not available to it. What is available is recognition, and that is what shipped: How do you know? now closes every lesson in Grade 3 and in Grade 1, 60 distinct claims between them, each with one reason that explains and two that do not. It is deliberately booked as approximated, not covered — recognising a good reason is a weaker act than giving one, and calling it coverage would report something nobody measured. Conjecturing is still thin and was left alone. Closing it needs the learner to form a question rather than pick one, and every device this deck has would again be a picker — the same substitution, with less of the characteristic left in it. Cambridge’s own framing expects TWM to be “stimulated… through practice, reflection and questioning”, which assumes an adult in the room; that is the part a deck cannot supply.

Accessibility

All six pages audited in a real browser: keyboard operation, accessible names, contrast against the ground actually painted behind each text, live regions, heading order and touch-target size. Everything below is fixed; the audit now reports zero findings.

  • Nine steps could not be used without a mouse. The worst of the findings. Steps built from cards, table cells, Venn regions and grid squares had click handlers on plain div, td, rect and circle elements — so a keyboard-only learner could not do the reflection, the drawing grid, the Venn, the Carroll, the fact family or the shape cards at all. Each now carries role="button", tabindex="0" and a spoken name, and the shell turns Enter and Space into a click on any of them.
  • Feedback was never announced. Twenty-two feedback and score lines changed silently, so a screen-reader learner got no result. Every .fb and .score is now a polite live region, set once in the shell rather than repeated in each slide.
  • Two contrast failures inherited from the Grade 1 shell. The muted label colour was 4.04:1 on the panel — under the 4.5:1 that small text needs — and a selected chip put white on lifted teal at 2.27:1. The muted tone is now 5.2:1, and the chip uses the dark ink on teal that the hub already uses. A third, an accent label at 14px on 3.28:1, is lifted and enlarged.
  • Three unlabelled SVGs in the regular-or-irregular step now sit inside a named button and are marked aria-hidden, so they are not announced twice.
Two of the audit’s own findings were the audit’s fault, and both were checked before anything was changed. It reported ~90 controls per lesson “on hidden slides but still in the tab order” — false: inactive slides are display:none, and tabbing for real reaches 25 stops with none on a hidden slide. It also reported several 1.00:1 contrasts — false: it treated rgba(255,255,255,0.07) as an opaque white ground rather than compositing it over the dark panel. The instrument was fixed first; 58 raw findings became 43 real ones.

What the verification caught along the way

Separate from coverage. Each lesson is exercised in a real browser, with the maths recomputed independently from what is drawn on screen rather than read back from the page’s own answer. Five defects were found that way, and every one was a piece of content outside Stage 3 or arithmetically wrong.

  • Eighths in the fraction equivalence table. 3Nf.06 names halves, quarters, fifths and tenths. Two pairs used eighths — and because there was no name for an eighth in the wording table, the slide threw and froze mid-lesson. Both the curriculum error and the crash were the same line.
  • Counting back past zero. Counting in steps of −100 could run 112, 12, −88. Negative numbers are Stage 5. Starts are now chosen so all four terms stay inside 0 to 1000.
  • A subtraction with a negative answer in the mystery box — “8 − 14 = −6” shown to a Grade 3 learner. The unknown is now built from the answer so the result cannot go below zero.
  • “Irregular” polygons that were equilateral. The irregular shapes were made by alternating the radius, which on an even-sided polygon gives every side the same length — precisely what the step tells a child regular means. They are squashed now, so the sides genuinely differ.
  • Check questions offering the same answer twice. Four generators could produce a duplicate option, leaving no single right answer. Fixed at the source, and a guard now regenerates any question whose options are not distinct.
One thing this audit cannot tell you. It checks that every objective is taught and that the maths on screen is right. It does not tell you whether a Grade 3 child finds these steps clear — that needs a child, not a checker.
