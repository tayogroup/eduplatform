## Read this before publishing

Three facts about this course are true of every Ehel Mathematics stage, and a
school should decide knowingly rather than discover them after a family has read
the syllabus.

1. **No unit has passed curriculum review.** All 17 units of Stage 6
   carry `Imported - curriculum review required`, as does the capstone. The
   content was structured from the source workbooks for screen presentation and
   has not been signed off by a curriculum reviewer.
2. **No lecture video exists.** All 17 units report
   `Video pending`, with no video and no poster image. If the syllabus or the
   catalogue implies video lessons, that promise is currently unmet.
3. **The capstone is one template repeated across all eight stages.** Its final
   product — an exhibition plan with labelled displays, a schedule, a budget, a
   visitor survey with a data display, and a written explanation of mathematical
   decisions — is *verbatim identical* from Stage 1 to Stage 8, as are its four
   project stages and its driving question bar the stage number. Only the unit numbers differ between stages.

Nothing below invents around those. They are stated where a reader needs them.

## Overview

Stage 6 Mathematics is the final year of Cambridge Primary, and it opens by asking a learner to hold the whole number line at once: millions at one end, thousandths at the other. It follows **Cambridge Primary Mathematics 0096, Stage 6**.

Seventeen units cover number and place value, sequences, averages, fractions and percentages, ratio and proportion, probability, angles, 2D and 3D shapes, data, the laws of arithmetic, and transformations. Addition and subtraction take two units, as do multiplication and division — split early and late in the year.

*Exploring Measures: Area & Time* is the one unit that combines strands rather than teaching one, which is a fair signal of where the year is heading: mathematics used together, not in isolated topics.

Across the year: **17 units** in three terms, **152 learning
outcomes**, **116 concepts**, **204 worked examples**,
**204 practice questions**, **204 fluency questions**,
**102 real-world problems**, **102 reasoning prompts**
and **204 games**. Each unit ends in a 12-question
challenge at a **80% pass mark**, and the year ends in a stage
capstone with a 34-question quiz.

**This is a self-paced course, not a taught one.** There are no live sessions, no
assignments to hand in and no marks in the course data — a learner works through
each unit and is checked by the unit challenge, the games and their own
self-assessment. What replaces the teacher is the **grown-up guide** attached to
every concept, written to the adult rather than the child.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Stage 6 assumes Stages 1 to 5. There is no foundation unit inside this course.

Before Unit 1 a learner sits the **Stage 6 Mathematics Placement Exam**: 34 questions, 34 marks, about 32 minutes. Attempts are unlimited and it is never a fail.

Sections:

- **Stage 5 skills** (20 questions)
- **Stage 4 skills** (8 questions)
- **Foundations (Stages 1–3)** (6 questions)

The report gives one of four results — ready, ready with a little review, a
critical section below its threshold, or *let's build strong roots first*, which
recommends **Stage 5 Mathematics**. Where the report names
earlier units to revisit, those links open the named unit directly.

The check can be retaken at any point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- Paper, pencil and **familiar household objects**. Every unit's "Practise
  together" activity asks for exactly that — build or draw a model of one idea,
  explain it to a family member, then write and solve a question of your own.
- A device that can display the course, with sound.
- An internet connection.
- A ruler and squared paper for the measurement, area and shape units.

Nothing on this list needs buying.

### Attendance

**This course has no live sessions.** There is nothing to attend and nothing to
miss, which is the single biggest difference between Mathematics and the English
courses at Ehel Academy.

What replaces attendance is a rhythm the school sets rather than the course:
{{how often a learner is expected to work, and how progress is checked}}. A
self-paced course without an agreed rhythm is the one most likely to stall
quietly, so put a real answer here rather than "at the learner's own pace".

### Homework

There is no homework to submit, because there are no assignments in this course.

What a learner does instead, in every unit: work through the concepts and visual
models, study the worked examples, complete the practice and fluency questions,
play the games to a mastery score of 3,
attempt the real-world problems and reasoning prompts, do the "Practise together"
activity with a family member, and finish with the unit challenge.

The one piece of work that leaves the screen is that activity — a model built or
drawn, explained aloud to someone at home. It is worth treating as the real
homework of this course.

### Assessment and grading

**There are no marks in this course.** Nothing is graded out of anything, and
nothing is compared between learners.

What exists instead:

- **The unit challenge** — 12 questions at the end of each unit,
  pass mark 80%. 17 of them across the year.
- **Games**, 12 per unit, each with a mastery score of 3.
- **Self-assessment** — *I can…* statements the learner rates themselves,
  133 across the year. No marks, and never used to rank a learner.
- **The stage capstone**, with a 34-question quiz.

> **A decision for the school.** With no marks and no assignments, this course
> produces a pass/fail per unit and nothing else. Whatever a family is told to
> expect on a report — a percentage, a comment, a completion count — the school
> has to define it: {{what is reported to families, in what form, and how
> often}}.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Do the practice, not just the reading.** The worked examples show a method;
  the 12 practice questions per unit are where it is learnt.
- **Explain your thinking.** Every unit carries reasoning prompts that ask for a
  reason rather than an answer, and the "Practise together" activity ends in
  explaining a model to someone at home.
- **Use the mistakes list.** Each unit's reference section names the common
  mistakes for that topic. Reading it before the challenge is worth more than
  repeating practice.
- **A grown-up is part of the design at this stage.** Every concept still carries a grown-up guide, and the activity is explained to a family member.

### Support and communication

**If a learner is struggling**, the placement check can be retaken at any time
and its report names the earlier units to revisit; those links open directly.
Stage 5 Mathematics remains available for a learner who needs the ground under this one.

## Before submitting for approval

1. **Decide whether to publish at all yet.** The units and the capstone are
   flagged as requiring curriculum review, and no lecture video exists.
2. Set the rhythm and the reporting — the two things a self-paced course cannot
   supply for itself.
3. Fill every remaining `{{...}}`.
4. Confirm term dates on the school's academic terms, not here.
5. Save as **draft**, then submit for approval. Only a school administrator can
   approve, never the author.
