Where Things Are and How They Move

Read a coordinate pair the right way round, plot points until they make a shape, find a position on a grid that has lost its lines, slide a shape without turning it, describe a slide someone else has made, and reflect a shape in a mirror line that runs either way.

For the grown-up: what to have to hand

Squared paper, and a ruler or any straight edge - the straight edge is what makes the no-gridlines step work, because you line it up from the point to each axis. A small cut-out triangle is worth having for the translation step: sliding a real one across the paper shows that it never turns.

Support and challenge
An easier way in

Say the order out loud every time: along the corridor, then up the stairs. Do that before writing either number down, and the commonest mistake in this whole lesson - giving the pair the wrong way round - cannot happen.

A harder challenge

In Step 41, reflect the shape in the vertical mirror, then reflect the RESULT in the horizontal mirror. Where does it end up, and is there a single translation or rotation that would have taken the original there in one move?

About

What this lesson is about

By the end of this lesson you will be able to…

  • Compare the relative position of coordinates, with or without the aid of a grid.
  • Use knowledge of 2D shapes and coordinates to plot points to form lines and shapes in the first quadrant.
  • Translate 2D shapes, identifying the corresponding points between the original and the translated shape.
  • Reflect 2D shapes in both horizontal and vertical mirror lines to create patterns on square grids.
Lecture

Unit lecture

The whole lesson, in three parts.

Part 1 of 3

Along first, then up

A coordinate pair names one place only because the order is fixed - the first number is how far along and the second is how far up. Swap them and both numbers are still right while the point is somewhere else entirely.

Part 2 of 3

A slide keeps the shape facing the same way

Translating a shape moves every vertex by the same amount in the same direction. Nothing turns and nothing changes size, so if one corner goes three right and two up, all of them do.

Part 3 of 3

A reflection turns it to face the mirror

Reflecting flips the shape across a line. Each point lands the same distance on the other side, so a vertex close to the mirror stays close and a far one stays far.

Step 36

Along first, then up

A coordinate pair says how far ALONG and then how far UP. The order is fixed, so (3, 1) and (1, 3) are two different places. Read the marked point and choose its pair.

Which pair is the marked point?

Try another point

Along the corridor, then up the stairs. The commonest mistake is to give the pair the wrong way round. Both numbers are correct and the point is still wrong, which is why the order is worth a saying you can repeat.
Step 37

Plot the shape

Coordinates do more than name a spot — plot several and they give you the vertices of a shape. Click the four points in the list, then see what you have drawn.

Plot these vertices

A shape is its vertices. Four points are enough to fix a rectangle completely — no measuring is needed, because the coordinates already say how long each side is.
Step 38

A grid with no lines

Maps and plans often mark only the numbers along the edges. The point is still exactly where the lines would have crossed, so you count from the axes instead of tracing a line.

Where is the point?

Line up with the edge. With no gridlines, put a ruler or a straight edge from the point down to the bottom axis to read the first number, then across to the side axis for the second.
Step 39

Slide it, do not turn it

A translation slides every point of a shape the same distance in the same direction. Nothing turns and nothing changes size, so every vertex moves by exactly the same amount.

Choose a translation

Where each vertex lands

Every vertex moves the same way. If one corner goes 3 right and 2 up, they all do. That is the check: work out one vertex, then add the same pair to the others rather than re-reading the grid each time.
Step 40

Describe the move

Reading a translation is the same skill backwards. The faded shape is where it started and the solid one is where it finished — say what happened.

Which translation was it?

Pick one corner and follow it. You do not need the whole shape. Choose a single vertex you can find in both positions, and the change in its coordinates IS the translation.
Step 41

Reflect it in the mirror

A reflection flips a shape across a mirror line. Each point lands the same distance on the other side, so a point close to the line stays close and a far one stays far.

Where is the mirror?

Each vertex and its image

A reflection is not a slide. A translation keeps the shape facing the same way; a reflection turns it round to face the mirror. A shape that looks unchanged after reflecting is one whose mirror line is its own line of symmetry.
