What this found

closedForty-eight teaching slides, and every objective covered

Stage 4 sets 46 content objectives across nine strands. This audit opened with 11 teaching slides against all of them, of which 6 were fully covered, 8 partial and 32 not covered at all. There are now 48 slides across six lessons, and all 46 objectives are covered.

Number was the last block. Numbers and How They Behave closes the nine that were untaught across 4Nc, 4Ni and 4Np and upgrades the five partials — number names in words, all ten tables, rounding to 10 000 and 100 000, and the two “Estimate and…” objectives that had never once asked for an estimate. Nothing in Stage 4 is now left uncovered.

closed4Nf.06 — the percentage is now named, not just modelled

The original finding was that Tenths and hundredths draws the hundred square this objective needs and never says percentage. Parts of a Whole slide 5 shades the same hundred squares and reads the result three ways at once — 25/100, 0.25 and 25% — with % explained as shorthand for “out of a hundred” and said aloud as “25 per cent”.

The model was never the problem. The name was, and the name is now attached to it.

closedThe three “Estimate and…” objectives now ask for an estimate

4Ni.05, 4Ni.06 and 4Nf.05 all open with the word Estimate. The lesson teaches the multiplying, the dividing and the adding accurately and well, and never asks the child what they think the answer will be before working it out.

All three are closed. Parts of a Whole slide 7 asks whether the answer will be less than a half, about a half or more, before it will show it. Numbers and How They Behave slide 6 does the same for the other two, rounding to the nearest hundred before multiplying and the nearest ten before dividing. Every generated problem there is constrained so the estimate is a useful number rather than zero — checked over 200,000 draws.

closedThe eight partials were near-misses, and every one is now closed

None of the eight is a misunderstanding — each teaches the right idea and stops short of the objective’s full wording. Rounding does 10, 100 and 1000 where the objective also names 10 000 and 100 000. Tables does 6s, 7s and 9s where the objective says all ten. Perimeter and area handles one rectangle where the objective also wants a compound shape and a formula. Adding fractions adds but never subtracts.

They were listed as partial rather than covered deliberately: a near-miss recorded as a pass is how a gap survives an audit. Each has since been met in full — rounding to all five sizes, all ten tables, compound area and the derived formulae, and subtraction of fractions alongside addition.

Scope of this audit

What was checked, and what was not

Checked: all 46 content objectives, against the published lesson. The objective list was extracted from the framework PDF and verified to have no numbering gaps in any strand — 4Nc.01–05, 4Ni.01–08, 4Np.01–05, 4Nf.01–07, 4Gt.01–04, 4Gg.01–09, 4Gp.01–03, 4Ss.01–03, 4Sp.01–02 — so nothing was quietly dropped by the parser.

How the evidence was gathered: by searching the slide prose and every string literal in the lesson’s script, because quiz stems and spoken lines live there and prose alone under-reports. Three apparent matches were checked in context and rejected: the 33 hits for data are data- attributes, thermometer belongs to the below-zero narration rather than to reading a scale, and bigger than describes an obtuse angle rather than comparing numbers.

Not checked: the eight Thinking and Working Mathematically objectives (TWM.01–.08), — they describe how a learner works rather than what they know, and cannot be evidenced by locating a slide.
