## Overview

Grade 6 English is the last year of Cambridge Primary, and it is built to be
finished with: a full year of topics, then a researched project defended in
front of an audience. It follows **Cambridge Primary English 0058, Stage 6**.

Ten units run across three terms, organised by subject rather than by form.
Unit 1 works through fables — *The Weaver and the Wind* and *Voices in the
Chamber* — and the year moves on through sport and health, life in the wild,
inventions and creativity, money, people and work, the power of nature,
entertainment and media, and the arts.

Across the year a learner meets **285 words** — more than any earlier grade —
reads **45 texts**, and works through **77 learning outcomes**. Six grammar
workshops run inside the final unit alone, bringing back the tenses, the first
conditional, relative clauses, precise description, reported speech and the
final edit.

Unit 10 is where this year separates itself. It is a **researched** capstone: a
multi-section product, **annotated source evidence** showing where the
information came from, three portfolio pieces, a **four-minute** presentation —
the longest talk in the primary years — and a written reflection. A learner
leaves Grade 6 having produced something sourced, made and defended, which is
the shape of the work that follows in lower secondary.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Grade 6 assumes Grades 1 to 5. There is no foundation unit inside this course.

Before Unit 1 every learner sits the **Grade 6 English Placement Exam**: 34
questions, 34 marks, about 30 minutes — the longest in the primary years, with
five prior years to place against. Three sections:

- **Grade 5 skills** (20 questions)
- **Grade 4 skills** (8 questions)
- **Foundations, Grades 1–3** (6 questions)

Attempts are unlimited and it is never a fail. Four results:

- **Ready for Grade 6** — 80% overall with at least 60% in every section.
- **Ready, with a little review** — 50% or more; start at Unit 1 after the review
  lessons the report names.
- **Grade 5 skills below 40%** — treated as critical, and the report routes to
  Grade 5 Units 3, 5 and 8 (*Reflections*, *Tell Me How*, *Share Your Views*)
  whatever the overall score.
- **Let's build strong roots first** — the report recommends **Grade 5 English**
  as the course to take.

The review lessons open directly in whichever earlier grade they belong to, even
where that grade is walked one unit at a time — the foundations section points
at Grade 3 Units 1, 3 and 6, and those open on the link.

The exam can be retaken at any point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- A writing folder that keeps drafts.
- **Somewhere to keep sources.** The capstone requires annotated source evidence
  — where each piece of information came from, marked up — so a learner who
  reads something useful in Term 1 should record where they found it then, not
  reconstruct it in Term 3.
- Materials for the Unit 10 product, which is multi-section and researched.
  Settle what it will be with the teacher early.
- A way to record audio. Every unit assignment includes a spoken piece, and the
  capstone presentation runs to four minutes.
- A device that plays audio, and an internet connection for live sessions and
  for research.

### Attendance

The course runs **60 live sessions** — 6 per unit, all 45 minutes.

Each session names its preparation: review the unit overview and the vocabulary
group before arriving.

Attendance is expected at all sessions; tell the teacher in advance where
possible. Sessions are recorded and a missed one should be watched before the
next. More than two missed in a unit and the teacher will contact the family to
agree how to catch up.

### Homework

Each unit carries **six activities** and **one assignment** — **10 assignments**
across the year.

The pattern is consistent all year: one written piece and one recording, both
**improved against the Grade 6 rubrics before submission**. Unit 1 asks for a
learner's own fable and a recording of a fable retold from memory.

Expect **45–60 minutes** on an assignment across the unit's two weeks, plus the
revision pass.

The Unit 10 capstone is five submissions, not four: the researched multi-section
product, the annotated source evidence, three portfolio pieces, the four-minute
presentation recording, and the reflection. The research and the sourcing cannot
be done in the last week.

### Assessment and grading

**Assignments — 352 marks across the year.** Nine unit assignments at 32 marks,
the Unit 10 capstone at 64.

A unit assignment is marked on the two Grade 6 rubrics, four criteria each at
four marks: **Writing** on Ideas, Organization, Language and Conventions;
**Speaking** on Clarity, Vocabulary, Sentence accuracy and Interaction. That is
16 and 16.

> **Two things the school must decide, because the course does not.**
>
> 1. **What earns full marks on a task.** Earlier grades print marking guidance
>    inside the assignment — Grade 3 in all ten units, Grades 4 and 5 in seven
>    each — naming exactly what a top answer does. **Grade 6 prints none.** The
>    rubric descriptors are all a teacher is given, so either the school adopts
>    them as the whole standard or the teacher sets task-level criteria. Say
>    which here: {{how full marks are judged, and who sets it}}.
> 2. **How the capstone's 64 marks divide.** Five submissions, two rubrics, and
>    no stated division: {{marks for the product / sources / portfolio /
>    presentation / reflection}}.
>
> Neither is an oversight to work around quietly. A learner is entitled to know
> what is being marked before doing the work, and in this grade only the school
> can tell them.

**Unit quizzes — 100 questions across the year**, ten per unit.

**Final course quiz.** After the capstone: 30 questions, 30 marks, about 30
minutes, in three sections — Words and Meaning, Reading and Language,
Communication and Application. Pass mark **80%**. Attempts are recorded as
"teacher or school policy": {{attempts allowed, and what happens on a first
fail}}.

**Self-assessment.** Six *I can…* statements per unit, rated **Not yet / With
help / By myself**. No marks.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Come prepared** — the unit overview and vocabulary group before the session.
- **Improve against the rubric before submitting.** Every assignment asks for it
  by name.
- **Show where information came from.** In a researched year, using a source
  without recording it is the habit the capstone is built to break.
- **Take turns and listen**; answering a follow-up properly is assessed.
- **A parent or another adult must be present whenever a learner uses a device
  or the Wehel AI tutor.** The tutor is for practising word meanings and
  language, not for producing the work.

### Support and communication

**If a learner is struggling**, the placement exam can be retaken at any point
and its report names the specific earlier units to revisit; those links open
those units directly. Grade 5 English remains available as a course for a
learner who needs the ground under this one.

**Extra help available:**

- The teacher, at the contact above.
- Wehel, the in-course AI tutor — with an adult present, and for practice rather
  than production.
- Recorded audio for the vocabulary and the readings.

**How the school will contact you:** when marked work is returned, after a
missed assignment, after two missed live sessions in a unit, and at the end of
each term. The capstone is conferenced at its milestones rather than collected
at the end.

**If you have a concern**, contact the teacher first. If it is unresolved after
{{n}} school days, contact {{school administrator role/name}}.

**For English as an additional language, or any additional need**, tell the
teacher at the start of the year.
