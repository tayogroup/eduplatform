# ================================================================ Tens and Ones
[tens-and-ones.html] Numbers to 100
name: Every number up to one hundred is made of tens and ones.
show: Look at 47. The 4 is in the tens place, so it means 4 tens, which is forty. The 7 means 7 ones. Forty and seven make 47.
warn: The 4 in 47 is not worth four. It is worth forty, because of where it sits.
hand: Tap 63 and say how many tens and how many ones it has.

[tens-and-ones.html] A quick look
name: Some amounts are small enough to see in one look, without counting.
show: Five dots in a dice pattern are five straight away. For a big group, make a guess first, like about twenty, then count to check how close you were.
warn: A guess does not have to be exactly right. A good guess is close, and counting tells you the real number.
hand: Look at the next group, say your guess out loud, then count.

[tens-and-ones.html] Numbers in words
name: Every number has a name you can say and write, as well as its digits.
show: 42 is forty-two. The tens come first, forty, and then the ones, two. 70 is seventy, and 17 is seventeen.
warn: Seventeen and seventy sound alike. Seventeen is ten and seven. Seventy is seven tens.
hand: Read the number, say its name, then tap the words that match.

[tens-and-ones.html] Build it with tens and ones
name: A ten is a stick of ten ones. Tens and ones together build any number up to one hundred.
show: Three tens and five ones make 35. Add one more ten and it becomes 45. Only the tens digit changes.
warn: Ten loose ones are the same as one ten. When you have ten ones, they make a stick.
hand: Build 52. Put in the tens first, then the ones.

[tens-and-ones.html] Which is more?
name: To see which number is more, look at the tens first.
show: 47 and 52. 4 tens against 5 tens, so 52 is more. For 63 and 68 the tens are the same, so the ones decide, and 68 is more.
warn: 49 is not more than 52 just because 9 is a big digit. The tens always come first.
hand: Look at the tens of both numbers before you look at anything else.

[tens-and-ones.html] First, second, third
name: Counting numbers tell you how many. First, second and third tell you which place.
show: In a race, the child who finishes before everyone else is first. The next one is second, then third, then fourth.
warn: Fourth is not four children. It is one child, in the fourth place in the line.
hand: Count along the line from the front, saying first, second, third, until you reach the one you want.

[tens-and-ones.html] Round to the nearest 10
name: Rounding means finding the ten that a number is nearest to.
show: 37 sits between 30 and 40. It is 7 away from 30 but only 3 away from 40, so 37 rounds to 40. 32 is nearer to 30.
warn: A number ending in 5, like 35, is exactly halfway. We round it up, so 35 rounds to 40.
hand: Find the two tens on either side, then tap the one that is closer.

[tens-and-ones.html] Odd or even?
name: A number is even if its counters all go into pairs. If one is left over, it is odd.
show: 8 counters make 4 pairs with none left, so 8 is even. 9 counters make 4 pairs and 1 left on its own, so 9 is odd.
warn: Only the ones digit decides. 38 ends in 8, so 38 is even, even though 3 on its own is odd.
hand: Put the counters in pairs and look for one on its own.

[tens-and-ones.html] Make 20, make 100
name: Some pairs of numbers fit together to make exactly 20, or exactly 100.
show: 6 and 14 make 20, because 6 and 4 make 10 and then there is one more ten. 30 and 70 make 100, because 3 tens and 7 tens make 10 tens.
warn: To make 100, count in tens, not ones. 40 needs 60 to make 100, not 6.
hand: Look at the bar to see how much is still missing, then tap the number that fills it.

[tens-and-ones.html] Adding two-digit numbers
name: To add two numbers, add the tens, add the ones, then put the two answers together.
show: 34 add 25. The tens: 30 and 20 make 50. The ones: 4 and 5 make 9. Put them together and you get 59.
warn: Keep tens with tens and ones with ones. Adding the 3 of 34 to the 5 of 25 mixes up the places.
hand: Press Show the next step for each part, and say the answer before it appears.

[tens-and-ones.html] Taking away by jumping back
name: To take away, you can jump back along the number line, tens first and then ones.
show: 65 take away 23. Jump back two tens, to 55 and then 45. Then jump back three ones, to 44, 43 and 42. So the answer is 42.
warn: Count the jumps, not the numbers you land on. Counting 65 as a jump makes the answer one out.
hand: Press back 10 once for each ten, then back 1 once for each one.

[tens-and-ones.html] One undoes the other
name: Adding and taking away undo each other.
show: 27 add 15 is 42. So 42 take away 15 must bring you back to 27. That is how you check an answer.
warn: The check must use the same numbers. To check 27 add 15, take 15 away from the answer.
hand: Take the answer, undo the step, and see if you land back at the start.

[tens-and-ones.html] Arrays: 2s, 5s and 10s
name: An array is rows of dots, with the same number of dots in every row.
show: 3 rows of 5 dots. Count down the rows in fives: 5, 10, 15. So 3 fives make 15.
warn: Every row must have the same number of dots. If one row is short, it is not an array.
hand: Add a row and count again in steps. What does the total go up by?

[tens-and-ones.html] Know them by heart
name: Some times facts are worth knowing by heart, so the answer comes straight away.
show: 5 tens are 50. 2 sevens are 14. 10 threes are 3 tens, which is 30. The 2, 5 and 10 times tables are the ones to learn first.
warn: If you are stuck, do not guess. Press Count it up, count in steps, and try to remember it for next time.
hand: Say the answer in your head first, then tap it.

[tens-and-ones.html] Sharing and grouping
name: Sharing gives one each, round and round. Grouping makes groups of the same size.
show: Share 12 sweets between 3 friends. One each, round and round, and each friend gets 4. Make groups of 4 from 12 sweets, and you get 3 groups.
warn: Sharing and grouping use the same numbers but ask different questions. Sharing asks how many each. Grouping asks how many groups.
hand: Press Share between 3 and watch the sweets go round.

[tens-and-ones.html] Show what you know
name: This is the check. It asks about everything in this lesson, from tens and ones to sharing.
show: Read each question and tap the answer. Get most of them right to finish the lesson, and the screen tells you how many you need.
warn: If you do not get enough, that is fine. Press Try again and the questions come round again.
hand: Take your time, and read each question twice before you tap.

[tens-and-ones.html] My stickers
name: Nothing to work out here. This is your shelf, with a sticker for every step you finished.

# ================================================================ Coins and Change
[coins-and-change.html] Know your money
name: Money comes as coins and notes, and each one is worth a different number of shillings.
show: A 10 shilling coin is worth ten single shillings. A 50 shilling note is worth fifty. The number printed on it tells you what it is worth.
warn: A big coin is not always worth more than a small one. Read the number on it, not its size.
hand: Tap each coin and note and listen to what it is worth.

[coins-and-change.html] Count what is in the purse
name: To count money, start with the biggest and count on.
show: A 50 note, a 20 coin and a 5 coin. Start at 50, count on 20 to 70, then 5 more to 75. That is 75 shillings.
warn: Counting the pieces is not counting the money. Three coins can be worth much more than three shillings.
hand: Tap the biggest one first, then keep counting on.

[coins-and-change.html] Make this exact amount
name: Now you choose the money to make an amount exactly.
show: To make 35 shillings, take a 20, then a 10, then a 5. Say 20, 30, 35 as you go. Exactly right.
warn: If you go past the amount, press Undo and take the last piece back out.
hand: Keep a running total in your head as you tap each piece.

[coins-and-change.html] The fewest pieces
name: The same amount can be made with lots of pieces or just a few. Take the biggest that fits, every time.
show: 70 shillings. A 50 fits, and leaves 20. A 20 fits, and leaves nothing. That is two pieces. Seven 10 shilling coins also make 70, but that is seven pieces.
warn: Starting with small coins still reaches the amount, but it takes far more pieces.
hand: Ask yourself which is the biggest piece that still fits, and tap that one.

[coins-and-change.html] What does it all cost?
name: The total is all the prices added together.
show: A pencil for 20 shillings and a banana for 11. 20 add 11 is 31, so the total is 31 shillings.
warn: Add every price in the basket. It is easy to add two prices and forget the third.
hand: Add the first two prices, then add the next one to that answer.

[coins-and-change.html] How much more?
name: When you do not have enough money, count on from what you have up to the price.
show: You have 35 shillings and the price is 50. Hop 5 to reach 40, then hop 10 to reach 50. 5 and 10 make 15, so you need 15 shillings more.
warn: Stop exactly on the price. A hop that jumps past it counts too much.
hand: Use the biggest hop that does not jump past the price.

[coins-and-change.html] Writing money
name: Money has its own way of being written, with the number and the letters sh or KSh.
show: 45 sh and KSh 45 both mean forty-five shillings. The letters say it is money. The number says how much.
warn: KSh goes in front of the number and sh goes after it, but the amount is exactly the same.
hand: Read the number first, then find the one that means the same amount.

[coins-and-change.html] Which purse is worth more?
name: To compare two purses, find the total in each one, then compare the totals.
show: One purse has two 20 shilling coins, which is 40. The other has two 10s and a 5, which is 25. 40 is more than 25.
warn: The purse with more coins is not always worth more. Count the money, not the coins.
hand: Count one purse, remember its total, then count the other one.

[coins-and-change.html] Saving and spending
name: Saving adds money to what you have. Spending takes money away.
show: Musa has 30 shillings and saves 20 more. 30 add 20 is 50. Then he spends 15. 50 take away 15 is 35.
warn: Read carefully whether the money comes in or goes out before you decide to add or take away.
hand: Ask yourself, is this saving or spending? Then add or take away.

[coins-and-change.html] Show what you know
name: This is the check for coins and change.
show: You will count money, add prices, and work out how much more. Get most of them right to finish the lesson, and the screen tells you how many you need.
warn: If you do not get enough, that is fine. Press Try again and the questions come round again.
hand: Whenever you count money, start with the biggest piece.

[coins-and-change.html] My stickers
name: Nothing to work out here. This is your shelf, with a sticker for every step you finished.

# ================================================================ Fair Shares
[fair-shares.html] Equal parts first
name: A fraction only works when the parts are all the same size.
show: A cake cut into two parts the same size gives two halves. If one part is bigger, neither part is a half.
warn: Two parts is not enough. They have to be two equal parts.
hand: Look at the parts. Are they all exactly the same size?

[fair-shares.html] Top number, bottom number
name: The bottom number says how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The top number says how many parts you take.
show: Three quarters. The whole is cut into 4 equal parts, so the bottom number is 4. You take 3 of them, so the top number is 3. Shade 3 of the 4 parts.
warn: The bottom number is not how many are left over. It is how many equal parts there are altogether.
hand: Count the parts in the whole first, then shade the number on top.

[fair-shares.html] A fraction of a group
name: You can take a fraction of a group of things, not only of a shape.
show: Half of 8 counters. Share the 8 into 2 equal groups of 4. One of those groups is half, so half of 8 is 4.
warn: Count what is in one group, not how many groups there are. A quarter of 12 is 3, because each of the 4 groups holds 3.
hand: Share the counters into equal groups first, then tap the groups you need.

[fair-shares.html] Same size, different name
name: Different fractions can be exactly the same size.
show: On the wall, one half is as long as two quarters put side by side. So one half and two quarters are the same amount with different names.
warn: Two quarters has bigger numbers than one half, but it is not more. It is the same size.
hand: Tap a brick and watch which other bricks light up to match it.

[fair-shares.html] Which is bigger?
name: To compare fractions, look at the size of the pieces and how many there are.
show: One half and one quarter. A half cuts the whole into 2 pieces and a quarter into 4, so a half is the bigger piece. Three quarters and one quarter have the same size pieces, so 3 pieces are more than 1.
warn: 4 is bigger than 2, but a quarter is smaller than a half. More pieces means each piece is smaller.
hand: Look at the bars and compare the shaded parts, not just the numbers.

[fair-shares.html] On the number line
name: Fractions sit on the number line between zero and one.
show: One half is exactly in the middle, between 0 and 1. One quarter is halfway from 0 to one half. Three quarters is halfway from one half to 1.
warn: A fraction is less than one whole, so it goes between 0 and 1, not out at 2 or 4.
hand: Find the middle first, then decide if your fraction comes before it or after it.

[fair-shares.html] Putting parts together
name: When the bottom numbers are the same, you add fractions by counting the parts.
show: One quarter and two quarters. Count the parts: 1 and 2 make 3. Together they make three quarters.
warn: Do not add the bottom numbers. The pieces are still quarters, so the bottom number stays 4.
hand: Count the shaded parts, and keep the bottom number just as it is.

[fair-shares.html] More than one whole
name: When you have more parts than make a whole, you have more than one whole.
show: Four quarters make one whole pizza. Add one more quarter and you have one whole pizza and one quarter.
warn: Five quarters is not a mistake. It just means one full pizza and a bit more.
hand: Add slices until a pizza is full, then keep going and count the whole pizzas.

[fair-shares.html] Fractions in real life
name: Fractions help us share things fairly in real life.
show: Amina has 8 sweets and gives half to her brother. Half of 8 is 4, so her brother gets 4 sweets and Amina keeps 4.
warn: Read the question to find the whole. Half of 8 sweets and half of one cake are different amounts.
hand: Find the whole, split it into equal parts, then take the parts the question asks for.

[fair-shares.html] Show what you know
name: This is the check for fractions.
show: You will find halves and quarters of shapes and groups, and compare them. Get most of them right to finish the lesson, and the screen tells you how many you need.
warn: If you do not get enough, that is fine. Press Try again and the questions come round again.
hand: For every question, ask yourself how many equal parts the whole is cut into.

[fair-shares.html] My stickers
name: Nothing to work out here. This is your shelf, with a sticker for every step you finished.

# ================================================================ Patterns That Grow
[patterns-that-grow.html] The part that repeats
name: A repeating pattern has a part that repeats, again and again.
show: Circle, square, circle, square. The part that repeats is circle, square. The orange box shows it.
warn: The part that repeats must show up whole each time. Circle, circle, square is a part of three shapes, not two.
hand: Tap a shape and see where the orange box says the repeat begins.

[patterns-that-grow.html] What comes next?
name: Once you find the part that repeats, you can tell what comes next.
show: Red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue, red. The part that repeats is red, blue, blue. After red comes blue.
warn: Do not just copy the last shape. Look at the whole part that repeats.
hand: Say the pattern out loud, and let your voice tell you what comes next.

[patterns-that-grow.html] Mend the pattern
name: When a shape is missing, the part that repeats tells you what goes in the gap.
show: Star, moon, star, moon, gap, moon. Every other shape is a star, so the gap needs a star.
warn: Check the shapes on both sides of the gap, not only the one just before it.
hand: Say the pattern from the start, and say what fits the gap.

[patterns-that-grow.html] Make your own
name: Now you make your own repeating pattern.
show: Choose a part, like triangle, triangle, circle. Put it down twice: triangle, triangle, circle, triangle, triangle, circle. That is a repeating pattern.
warn: If the part changes each time, it is not a repeating pattern. Keep the part exactly the same.
hand: Build at least eight shapes, so your part shows up twice or more.

[patterns-that-grow.html] Counting on in steps
name: Counting on in steps means adding the same amount each time.
show: Start at 3 and count on in twos: 3, 5, 7, 9, 11. Start at 20 and count on in fives: 20, 25, 30, 35.
warn: Keep the same step all the way. Do not switch from twos to ones halfway through.
hand: Say the start number, then add the step in your head each time.

[patterns-that-grow.html] How does it go up?
name: A number pattern goes up by the same amount each time. That amount is the step.
show: 15, 20, 25, 30. From 15 to 20 is 5, and from 20 to 25 is 5 again. It goes up in fives.
warn: Check more than one jump. One jump on its own can trick you.
hand: Work out two jumps and see if they match.

[patterns-that-grow.html] The missing number
name: When a number is missing, work out the step, then fill the gap.
show: 10, 20, gap, 40. It goes up in tens, so the gap is 30.
warn: The gap is not always at the end. Count on from the number just before it.
hand: Find the step from two numbers that sit next to each other, then use it.

[patterns-that-grow.html] Counting back in steps
name: Counting back in steps means taking away the same amount each time.
show: Start at 50 and count back in tens: 50, 40, 30, 20. Start at 19 and count back in twos: 19, 17, 15, 13.
warn: Counting back makes the numbers smaller every time. If a number gets bigger, you went the wrong way.
hand: Say the start number, then take away the step each time.

[patterns-that-grow.html] Hundred square patterns
name: The hundred square hides number patterns.
show: The coloured numbers 5, 15, 25 and 35 go straight down one column. Each one is ten more, so the next is 45.
warn: Going down one row adds ten. Going along one square adds one. Do not mix them up.
hand: Work out how much the coloured numbers go up by, then tap the next one.

[patterns-that-grow.html] Show what you know
name: This is the check for patterns.
show: You will find what comes next in shape patterns and number patterns. Get most of them right to finish the lesson, and the screen tells you how many you need.
warn: If you do not get enough, that is fine. Press Try again and the questions come round again.
hand: For every pattern, find the part that repeats or the step first.

[patterns-that-grow.html] My stickers
name: Nothing to work out here. This is your shelf, with a sticker for every step you finished.

# ================================================================ Sides and Corners
[sides-and-corners.html] Sides and corners
name: Flat shapes have straight sides, and corners where two sides meet.
show: A triangle has 3 sides and 3 corners. A square has 4 sides and 4 corners. Tap each side as you count it.
warn: Count each side only once. Go round the shape in order so you do not miss one or count one twice.
hand: Pick a shape, tap every side, then tap every corner.

[sides-and-corners.html] Name the shape
name: Counting the sides tells you the name of a flat shape.
show: 3 sides make a triangle. 4 sides make a shape like a square or a rectangle. 5 sides make a pentagon, and 6 sides make a hexagon.
warn: A shape turned on its side is still the same shape. A tipped square is still a square.
hand: Count the sides first, then tap the name.

[sides-and-corners.html] Shape riddles
name: A shape riddle gives clues, and each clue rules some shapes out.
show: I have 4 sides. All my sides are the same length. The first clue leaves squares and rectangles. The second clue leaves only the square.
warn: Use every clue, not just the first one. The first clue often fits two shapes.
hand: Read one clue at a time, and think which shapes it rules out.

[sides-and-corners.html] Make it match
name: A line of symmetry is a fold line. Both halves match exactly.
show: A square coloured just left of the line needs a partner just right of it. A square two steps from the line needs its partner two steps away on the other side.
warn: The partner goes the same distance from the line on the other side, not in the same spot as the first square.
hand: Pick one coloured square on the left and colour its partner on the right.

[sides-and-corners.html] Does it fold in half?
name: A line of symmetry folds a shape into two halves that match exactly.
show: A heart folded down its middle makes two halves that match. A heart folded across the middle does not.
warn: Two halves the same size are not enough. They must match when one is folded over onto the other.
hand: Imagine folding the shape on the dotted line. Would every part land on its partner?

[sides-and-corners.html] Solid shapes
name: Solid shapes have faces, edges and corners. A face is a flat part you could paint.
show: A cube has 6 flat faces, and every face is a square. The line where two faces meet is an edge. A dice is a cube.
warn: A sphere has no flat face at all. It is curved all over.
hand: Pick a shape and count its flat faces.

[sides-and-corners.html] Where is it?
name: Position words say exactly where something is.
show: The cat is above the box. The ball is between the two chairs. The book is to the left of the lamp.
warn: Left and right are easy to swap. Your left hand makes the letter L with its thumb and first finger.
hand: Read the position word, find the thing it names, then tap the right square.

[sides-and-corners.html] Turning
name: A turn can be a quarter turn, a half turn, a three quarter turn or a whole turn. Clockwise is the way the hands of a clock go.
show: The arrow points up. A quarter turn clockwise makes it point right. Another quarter turn makes it point down. Two quarter turns make a half turn.
warn: A whole turn brings the arrow back to where it started, so it can look as if nothing happened.
hand: Turn the arrow one quarter at a time and say where it points.

[sides-and-corners.html] The middle of a circle
name: Every circle has a centre, and every point on the edge is the same distance from it.
show: Tap anywhere on the edge and a line joins it to the centre. Tap another point, and the new line is exactly the same length.
warn: In a shape that is not round, the edge is near the middle in some places and far in others, so it is not a circle.
hand: Tap three points on the edge and compare the lines.

[sides-and-corners.html] Shapes in real things
name: Real objects have shapes hiding in them.
show: A door is a rectangle. A tin of beans is a cylinder. A football is a sphere. A dice is a cube.
warn: Ask first whether the thing is flat or solid. A clock face is a flat circle, but a tin is a solid cylinder.
hand: Look at the object, decide flat or solid, then pick its shape.

[sides-and-corners.html] Spin it all the way round
name: Some shapes look exactly the same several times as you turn them all the way round.
show: A square looks the same after a quarter turn, a half turn, three quarters of a turn and the whole turn. That is 4 times, one for each equal corner.
warn: Count the whole turn at the end, when it is back where it started. That counts as one of the times.
hand: Turn it a bit at a time and count each time it looks exactly the same.

[sides-and-corners.html] Show what you know
name: This is the check for shapes.
show: You will count sides and corners, name shapes, and find lines of symmetry and turns. Get most of them right to finish the lesson, and the screen tells you how many you need.
warn: If you do not get enough, that is fine. Press Try again and the questions come round again.
hand: When a shape is on the screen, count its sides before you answer.

[sides-and-corners.html] My stickers
name: Nothing to work out here. This is your shelf, with a sticker for every step you finished.

# ================================================================ Which Way From Here
[which-way-from-here.html] Saying where
name: You can say exactly where something is with position words.
show: The bird is above the tree. The dog is below the table. The ball is next to the box. The cat is between the two chairs.
warn: Between needs two things, one on each side of it.
hand: Find the thing, then say the word that tells where it is.

[which-way-from-here.html] Whose left is it?
name: Left and right depend on which way you are facing.
show: When the robot faces up the page, its left is your left. When it faces down the page, its left is on your right.
warn: Do not use the left of the page. Turn with the robot and use its left.
hand: Turn your own body to face the same way as the robot, then decide.

[which-way-from-here.html] Drive it to the flag
name: A route is a list of moves: turn left, turn right and go forward.
show: The flag is two squares up and one square to the right. Face up, go forward two, turn right, and go forward one.
warn: Forward always means the way the robot is facing. Turn first, then go.
hand: Plan your first two moves before you press anything.

[which-way-from-here.html] Say the route
name: Now you read a route somebody else walked, and say it in words.
show: The trail goes forward three squares, turns right, and goes forward two more to the flag.
warn: Say each turn as the walker would turn, not as the page looks. A right turn is the walker turning to their right.
hand: Put your finger on the star and follow the trail one square at a time.

[which-way-from-here.html] Over the mirror line
name: A mirror line flips a shape over to the other side.
show: A dot 2 squares from the line lands 2 squares away on the other side. A dot that touches the line stays touching it.
warn: The reflection is not just slid across. It is the same distance from the line, on the other side.
hand: Count the squares from the dot to the line, then count the same number on the other side.

[which-way-from-here.html] Show what you know
name: This is the check for position and direction.
show: You will use position words, left and right, routes and mirror lines. Get most of them right to finish the lesson, and the screen tells you how many you need.
warn: If you do not get enough, that is fine. Press Try again and the questions come round again.
hand: For left and right, always think about which way the person or robot is facing.

[which-way-from-here.html] My stickers
name: Nothing to work out here. This is your shelf, with a sticker for every step you finished.

# ================================================================ How Much, How Long
[how-much-how-long.html] Longer and shorter
name: To compare lengths, start them all in the same place and look at where each one ends.
show: The pencil and the crayon start at the same line. The pencil ends further along, so the pencil is longer.
warn: A thing is not longer just because it is fatter. Only the length along it matters.
hand: Look along to the end of each one, then tap the one you are asked for.

[how-much-how-long.html] Measure with cubes
name: You can measure a length by counting cubes laid along it.
show: The pencil is as long as 7 cubes in a line, so the pencil is 7 cubes long.
warn: Every cube must be the same size, with no gaps between them. Cubes of different sizes give a number that means nothing.
hand: Add cubes until the row is as long as the object, then count them.

[how-much-how-long.html] Reading a ruler
name: A ruler measures in centimetres, starting from zero.
show: The object starts at 0 and ends at the 8. So it is 8 centimetres long.
warn: Start at zero, not at the very end of the ruler, and not at 1.
hand: Find where the object ends and read the number there.

[how-much-how-long.html] Which unit?
name: Pick the unit that fits the size of the thing you are measuring.
show: A pencil is a few centimetres long, so centimetres fit. A classroom is a few metres long, so metres fit.
warn: Measuring a room in centimetres gives a huge number, and measuring a pencil in metres gives almost nothing.
hand: Picture the thing, then choose centimetres for small things or metres for big ones.

[how-much-how-long.html] Balance the scales
name: A balance tips down on the heavier side. When it is level, both sides weigh the same.
show: The apple side is down, so the apple is heavier. Add a 100 gram weight, then a 200 gram weight. When the beam is level, the apple weighs 300 grams.
warn: If the weights side goes down, you have added too much. Take the weights off and try a smaller one.
hand: Add one weight at a time and watch which way the beam tips.

[how-much-how-long.html] Reading a kitchen scale
name: A kitchen scale has a needle that points to the mass in grams.
show: Each mark is 100 grams. If the needle points at the fourth mark after zero, the mass is 400 grams.
warn: Count the marks from zero, and count each mark as 100, not as 1.
hand: Put your finger on zero and count the marks round to the needle in hundreds.

[how-much-how-long.html] Reading a jug
name: A measuring jug shows how much liquid is inside, in millilitres.
show: Each mark is 100 millilitres. Pour in three times and the level reaches the third mark, which is 300 millilitres.
warn: Read the level where the top of the water is flat, straight across from the mark.
hand: Pour some in, then count the marks up from the bottom.

[how-much-how-long.html] Which holds more?
name: To find which container holds more, fill each one with cups of water and count the cups.
show: The jug takes 6 cups and the bottle takes 4. The jug took more cups, so the jug holds more.
warn: A tall thin container can hold less than a short wide one. Count the cups, do not judge by how tall it is.
hand: Press Fill them with cups and count each cup as it goes in.

[how-much-how-long.html] Estimate, then measure
name: An estimate is a sensible guess. Then you measure to find out.
show: The ribbon looks about as long as your hand, so a guess of about 15 centimetres is sensible. Then the ruler shows the real length.
warn: A good estimate does not have to be exact. It just needs to be close.
hand: Look at the ribbon, make your guess, then see how close you were.

[how-much-how-long.html] Draw a line yourself
name: Now you draw a line of an exact length.
show: To draw a line 6 centimetres long, start at 0 and stop at the 6. The line in between is 6 centimetres long.
warn: Do not start at 1. Every line starts at 0.
hand: Tap the ruler at the number where your line should end.

[how-much-how-long.html] Between the marks
name: A measuring scale is a number line, and some of its marks have no number written on them.
show: The marks say 30 and 40, with one small mark halfway between. The ribbon ends at that small mark, so it is 35 centimetres long.
warn: Work out what the small mark is worth before you read the arrow. Here the halfway mark is worth 5 more.
hand: Find the numbered mark just before the arrow, then decide what the small mark adds.

[how-much-how-long.html] Show what you know
name: This is the check for length, mass and capacity.
show: You will read rulers, scales and jugs, and choose units. Get most of them right to finish the lesson, and the screen tells you how many you need.
warn: If you do not get enough, that is fine. Press Try again and the questions come round again.
hand: On every scale, find zero and work out what each mark is worth.

# ================================================================ Half Past, Quarter To
[half-past-quarter-to.html] The two hands
name: A clock has two hands. The short hand shows the hour. The long hand shows the minutes.
show: The short hand points at 3 and the long hand points at 12. That is three o clock.
warn: The long hand is not the hour. Always read the hour from the short hand.
hand: Move the hour hand, then the minute hand, and say the time out loud.

[half-past-quarter-to.html] Quarter past, quarter to
name: The clock face splits into quarters.
show: When the long hand points at the 3, it is quarter past. At the 6 it is half past. At the 9 it is quarter to the next hour.
warn: Quarter to 4 comes before four o clock, not after it. The short hand is nearly at the 4.
hand: Look where the long hand points, then say past or to.

[half-past-quarter-to.html] Counting round in fives
name: The long hand shows the minutes, and you count round the clock in fives.
show: The long hand points at the 4. Count 5, 10, 15, 20. It is twenty minutes past.
warn: The number the long hand points to is not the minutes. At the 4 it is 20 minutes, not 4.
hand: Point at each number from the 12 and count in fives until you reach the long hand.

[half-past-quarter-to.html] Clock face and digital
name: A digital clock writes the hour first, then the minutes.
show: Half past 2 is written 2:30. Quarter past 7 is written 7:15.
warn: Quarter to 5 is written 4:45. The hour shown is still 4, because it is not yet five o clock.
hand: Read the hour from the short hand, then the minutes from the long hand, and find the match.

[half-past-quarter-to.html] Seconds to years
name: We measure time in seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, months and years.
show: There are 60 seconds in a minute and 60 minutes in an hour. There are 24 hours in a day, 7 days in a week and 12 months in a year.
warn: Not every month has the same number of days, but every week has 7 days.
hand: Say the units in order, from the shortest to the longest.

[half-past-quarter-to.html] Days, months, calendar
name: A week has 7 days, always in the same order, and a year has 12 months.
show: After Monday comes Tuesday, then Wednesday. A calendar shows a month in rows of 7, one row for each week.
warn: A month is not the same as 4 weeks. Most months have a few more days than that.
hand: Find a date on the calendar and read the day before it and the day after it.

[half-past-quarter-to.html] Show what you know
name: This is the check for time.
show: You will read clocks, match them to digital times, and use days and months. Get most of them right to finish the lesson, and the screen tells you how many you need.
warn: If you do not get enough, that is fine. Press Try again and the questions come round again.
hand: On every clock, read the short hand first and then the long hand.

# ================================================================ Count It, Chart It
[count-it-chart-it.html] Sort them into groups
name: Sorting puts things that are alike into the same group.
show: Every fruit goes in one hoop and every toy goes in the other. Each thing belongs in exactly one hoop.
warn: Sort by the rule on the hoop, not by colour or size, unless the rule says so.
hand: Tap a thing, read the hoop labels, then tap the hoop it belongs in.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Tally charts
name: A tally chart counts things as you go, with one mark for each.
show: Four marks stand in a row, and the fifth goes across them. That bundle is 5, so two bundles and 2 more marks is 12.
warn: The fifth mark goes across. A fifth mark standing up makes the count hard to read.
hand: Tap each thing once, then count the bundles in fives.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Pictograms
name: A pictogram shows data with pictures.
show: Here each picture stands for one thing. If the apple row has 6 pictures, then 6 children chose apples.
warn: Line the pictures up neatly, one under another, so the rows are fair to compare.
hand: Count the pictures in the row the question asks about.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Sorting two ways
name: A Carroll diagram sorts things two ways at once.
show: Across the top it says red or not red. Down the side it says round or not round. A red ball goes in the box that is red and round.
warn: Check both labels. A thing can fit one label and not the other.
hand: Read the top label, then the side label, and find the box where both are true.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Build a block graph
name: A block graph stacks one block for each thing counted.
show: The numbers say 4 children like football. Add 4 blocks to the football column.
warn: Every block must be the same size, and all the columns start on the same line.
hand: Add blocks one at a time until each column matches its number.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Reading a block graph
name: To read a block graph, count the blocks in a column.
show: The tallest column has 7 blocks, so 7 is the most. To find how many more, take the shorter column away from the taller one.
warn: Count the blocks. Do not guess from how tall a column looks.
hand: Put your finger on the column and count the blocks up from the bottom.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Toss a coin
name: A coin can land two ways, heads or tails.
show: Toss it ten times. You might get 6 heads and 4 tails, or 5 and 5. The tally keeps count for you.
warn: You cannot know how the next toss will land. Heads last time does not make tails come next.
hand: Toss the coin ten times, then read the tally.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Spin and record
name: This spinner is half orange and half teal, so either colour can come up.
show: After ten spins you might have 4 orange and 6 teal. Each spin adds one block to its colour.
warn: Half and half does not mean it always comes out 5 and 5. It can be a little different every time.
hand: Spin it ten times, then compare the two columns.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Ask, collect, answer
name: An investigation starts with a question. Then you collect answers to find out.
show: Ask what the class likes best. Tap a row for each answer you hear. At the end, the row with the most marks answers the question.
warn: Record every answer as you hear it. Trying to remember them all at the end goes wrong.
hand: Listen to each answer and tap its row straight away.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Regular or random?
name: A regular pattern repeats over and over. A random one has no repeat.
show: Heads, tails, heads, tails, heads, tails keeps repeating, so it is regular and you can say what comes next. A real coin tossed ten times gives a mix with no repeat.
warn: One or two repeats by chance do not make a pattern regular. It has to keep repeating.
hand: Look for a part that repeats all the way along before you decide.

[count-it-chart-it.html] Show what you know
name: This is the check for sorting, charts and chance.
show: You will read tally charts, pictograms and block graphs, and sort things two ways. Get most of them right to finish the lesson, and the screen tells you how many you need.
warn: If you do not get enough, that is fine. Press Try again and the questions come round again.
hand: Before you answer, read the labels on the chart.

name: Nothing to work out here. This is your shelf, with a sticker for every step you finished.
