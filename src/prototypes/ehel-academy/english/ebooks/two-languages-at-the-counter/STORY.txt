TWO LANGUAGES AT THE COUNTER
Grade 4 - Level 4
Amal (Grade 4) series, book 5 (Term 1, Unit 1: Daily Life & Communication)

1. Two Languages at the Counter. Written by Ehel Academy. Illustrated by Ehel Academy Learning Studio.
2. The market was busy, and a queue had grown at the post counter.
3. A man near the front held a form he could not fill in. He turned it over twice and said nothing at all.
4. Omar looked up. "Good morning," he said — and then he said it again, in the man's own language.
5. The man's shoulders dropped. He began to talk quickly, and Omar wrote the address down for him, letter by letter.
6. Amal was waiting behind him with a parcel for her cousin. She had never heard Omar speak that language before.
7. "How many languages do you know?" she asked, when it was her turn. "Two," said Omar. "I had to master the second one."
8. "Why did you have to?" "Because people come to this counter who cannot read the form. It is necessary, so that nobody leaves without help."
9. "Was it hard?" "It took a year of daily effort," he said. "I learned ten words a week, and I kept going."
10. By midday he had served a woman in one language and her neighbour in the other, without ever losing his patient smile.
11. Amal wrote one line in her notebook: a language is a door, and Omar keeps two of them open.
12. That evening she began a list of her own. Ten new words a week, she wrote at the top. It was a fair place to start.

Unit vocabulary reinforced: language, master, necessary, citizen, effort, daily, continue, leave, clearly.
Themes: learning something hard so that somebody else does not have to struggle
Cameos: Theo is the customer; Omar's second language is his own answer in the Unit 1 interview.
