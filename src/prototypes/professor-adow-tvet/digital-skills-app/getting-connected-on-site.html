Getting Connected on Site

0

What this lesson is about

Tell a local network from the internet, name the parts of a small site network, choose wired or wireless with a reason, diagnose a dead connection in the right order, and know what should never be sent over a connection you do not own.

By the end of this lesson you can:

  1. Distinguish a local network from the internet.
  2. Name the parts of a small site network and what each does.
  3. Choose wired or wireless for a job and justify it.
  4. Join a named network and notice when you have joined the wrong one.
  5. Diagnose a connection failure in order.
  6. Say what must not be sent over a shared connection.
  7. Say how a job's records reach the office, and what happens when they do not.
Where this leadsThis module serves all six departments. Every trade in this school now works next to an embedded computer, and none of them calls it one. Where a criterion would map onto a Kenyan TVET unit it carries NO mapping rather than a guessed one: the Carpentry & Joinery Level 4 framework is the only one in hand, and digital skills are not in it. A claimed code that does not exist is worse than an honest blank.

A small site network

Assessed here

Tap each part. Two of these you own and can fix; two you do not.

What goes wrong hereCalling the whole thing 'the internet'. The router and the devices around it are YOUR network — they can talk to each other with the line out completely dead. Once you can see those as two separate things, 'the wi-fi is fine but nothing loads' stops being a contradiction and becomes a description of exactly where the fault is.

Wired or wireless

Assessed here

What goes wrong hereTreating wireless as the modern option and wired as the old one. They solve different problems. Wireless buys movement and costs reliability; a cable buys reliability and costs flexibility. A workshop that has thought about it usually has both, with the things that never move on cables.

What not to send over a connection you do not own

Assessed here

What goes wrong hereReading 'the connection is encrypted' as 'this is private'. Encryption protects what you send from being read on the way. It says nothing about who owns the equipment, what it records about you, or where it sends it afterwards.

How the records get to the office

Assessed here

What goes wrong hereTreating 'I entered it' as 'it is recorded'. Entering data puts it on the device in your hand. It becomes a record when it reaches the system the business actually reads, and on a site with a patchy connection those two moments can be hours apart.

End of the lesson
