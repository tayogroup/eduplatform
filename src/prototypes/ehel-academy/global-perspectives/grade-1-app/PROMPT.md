# The prompt this build was made from (improved)

The request arrived as one sentence, with the framework PDF attached:

> Build a new Grade 1 Global Perspectives model, modeling the design/style/UI
> after english/grade-1-v2, with interactive content, interactions with kids,
> visual examples, and experimentation, and demonstration, strength, and
> quality after the new math and science. Build the content using the
> Curriculum Framework Cambridge Primary Global Perspectives code 0838. Create
> an improved prompt.

Below is the version that states what that sentence assumes, what it leaves
open, and the checks that decide whether it was done. It is written so it can
be reused for the next subject or stage by changing the named things, and it
is the Computing build's prompt (`computing/grade-1-app/PROMPT.md`) with the
Global-Perspectives-shaped parts stated.

---

## Build a Grade 1 Global Perspectives standalone lesson course for Ehel Academy

### What to build

A **standalone lesson build** at
`src/prototypes/ehel-academy/global-perspectives/grade-1-app/`, the fifth of
its kind after `mathematics/grade-1-app/g1v2` (live at
`app/mathematics/grade-1-v2`), `english/grade-1-app` (live at
`app/english/grade-1-v2`), `science/grade-1-app` (live at
`app/science/grade-1-v2`) and `computing/grade-1-app`. One self-contained HTML
page per lesson plus a hub, each page carrying its own CSS, its own activity JS
and its own copy of the voice engine, bypassing `shell/course-app.js`.
Generated by a Python kit from authored content, then wired by the shared
pipeline in `mathematics/lesson-app-tools/` and gated by its `check-lessons.py`.

**Make it its own kit, `global-perspectives/lesson-kit/`, on the Computing
kit's shape.** Do not generalise `science/lesson-kit/` or
`computing/lesson-kit/` to another subject: each is live or about to be for
its own grades, and their safety proof was a byte-identical rebuild of their
own pages. Copy the three design files (`lesson.css`, `voice.js`, `deck.js`)
verbatim and give Global Perspectives its own `gp.js`, `gp.css`, rules,
builders and gate, so a change to any kit cannot reach another's live pages.

**Design, style and UI: the English build's, which is the Mathematics build's.**
The dark Wehel palette, the deck, the dot rail, the gold action colour, the
voice bar, the two header bars from the shared tool. Add subject-specific
styles in a separate file and never a new hue: gold is the action, teal is
the voice and the taught thing, coral is the friend who is talking, plum is a
second series, good/bad are the only verdicts.

**Interaction strength and quality: the Mathematics, Science and Computing
builds'.** Every step is a machine the child drives, not a page they read —
and in this subject the machine is the SKILL done on real people and real
sources: a question is built from a question word and an ending and read
back; a picture is read by finding the things in it; six classmates are asked
in turn and their answers fill a pictogram as they answer; an answer is
relevant because it is ABOUT the question, decided by a tag the builder and
the gate compute rather than an authored flag; an action is a solution because
its effect is what the issue needs; a share lets both children finish or does
not, by arithmetic; and the reflection steps read the child's OWN record of
the team step they just played. Where the child's own opinion is the answer,
the page marks nothing and reads the opinion back. Each step has the four-move
authored explainer behind Explain and a `data-say` line spoken on arrival.
Sound is synthesised; nothing is recorded.

### The content: Cambridge Primary Global Perspectives 0838, Stage 1, all of it

- The framework is already extracted to
  `src/curriculum/cambridge-global-perspectives-0838.json` (Cambridge prints
  no objective codes for this subject; the extractor assigns
  `<stage><subStrand>.01`, stable because it derives from the sub-strand).
  Stage 1 has **18** objectives across six strands: Research, Analysis,
  Evaluation, Reflection, Collaboration, Communication. `npm run
  validate:frameworks` must keep passing.
- Organise lessons by **skill cluster, each anchored on one topic a
  five-year-old can talk about** — not by the four units of the Word-pack
  course under `global-perspectives/grade-1/data/` (built from a Teacher &
  Parent Guide, an Activity Sheet and a Mini-Project). Eight lessons: asking
  (pets); finding out and recording (getting to school, playtime); what we
  know and answering what was asked (keeping healthy); consequences and
  solutions (water and looking after things); sources and opinions (food);
  teamwork and who did what (the class garden, the class mural); talking and
  listening (my family); and a look back over the course.
- **Every step names the objective codes it exercises**, the builder refuses
  a code Stage 1 does not publish, and a gate on the BUILT pages fails if any
  of the 18 is reached by no step. Reflection is a strand, so every lesson
  ends its own steps with a look-back drawn from the lesson's own about lines
  and step titles (1Fv.01, 1Fl.01), and Lesson 8 authors one over the whole
  course.
- Language: British English, five-to-six-year-old reading level, the learner
  addressed as "you", one idea per sentence. Distractors are true things about
  OTHER topics, never nonsense — relevance is the skill. Home projects are
  talk-shaped (an interview, a family survey, a walk), because Stage 1 Global
  Perspectives is a talk-with-a-grown-up subject.

### The shapes that are the point

- **Question builder (1Rq.01):** a card says what you want to know; the child
  picks a question word and an ending and presses Ask it; a valid question
  aimed at the wrong thing is answered as a good question about something
  else, not as wrong.
- **Picture source (1Ri.01):** a drawn scene with things to find; each says
  the fact it shows; the closing question is keyed by a spot in the picture.
- **Survey and pictogram (1Rc.01, 1Rf.01, 1Ad.01):** nobody answers until
  asked; each recorded answer is one more picture in a row; the pictogram
  questions are keyed by arithmetic the builder re-computes from the
  classmates' answers.
- **Relevance (1Mi.01, 1Ml.01, 1Ap.01, 1Ea.01):** every option carries a
  topic tag; the right one is the one whose tag matches; a wrong pick says
  what it WAS about. A follow-up question is offered only after the talk has
  been heard line by line.
- **Consequences and solutions (1Ac.01, 1As.01):** predict, then see what
  happens to YOU; try an action on an issue and see what it did; only the
  action whose effect the issue needs fixes it.
- **Team and contributions (1Cc.01, 1Ct.01, 1Fc.01, 1Ft.01):** the garden or
  mural grows one stage per round played well; a share is arithmetic; every
  action is logged, and the reflection step reads that log to ask which of
  these YOU did and what each friend did.

### Wiring and progress

- `app.config.json` describes the build for the shared tools;
  `progressUnitPrefix` is `l` because these lessons are not the shell
  course's units (read THE UNIT PROBLEM in `wire-progress.py`); `courseKey`
  is the shell's `ehel-gp-g01`.
- Run, in order: `build-lessons.py`, `build-hub.py`, then `wire-navigation`,
  `wire-platform-controls`, `preload-platform`, `wire-progress`,
  `add-header-bars`, `check-lessons`, then the kit's own `check-coverage.py`.
- Do NOT deploy. `deploy.mjs --app .` plans; `--upload` is an owner decision,
  and routing Grade 1 Global Perspectives here is a Moodle setting nobody's
  course points at yet.

### Definition of done

1. `build-lessons.py` builds 8 pages with 0 refusals and reports 18/18.
2. `check-lessons.py` and `check-coverage.py` exit 0; `validate:frameworks`
   exits 0.
3. Every inline classic script parses under `node --check`.
4. The gate is mutation-tested and each of these must fail it, with the pages
   restored byte-identical afterwards: a lesson losing an objective, an
   unpublished code, a quiz with no key, a sort into a missing bin, a survey
   answer edited after its pictogram was keyed, a pictogram key that
   disagrees with its rows, two answers about the question, a follow-up
   question nobody could ask, an issue no given action fixes, a share round
   where no option lets both finish, two sources about the topic, a question
   the ending does not allow, a look-back that lists a learned line as not
   learned, a know board or an opinion round with no distractor, a
   contribution record with nothing the child did, a lesson under its floor.
5. Each lesson is opened in a browser from a static server and every step is
   driven to completion; the dot rail, the header progress and the sticker
   shelf agree. The only console errors are the five platform modules that
   404 in local dev by design. No step overflows at 375px.
6. A README beside the build says what it is, what is generated, what will
   bite, and what was deliberately not done.

### What is deliberately out of scope

Deploying; routing a learner; recorded narration (the voice engine speaks);
reusing the Word-pack course's text; anything at Stage 2+; recording a real
class's survey (the classmates are drawn — a real whole-class survey is the
teacher's, and the home projects send the child to do one).

### Why the original sentence needed this

- "Grade 1 Global Perspectives model" could mean a content model (the JSON
  the shell renders) or a lesson app. The four reference builds settle it: a
  standalone lesson app.
- "modeling the design after english/grade-1-v2" and "quality after the new
  math and science" are one instruction, because the builds share one design
  and differ only in what the child drives. Global Perspectives has no
  experiments and no programs; it has SKILLS done on people and sources, so
  the machines are relationships — relevance, arithmetic on a share, a log of
  who did what — rather than simulations.
- "experimentation, and demonstration" in this subject is asking, recording
  and looking at what was recorded, which is exactly 1Rc.01, 1Rf.01 and
  1Ad.01, so it is stated as objectives with codes, which is what lets a gate
  say whether it was done.
- "Curriculum Framework 0838" was already extracted, and Cambridge prints no
  codes for it, so the codes in every page are Ehel's own and the grown-up
  page says so.
