# Grade 3 Computing (computing/grade-3-v2) validation

Reviewed 2026-09-11; its fixes were deployed the same day, with Grade 4 for the shared kit changes. Revised the same day after the remaining items (a43aee2ac), which are committed and not deployed. Reviewer: Claude (automated measurement + judgement); no human teacher review yet.  
Against the 27-area framework in 'computing-grades validation.docx'. Scale: 1 Unsatisfactory; 2 Needs major improvement; 3 Acceptable with revisions; 4 Strong; 5 Excellent.  
What was reviewed: the standalone lesson build that ehel-comp-g03 launches (computing/grade-3-app, served as app/computing/grade-3-v2), not the Word-pack course under computing/grade-3, which the shell serves when no override applies and which declares a different framework.  

The Word report beside the owner's other validation reports (OneDrive: `computing-grade-3-v2 validation report.docx`) carries the same content; both are written from one script.

## Summary

The build teaches all 36 Cambridge Primary Computing 0059 Stage 3 objectives across fourteen lessons, more deeply than any other grade. A check by hand of every objective against the steps that claim it found each one taught through something the child does and nothing from Stage 4. The first version found one pair only partly met, 3MD.02 and 3MD.03, which name discrete and categorical data while the lessons recorded and charted only a count; since the second round the same eight children also say which pet they would most like, and the child records it and reads it as a table, a bar chart and a pictogram. 98 of the 104 teaching steps can tell a child “not quite”, each of the 145 questions has one key and a reason, and every lesson was played to the end in a browser after the last change, with no errors and nothing wider than a phone screen.

Grade 3 already carries the Grade 1 validation's shared fixes, deployed on 2026-09-11. The one that mattered most was checked again here on the live pages: all fourteen lessons store every step, are recorded complete, and reopen where the learner left them.

Reading every learner-facing line found eleven kinds of error, all corrected. Lesson 3 taught that toast comes before butter “because butter melts on hot toast”, a reason that does not explain the order, in three places including a quiz key; it now says butter in a toaster would melt and burn. Its cereal activity marked “Boil the milk” as a step nobody needs, which many families take. Lesson 7's home hunt counts a doorbell among machines with a computer inside, when lesson 14 teaches that an old doorbell has none. Lesson 11 called a laptop's network cable “a cable to the wall socket”, which a child reads as the charger, in a sort and a quiz. Lesson 9 showed a child the objective code “3MD.03” in a quiz reason and answered a shoe-size question with “Count the feet”. The rest were smaller: a machine said to take “a WORD in” whose second round takes a number, a filter rule stated as true of one table only, a surprise “sent to your dad” that was for him, a padlock the most-used browser replaced in 2023, a reason that read “Cost is a cost.”, “programme” for program, and a plug “on a phone timer”.

Four home projects changed for safety. One asked a child, with no grown-up named, to change the inputs of a kettle and a toaster and see what the output did (it is now a torch, a light switch, a TV remote and a washing machine, with a grown-up); one had a grown-up follow the child's toast algorithm “silly mistakes and all” (it is now packing a school bag); a recipe to change and try, and a hunt for inputs along the street, named no grown-up (both now do).

The measurements found three things the reading could not. The Stage 3 machines had never been measured, and 64 of their controls were under 44 px on a phone: the cipher keys, the spreadsheet's column letters and choice chips, and the number chips inside program blocks; two label styles were 11 and 12 px. Eight pictures needed 2020 or 2021 devices, in 20 places, and the kit drew the shake input with a 2022 face that an older tablet shows as an empty box. All three are fixed, the last two in the shared kit. Contrast passes everywhere it was measured.

This review's first round was deployed on 2026-09-11, and all fourteen lessons were played to the end again on the live pages afterwards. The second round, the same day, did the content work the first left open. Categorical data is recorded and charted (lesson 9). Lessons 2 to 14 open with a line recalling the lesson before, and every lesson has a two-question warm-up that is never marked. The seven activities that repeated Grades 1 and 2 are Grade 3's own: a smoothie to follow and to change, a kite to debug and to change, a cake and a present to cut the waste from, a fruit salad to sort, and a new two-bug program, drawn by four new scenes in the shared kit. And the voice is handed a letter standing on its own in capitals, “A is 1” where the page shows “a is 1”, the form speech engines read as the letter's name; the rule is in the shared kit, so it reaches every grade, and an audit lists every line it changes. The second round is committed and not deployed.

What is left is work no measurement can do: a teacher reading the content and the question keys, somebody listening to the cipher lesson and watching children use a lesson, and a walk-through with a screen reader.

Change status at 2026-09-11, second version: 10 items need no change or are done, 5 have their fix committed and wait for the deploy, 5 have their fix deployed with a human check or an owner decision still open, 7 are open and not started.

Average score 4.3 / 5 across 27 areas (4.1 in the first version); lowest 3 (Multimedia Quality); 8 areas at 5.

- Second version, the remaining items (a43aee2ac, not deployed). Four scores rose: Cambridge Curriculum Alignment 4 to 5 (area 5), Lesson Structure 4 to 5 (9), Assessment Balance 3 to 4 (11) and Duplication 3 to 4 (26).

## Verdict on Grade 3 Computing

Nothing found stops the children already using Grade 3. Its fourteen lessons were played to the end on the live pages before this review changed anything, and every record was complete. The errors this review found are corrected and live, and the fourteen lessons were played to the end on the live pages again after the deploy. The build teaches all 36 Stage 3 objectives through activities, more deeply than any other grade, and holds the Stage 4 line. The second round meets 3MD.02 and 3MD.03 in full, adds a recap and a warm-up, and gives Grade 3 its own tasks; it is committed and waits for the owner's word to deploy.

Before it is called validated rather than measured, a teacher should read the content and the question keys, somebody should hear the cipher lesson and watch a child use a lesson, and one should be walked with a screen reader.
