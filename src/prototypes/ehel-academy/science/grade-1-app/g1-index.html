Grade 1 Science

Eight lessons, in the order they are meant to be done. Every one has a real experiment in it: predict, try it, say what happened. About 345 minutes in all, one lesson a week.

Lesson 1: Alive or Never Alive17 steps · about 40 minutes · 10 objectives

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 1)

  • 1Bp.01 Identify living things and things that have never been alive.
  • 1TWSc.01 Sort and group objects, materials and living things based on observations of the similarities and differences between them.
  • 1TWSp.01 Ask questions about the world around us and talk about how to find answers.
  • 1Bp.02 Know that animals, including humans, need air, water and suitable food to survive.
  • 1Bp.03 Know that plants need light and water to survive.
  • 1TWSp.02 Make predictions about what they think will happen.
  • 1TWSc.04 Follow instructions safely when doing practical work.
  • 1TWSa.01 Describe what happened during an enquiry and if it matched predictions.
  • 1TWSc.05 Collect and record observations and/or measurements by annotating images and completing simple tables.

Do the experiment for real

  • Does a plant need water?. Two small plants or two cuttings in cups. Water one every day and not the other. Look every day for a week and talk about what changes.
  • Two plants, one watering can. You need: Two small plants or two cuttings in cups, water, a sunny windowsill. Put both plants on the windowsill. Water one plant every day. Give the other no water at all. Look at both plants every day for a week, and say what you see. Look for: Which plant droops and goes yellow, and how many days it takes.
  • Alive or never alive hunt. You need: A garden or a park, a grown-up, paper and a pencil. Walk round slowly with a grown-up and point at ten things. For each one, ask: does it grow, need food and water, or have young? Make two lists: alive, and never alive. Look for: Things that trick you, like a fallen leaf. It was alive once.
  • Feed a pet, or a bird. You need: A pet, or a bird table with seeds and a dish of water. Put out food and water. Watch from a window, without moving, for five minutes. Say which needs you gave: water, food, or both. Look for: Whether the animal eats, drinks, or both.

Answer keys

  • Sort them into two bins Alive: hen, fish, palm tree, bee, grass; Never alive: cup, river, brick, cloud, toy bear
  • What does the cat need? Needs it: water, air, cat food; Does not need it: ball of wool, cat bed, ribbon, television
  • Does a plant need water? prediction: It will droop and go yellow; what happened: It drooped and went yellow
  • Show what you know
    1. Which one is alive? → a camel
    2. Which one was never alive? → a stone
    3. A car moves along the road. Is it alive? → No. Moving is not enough. It never eats or grows.
    4. What does a cat need to stay alive? → air, water and food
    5. You feel thirsty. What is your body asking for? → water
    6. A plant gets no water for five days. What happens? → it droops and goes yellow
    7. A mango tree does not walk about. Is it alive? → Yes. It grows, takes in water and makes seeds.
    8. A hen has chicks. What does that tell you? → The hen is alive, because living things can have babies.
    9. What would happen to a pet fish if nobody ever fed it? → It could not stay alive, because animals need food

Do the experiment for real

  • Does a plant need light?. Two small plants. Put one on a sunny windowsill and one inside a cupboard, and water both. Look after a few days: the one in the dark goes pale.
  • Grow a bean in a cup. You need: A clear plastic cup, a broad bean (do not eat it), kitchen paper, water, a grown-up. With a grown-up, line the cup with wet kitchen paper and push the bean between the paper and the side of the cup. Keep the paper damp and the cup on a windowsill. Look every day. Draw what you see. Look for: The root comes first, going down. Then the shoot, going up.
  • Plant in the dark. You need: Two small plants, a cupboard, water. Put one plant on a sunny windowsill and one inside a dark cupboard. Water both the same. After four days, put them side by side. Look for: The plant from the dark is pale, thin and floppy.
  • Measure a plant in cubes. You need: A plant, some building cubes or sugar cubes. Stand the cubes in a tower next to the plant. Count the cubes up to the top leaf. Do it again in a week. Look for: Did the number go up? By how many?

Answer keys

  • Which part?
    1. Which part drinks water from the soil? → roots
    2. Which part catches sunlight and makes food? → leaves
    3. Which part makes the seeds? → flower
    4. Which part holds the plant up tall? → stem
    5. Which part is hidden under the soil? → roots
    6. Which part is usually the most colourful? → flower
  • Does a plant need light? prediction: It will go pale and droopy; what happened: It went pale, thin and droopy
  • Show what you know
    1. Which part holds the plant in the soil? → roots
    2. Which part makes food using sunlight? → leaves
    3. A new plant grows from a... → seed
    4. What do plants need to stay alive? → light and water
    5. Which part carries water up to the leaves? → the stem
    6. A plant is kept in a dark cupboard with water. What happens? → it goes pale and droopy
    7. Which grew first from the seed? → the root
    8. A seedling is 3 cubes tall and a sunflower is 8 cubes tall. Which is taller? → the sunflower
    9. What would happen to a plant if all its roots were cut off? → It could not take in water, so it would droop

Do the experiment for real

  • The feely bag. You need: A bag or a pillowcase, and a few small things, like a spoon, a sponge, a key and a potato. Nothing sharp.. A grown-up puts the things in the bag without showing you. Nothing sharp. Put your hand in and feel one, without looking. Say what it is, then pull it out and check. Look for: Which sense told you? Touch. What tricked you?
  • Measure your family in hands. You need: Your hand, a wall, a pencil, paper. Stand each person against the wall and mark their height with a pencil. Measure from the floor to the mark in your hand spans. Ask a grown-up to measure the same mark with their hand. Write each name and number in a table. Look for: Who is tallest? Why does a grown-up's hand span give a smaller number?
  • Same and different survey. You need: Paper and a pencil, three people. Ask three people: what colour are your eyes? Is your hair curly or straight? Draw a table with a row for each person. Count what is the same and what is different. Look for: Most people have the same parts. The details are different.

Answer keys

  • Which sense is it?
    1. You know bread is baking before you see it. Which part told you? → nose
    2. A dog barks behind you. Which part told you? → ears
    3. The sand is hot under your feet. Which part told you? → skin
    4. You see a red bird in the tree. Which part told you? → eyes
    5. The lemon is sour! Which part told you? → tongue
    6. The blanket feels soft. Which part told you? → skin
    7. Thunder rumbles far away. Which part told you? → ears
    8. Smoke! Which sense warns you first, before you see the fire? → smell, with your nose
  • The feely bag
    1. It feels soft and fluffy. → a woolly hat
    2. It feels hard, cold, small and round. → a marble
    3. It feels rough and bumpy, like sandpaper. → a rock
    4. It feels light, soft and tickly. → a feather
    5. It feels smooth, cold and hard, and it has a handle. → a metal spoon
  • Everyone, or not everyone? Everyone: a nose, a head, skin, a heart; Some people: curly hair, glasses, brown eyes, a missing tooth
  • Show what you know
    1. Which body part do we use to see? → eyes
    2. Which body part do we use to hear? → ears
    3. We smell a flower with our... → nose
    4. How many senses do we have? → five
    5. We taste an orange with our... → tongue
    6. Which is at the end of your leg? → your foot
    7. What does every person have? → a head and a body
    8. Sami is 10 hand spans tall and Nora is 8. Who is shorter? → Nora
    9. Why would it be hard to find the marble in the feely bag if you wore thick gloves? → The gloves stop your skin feeling the shape

Do the experiment for real

  • Squash, bend, twist, stretch. A ball of play dough, an elastic band and a stone. Squash, bend, twist and stretch each one. Which changed shape? Which did not?
  • Material hunt. You need: Paper and a pencil, your house. Find something made of wood, metal, plastic, glass, paper and fabric. Draw each one and write its material. Find one object made of two materials. Look for: An object can have more than one material. A pencil is wood and something else.
  • The waterproof test. You need: A tray, a cup of water, small pieces of paper, fabric, plastic, foil, a leaf. Lay each piece on the tray. Drip a little water on each one. Wait a minute and lift each piece up. Look for: Which pieces let the water through, and which kept it out.
  • Squash it, bend it. You need: Play dough, an elastic band, a stone, a sponge. Squash each thing. Then bend it. Then stretch it. Stretch the elastic band gently, away from your face. Say what happened to each one. Put them in two piles: changed shape, did not change shape. Look for: Does the sponge go back to its shape? Does the play dough?

Answer keys

  • Object, or material? Object: cup, shoe, key, book; Material: metal, wood, fabric, glass
  • Sort by material Wood: pencil, chair; Metal: coin, nut and bolt; Plastic: shampoo bottle, toothbrush; Glass: drinking glass; Paper: newspaper; Fabric: scarf, socks
  • Describe it
    1. A pillow feels... → soft
    2. A stone feels... → hard
    3. Sandpaper feels... → rough
    4. A glass window is... → smooth and see-through
    5. An elastic band is... → bendy and stretchy
    6. A metal spoon is... → hard and shiny
    7. A wooden ruler is... → stiff
    8. A metal spoon is hard. What else is it? → shiny and waterproof too
    9. A sponge is soft. What else is it? → bendy, and it soaks up water
  • Squash, bend, twist, stretch prediction: Nothing. The stone will keep its shape; what happened: Nothing. It kept its shape
  • Show what you know
    1. A metal spoon. Which word is the material ? → metal
    2. What is a wooden chair made from? → wood
    3. Which material can you see through? → glass
    4. Which of these feels rough? → sandpaper
    5. Which of these is bendy? → an elastic band
    6. You squash a ball of clay. What happens? → it changes shape
    7. You squash a stone as hard as you can. What happens? → nothing, it keeps its shape
    8. Which is the best material for a raincoat? → plastic, because it is waterproof
    9. Why would a sponge be a bad material for a cup? → It soaks up water, so the drink would leak out

Do the experiment for real

  • Push gently, push hard. A ball on a smooth floor and some tape marks a step apart. Push gently, then hard, and count how many marks the ball passes each time.
  • Float or sink?. A bowl of water and eight things from the kitchen (an apple, a stone, a coin, a cork, a leaf, a spoon). Predict out loud before each one goes in.
  • Float or sink in the sink. You need: A bowl of water and eight small things: an apple, a stone, a coin, a cork, a leaf, a spoon, a lid, a grape. Do this with a grown-up. Before each one goes in, say: float or sink. Drop it in gently and watch. Put the floaters in one pile and the sinkers in another. Look for: Which prediction was wrong? Heavy things do not always sink.
  • Gentle push, hard push. You need: A ball, a smooth floor, some tape. Stick tape marks on the floor a step apart. Push the ball gently and count the marks it passes. Push it hard and count again. Look for: How many more marks the hard push passed.
  • Push or pull hunt. You need: Paper and a pencil. Walk round the house and find ten things you push or pull: doors, drawers, taps, switches. Draw each one. Write push or pull next to it. Look for: Some things need a push and a pull. A drawer is both.

Answer keys

  • Push or pull? Push: kicking a ball, pressing a doorbell, pushing a trolley, pushing a swing; Pull: opening a drawer, tug of war, pulling a dog back on its lead, pulling a door open
  • Push gently, push hard prediction: The ball will roll further; what happened: The ball rolled further, nine steps instead of three
  • Float or sink? apple: Float; stone: Sink; wooden log: Float; metal key: Sink; leaf: Float; coin: Sink; empty plastic bottle: Float; brick: Sink

    6. Which of these floats? → an apple
    7. A ball rolls towards you. You put your hand in front. What happens? → it stops
    8. How does a spinning top move? → it spins round and round
    9. Omar pushed the same ball twice. It rolled further the second time. Why? → He pushed it harder the second time
