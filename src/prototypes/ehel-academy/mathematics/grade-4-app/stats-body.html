Asking, Sorting and Chance

1

What would answer it?

Pick a question, then decide what to collect. Some answers are groups you can name. Some are numbers you can count.

What kind of data would you collect?

2

Tally, then count

Twenty-two children answer one at a time. Mark each one, and cross the gate on every fifth. Then count the gates.

3

One set, three pictures

The same 22 answers, drawn three ways. Each one is good at something different.

4

Sorting two ways at once

Sort 1 to 20 by two questions at once: is it even, and is it more than 10? A Venn diagram overlaps two hoops. A Carroll diagram uses four boxes.

5

Two classes, one question

Both classes have 22 children and answered the same question. What is the same, and what is different?

6

Impossible to certain

Some things cannot happen and some are sure to. Most sit somewhere in between. Put each one on the line.

7

Spin it, and spin it again

Four equal parts, so each should come up a quarter of the time. A few spins are lumpy. Many spins even out.
