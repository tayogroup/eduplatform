## Overview

Stage 1 Science is a first year of science for five- and six-year-olds, and it is built around noticing. It follows **Cambridge Primary Science 0846, Stage 1**.

Six units ask what it means to be alive, how a plant grows, what a body is and does, what the things around us are made of, what a push or a pull does to an object, and where sound comes from. Nothing is abstract: a learner looks at something real and is asked what they notice about it.

Across the year: **6 units** in 3 terms, **36 learning outcomes**,
**25 concepts**, **25 visual models**, **48 worked examples**,
**72 practice questions**, **72 fluency questions**, **36 real-world
problems**, **24 reasoning prompts**, **36 hands-on activities** and
**72 games**. Each unit ends in a 12-question challenge at a **80% pass
mark**, and the year ends in the stage capstone with a 18-question quiz.

**This is a self-paced course, not a taught one.** There are no live sessions, no
assignments to hand in and no marks in the course data. Unlike Ehel
Mathematics, Science does **not** attach a grown-up guide to its concepts — the
explanations are written to the learner, and the course expects to be read
rather than taught.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Stage 1 assumes no prior science.

Before Unit 1 a learner takes the **Stage 1 Science Readiness Check**: 12 questions, about 10 minutes, on noticing the world, materials around me, and asking questions. Attempts are unlimited and it is never a fail.

Sections:

- **Noticing the world** (5 questions)
- **Materials around me** (4 questions)
- **Asking questions** (3 questions)

The report gives one of four results — ready, ready with a little review, a
critical section below its threshold, or *let's build strong roots first*, which
recommends **Stage 1 Science, Unit 1 with a grown-up**. Where it names earlier units to
revisit, those links open the named unit directly.

The check can be retaken at any point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- **Household materials for 36 hands-on activities** — 6 units × 6 each. Science
  at Ehel Academy is not a reading course: each unit expects things to be
  handled, sorted, grown, shone through or pushed. Each activity lists its own
  materials, and none of them needs buying.
- A device that can display the course, with **sound** — the narration is
  complete and is a real part of the teaching, not an accessibility extra.
- An internet connection.
- A grown-up on hand for the practical work, and for anything involving water, heat or small parts.

### Attendance

**This course has no live sessions.** There is nothing to attend and nothing to
miss.

### Homework

There is no homework to submit, because there are no assignments in this course.

In every unit a learner works through the concepts and visual models, the
explorations, the methods and worked examples, 12 practice and 12 fluency
questions, 6 real-world problems, the reasoning prompts, **6 hands-on
activities**, 12 games to a mastery score of 3, and finishes on the
unit challenge.

The activities are the part that leaves the screen, and they are the part worth
protecting when a week gets busy.

### Assessment and grading

**There are no marks in this course.** Nothing is graded out of anything and
nothing is compared between learners.

What exists instead:

- **The unit challenge** — 12 questions at the end of each unit, pass mark
  80%. 6 across the year.
- **Games**, 12 per unit, mastery score 3.
- **Self-assessment** — 36 *I can…* statements across the year, carrying no
  marks and never used to rank a learner.
- **The stage capstone** and its 18-question quiz.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Do the activities, not just the reading.** Six per unit, 36 across the year.
  A learner who skips them has read a science course rather than done one.
- **Say what you notice before you say why.** The explorations ask for an
  observation first and an explanation second, which is the habit the subject is
  teaching.
- **Use the vocabulary and the connections.** Each unit's reference section
  carries its rules, terms, vocabulary, common mistakes and the links to other
  units — the last of these is what turns six units into one subject.
- **Safety is part of the work.** Practical activities are done with an adult
  present where anything involves heat, electricity, chemicals or small parts.
  The capstone asks for a safety checklist, which is a fair signal of how the
  course expects practical science to be treated.
