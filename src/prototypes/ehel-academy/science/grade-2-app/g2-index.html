Lesson 1: Animals and Their Coverings17 steps · about 40 minutes · 7 objectives

What it teaches (Cambridge Primary Science 0097, Stage 2)

  • 2Bs.01 Compare how animals, including humans, are similar and different in their external body parts and skin covering.
  • 2TWSc.01 Sort and group objects, materials and living things based on observations of the similarities and differences between them.
  • 2Bp.03 Describe how the offspring of animals, including humans, change as they become older.
  • 2Bp.04 Know that animals, including humans, produce offspring that have a combination of features from their parents.
  • 2TWSm.03 Describe the difference between a diagram and a picture.
  • 2TWSp.01 Ask questions about the world around us and talk about how to find answers.
  • 2SIC.03 Know that everyone uses science and identify people who use science professionally.

Do the experiment for real

  • Covering hunt. You need: A garden, a park or a window, paper and a pencil. Spot five animals: birds, insects, a cat, a dog, a snail. For each one, say what covers it. Draw a table: animal and covering. Look for: Which covering did you see most? Insects have a hard case.
  • Draw a diagram of a pet. You need: A pet or a picture of an animal, paper, a pencil, a ruler. Draw the animal simply. Draw a straight line from each part out to the edge. Write a label at the end of each line. Look for: A diagram names the parts. A picture only shows the look.
  • Family look-alikes. You need: Photos of your family. Look at a child and both parents. Find two things the child got from one parent and two from the other. Say what is different from both. Look for: Offspring get a mix, not a copy.

Answer keys

  • Sort by covering Fur: dog, mouse, rabbit; Feathers: duck, penguin, hen; Scales: crocodile, lizard; Bare skin: person, dolphin
  • Alike and different
    1. A cat and a dog. What do they BOTH have? → fur and four legs
    2. A bird and a fish. What is DIFFERENT? → feathers and wings, or scales and fins
    3. You and a monkey. What do you BOTH have? → two arms and two legs
    4. How many legs does a spider have? → eight
    5. Which animal has NO legs? → a snake
    6. What do you have that a fish does not? → arms and legs
  • Diagram, or picture? Diagram: a bird drawing with labels, a fish drawing with labels; Picture: a photo of a bird, a photo of a fish, a painting of a cat
  • Show what you know
    1. What is a bird covered with? → feathers
    2. What is a fish covered with? → scales
    3. Which animal has bare skin and hair only on its head? → a person
    4. What comes FIRST in a hen's life? → an egg
    5. A newborn kitten... → has its eyes shut and drinks milk
    6. A brown dog and a spotty dog have puppies. The puppies are... → a mix: some brown, some spotty, some both
    7. A drawing of a plant with lines pointing to 'root', 'stem' and 'leaf' is a... → diagram
    8. Which two animals BOTH have four legs and fur? → a cat and a dog

Do the experiment for real

  • The two-minute brush. You need: A toothbrush, toothpaste, a timer or a two-minute song. Put a pea of paste on the brush. Brush in small circles: front, sides, backs, tops. Keep going until the song ends. Do it morning and night for a week. Look for: Does two minutes feel longer than you thought?
  • The egg and the fizzy drink. You need: Two boiled eggs still in their shells, a cup of fizzy drink, a cup of water, a grown-up. Put one egg in the fizzy drink and one in the water. Leave them overnight. Rinse them and compare the shells. Look for: Eggshell is a bit like the outside of a tooth. What has the fizzy drink done to it?
  • Healthy day chart. You need: Paper and a pencil. Draw a chart with a row for each day of the week. Each day tick: fruit or vegetables, water, played outside, washed, slept well. Count the ticks at the end of the week. Look for: Which one was hardest to tick every day?

Answer keys

  • Healthy or not? Healthy habit: eating vegetables, washing hands before eating, running and playing outside, a good night's sleep, washing every day, drinking water; Not healthy: sweets all day, sitting still all day, fizzy drinks every day
  • Health check
    1. Your friend has a hot forehead and feels shivery. What is that a sign of? → a fever, being ill
    2. Before you eat, you should... → wash your hands
    3. Which keeps your heart and muscles strong? → moving and playing every day
    4. How many times a day should you brush your teeth? → two
    5. Which teeth grind food at the back of your mouth? → molars
    6. You feel ill. What should you do first? → tell a grown-up
  • Show what you know
    1. Which teeth are flat and at the front, for cutting? → incisors
    2. Which teeth are pointed, for tearing? → canines
    3. How long should you brush your teeth for? → two minutes
    4. What feeds the germs that make holes in teeth? → sugar
    5. What are the three things a body needs to stay healthy? → good food, keeping clean and moving
    6. Which is a sign of illness? → a fever and a cough
    7. Why do your adult teeth matter so much? → they have to last your whole life
    8. Where did you find the answer 'brush for two minutes'? → in the fact card, a secondary source

Do the experiment for real

Count each kind: ants, woodlice, snails, worms, spiders. Draw a block graph: one block per animal. Look for: Which spot had the most? Damp, dark places are full of life.

Find one thing that is natural AND manufactured, like a wooden table with a plastic top. Look for: Most things are manufactured. Which natural ones did you find?

    3. Why is a jumper made of wool? → wool is soft and warm
    4. Why are wellington boots made of rubber? → rubber is waterproof
    5. Why is a towel made of cotton? → cotton soaks up water
    6. Why is a bicycle frame made of metal? → metal is strong and stiff
    7. Would a sponge make a good window? → No. A sponge is not see-through
  • Show what you know

    6. Why is a raincoat made of plastic? → plastic is waterproof
    7. Glass is made by melting... → sand
    8. Which is the best material for a towel? → cotton, because it soaks up water

Do the experiment for real

  • Melt it, freeze it. You need: An ice cube, a plate, an ice tray, a freezer. Put an ice cube on a plate in a warm room and watch it melt. Pour the water into the ice tray and put it in the freezer. Look the next morning. Look for: The same water came back as ice. Reversible.
  • Cook an egg with a grown-up. You need: An egg, a pan, a hob, a grown-up. Crack the egg into a bowl. Look: runny and clear. A grown-up cooks it in the pan. Watch it change. Let it cool. Is it runny again? Look for: It stays cooked. A new material. You cannot get the egg back.
  • Toast test. You need: A slice of bread, a toaster, a grown-up. Look at the bread. Soft and pale. A grown-up toasts it. Let it cool and look again. Look for: Brown and crisp, and cooling does not make it bread again.

Answer keys

  • Cook an egg, then try to un-cook it prediction: It will stay cooked; what happened: It stayed cooked. It did not go back to raw
  • New material, or the same one back? Same material back: ice melting, clay squashed flat, chocolate melting, paper folded, water freezing; New material: bread toasting brown, a candle burning, an egg frying, wood burning, cake mix baking
  • Same or new?
    1. Butter melts in a hot pan. Can you get solid butter back? → Yes, let it cool. Same material.
    2. A marshmallow is toasted brown over a fire. Same material? → No. Toasting made a new material.
    3. A snowman melts in the sun. What is the water? → the same material as the snow
    4. A match is struck and burns. Same material after? → No. Burning made ash and smoke, new materials.
  • Show what you know
    1. You cook an egg. Can you cool it back into a raw egg? → No. Cooking made a new material.
    2. Ice melts. Is the water a new material? → No. It is the same water, and can freeze again.
    3. Which change makes a NEW material? → burning wood
    4. Which change keeps the SAME material? → melting butter
    5. Dough goes in the oven. What comes out? → bread, a new material
    6. Who works the cooker in a heating experiment? → a grown-up
    7. The pan has stopped steaming. What do you do? → wait, or use oven gloves
    8. Long ago people tried to make gold by heating other materials. What happened? → It never worked, but it started chemistry

Do the experiment for real

  • Gentle, medium, hard. A ball on a smooth floor and some tape marks a step apart. Push gently, then hard, and count how many marks the ball passes each time.
  • A force changes shape too. A ball of play dough, an elastic band and a stone. Squash, bend, twist and stretch each one. Which changed shape? Which did not?
  • Three pushes. You need: A ball, a smooth floor, tape, and your feet to measure with. Mark a start line with tape. Push gently, then medium, then hard. Measure each roll in foot-lengths. Draw a block graph of the three. Look for: Do the columns get taller each time? That is the pattern.
  • Squash, bend, twist, stretch. You need: Play dough, an elastic band, a stone, a sponge, a wooden spoon. Try all four actions on each thing. Say which changed shape and which did not. Say which went back to its shape by itself. Look for: The band springs back. The clay does not. The stone never changed.
  • What stopped it?. You need: A toy car or a ball, a carpet, a wooden floor, a wall. Push the car the same way on carpet and on wood. Watch where it stops each time. Push it gently at the wall. Look for: The car goes further on wood. Why? What stopped it at the wall?
