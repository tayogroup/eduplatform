## Overview

Grade 2 English is the year of the calendar and the complete sentence. It
follows **Cambridge Primary English 0058, Stage 2**.

Unit 1 opens on saying and spelling your own name, introducing a partner, and
saying what people like — then adds the days of the week, the months of the
year, and the order words for dates. The year moves out from there: neighbours
and jobs, races and movement, the sky, measuring, bugs, the world around us,
home, and the city.

Across the year a learner meets **306 words**, reads **43 texts**, and works
through **71 learning outcomes**. Unit 10 publishes: a six-page booklet, three
chosen portfolio pieces each with a reason, a two-minute presentation, and a
reflection that sets a goal for Year 3.

A learner arrives from Grade 1 having met letters, sounds and first words. Grade
2 is where those become sentences a child writes on purpose, with the capital
letters checked.

## Course policies

### Prerequisites

Grade 2 assumes Grade 1. There is no foundation unit inside this course — Grade
1's *Alphabet & Sounds* is where a learner who has not met the letters is sent.

Before Unit 1 every learner sits the **Grade 2 English Placement Exam**: 25
questions, 25 marks, about 20 minutes, in three sections, all of them Grade 1
material:

- **Grade 1 words** (9 questions)
- **Grade 1 sounds and reading** (8 questions)
- **Grade 1 sentences and speaking** (8 questions)

Attempts are unlimited and it is never a fail. The report names specific Grade 1
units to revisit — *Family Time*, *On the Farm*, *City Places* for the words;
*Alphabet & Sounds*, *My Five Senses*, *Wonderful Water* for sounds and reading
— and those links open the named unit directly even though Grade 1 is otherwise
walked one unit at a time. The lowest band recommends **Grade 1 English** as the
course to take.

The exam can be retaken at any point in the year.

### Materials and equipment

- Something to write and draw with, and a folder that keeps finished work. The
  Unit 10 booklet and portfolio are built from work saved across the whole year,
  so keep it from Unit 1.
- Paper, colours, scissors and glue for the six-page booklet in Unit 10.
- A way to record audio or video. Every unit assignment includes a spoken piece,
  and the capstone presentation runs to two minutes.
- A device that plays audio and video — each live session expects the teacher
  lecture to have been watched beforehand.
- An internet connection for live sessions.

Nothing on this list needs buying beyond ordinary stationery, and the capstone
marking says so explicitly.

### Attendance

The course runs **60 live sessions** — 6 per unit, all 45 minutes.

Each session names its preparation, and at this age it is specific: watch the
teacher lecture, then learn that session's word group — Unit 1 session 1 asks
for *name, partner, like, spell, friend, hello, goodbye*.

Attendance is expected at every session. Tell the teacher in advance where
possible; sessions are recorded and a missed one should be watched before the
next. More than two missed in a unit and the teacher will contact the family to
agree how to catch up.

### Homework

Each unit carries **six activities** and **one assignment** — **10 assignments**
across the year.

An assignment is three pieces, not two. Unit 1 asks for a piece of writing with
every capital letter checked, a recording introducing yourself, and a second
piece of writing using a date. All three are **checked against the writing and
speaking rubrics before they are sent**, which is a habit this course starts
early and every later grade keeps.

Expect **30–45 minutes** on an assignment across the unit's two weeks.

The Unit 10 capstone is four submissions: the published booklet, three portfolio
pieces with a reason for each, the two-minute presentation recording, and the
reflection.

### Assessment and grading

**Assignments — 352 marks across the year.** Nine unit assignments at 32 marks,
the Unit 10 capstone at 64.

A unit assignment is marked on two four-level rubrics: **Writing** on Ideas,
Organisation, Language and Conventions, and **Speaking** on Clarity, Vocabulary,
Sentence accuracy and Interaction — four criteria each at four marks. That is 16
and 16, and it accounts for the full 32.

**The capstone's 64 marks are published in full**, which is unusual in this
series and worth repeating to families:

| Component | Marks | How |
|---|---|---|
| Booklet | 24 | 4 per page × 6 pages: 2 for two accurate sentences, 1 for a matching picture, 1 for a correct label |
| Portfolio choice | 12 | 4 per piece: naming the unit, naming the skill, giving a real reason |
| Presentation | 16 | The Speaking rubric — clarity, vocabulary, sentence accuracy, interaction, 4 each |
| Reflection | 12 | 4 for a past sentence with *-ed*, 4 for a present sentence with *-ing*, 4 for a future goal with *will* |

Two marking rules come with it, and both belong in front of families:

> **"Do not award marks for drawing skill or for the materials a family can
> afford."** A child is marked on English, not on what their household could buy
> or how well they draw.
>
> **"Accept scribed or recorded work where a learner needs it."** A learner who
> cannot yet write at length is not thereby marked down.

**Unit quizzes — 100 questions across the year**, ten per unit.

**Final course quiz.** After the capstone: 30 questions, 30 marks, about 30
minutes, in three sections — words and reading, grammar and sentences, and
communication and writing. Pass mark **80%**, and attempts are **unlimited with
targeted review between attempts**. No school decision is needed here; the
course sets the rule itself.

**Self-assessment.** *I can…* statements rated **Not yet / With help / By
myself**, 63 across the year. No marks.

### Behaviour and participation

- **Watch the lecture and learn the words before the session.** The session is
  built on the assumption it was done.
- **Check your own work against the rubric before sending it.** Three pieces,
  every capital letter checked.
- **Speak up in the live sessions.** Interaction is one of the four speaking
  criteria from this year onward.
- **Take turns and listen.** Unit 1 asks a learner to introduce a *partner*, not
  just themselves, which only works if they listened.
- **A grown-up nearby for the recordings.** Less than in Grade 1, where an adult
  is assumed in every unit, but this is still a seven-year-old holding a
  microphone.

### Support and communication

**If a learner is struggling**, the placement exam can be retaken at any point
and its report names the exact Grade 1 units to revisit; those links open those
units directly. Grade 1 English remains available as a course for a learner who
needs the ground under this one.

**Extra help available:**

- The teacher, at the contact above.
- Wehel, the in-course AI tutor, available while the learner is working.
- Recorded audio for the vocabulary and the readings, and the teacher lecture
  for every unit.

**How the school will contact you:** when marked work is returned, after a
missed assignment, after two missed live sessions in a unit, and at the end of
each term.

**If you have a concern**, contact the teacher first. If it is unresolved after
{{n}} school days, contact {{school administrator role/name}}.

**For English as an additional language, or any additional need** — including a
learner who needs work scribed or recorded — tell the teacher at the start of
the year. The capstone marking already provides for it.
