Grade 5 Mathematics

Grade 5 in 10 lessons and then a check, in the order they are meant to be done. Start at the top — each one gets you ready for the next, and the last one checks the lot. 60 numbered steps altogether, which is a term of work rather than one sitting.

Number and shape

Squares, Cubes and Roots

Build a square out of dots and watch it grow by odd numbers, test whether a number is square, undo a square to get its root, stack a cube and take it apart again, and build a triangle of dots.

7 stepsStart Patterns

Rules and Patterns

Run a rule forwards to make a sequence, work a rule out backwards from its terms, find two unknown numbers from two facts, catch the squares and cubes hiding in a sequence, count matchstick patterns, and jump along a number line.

6 stepsStart Number

How Whole Numbers Are Built

Add up the odd numbers and then the even ones, learn which operation comes first, pair up the factors of a number, find what two numbers share, spot a factor at a glance, and sieve out the primes.

7 stepsStart Number

Past the Whole Numbers

Round a number to a size you can say out loud, read the numbers below zero, fill in the gaps between the whole ones, move the digits by ten, cut a whole into equal parts, and compare two shares out of a hundred.

7 stepsStart Problems

Real Life

Twenty-seven problems, one at a time. Each one asks you to pick the tool first and only then work it out, because choosing the method is the part that is actually hard.

27 problemsStart Geometry

Shapes and Angles

Triangles sorted by their sides, angles named by eye against a right angle, the 180° on a straight line, perimeter set against area, compound shapes cut into rectangles, nets folded into cubes, and a symmetrical pattern finished.

7 stepsStart Position

Where Things Are and How They Move

Coordinate pairs read the right way round, points plotted until they make a shape, a grid with its lines taken away, and shapes slid and reflected across a mirror line.

6 stepsStart Time

Time and How We Write It

Races timed to hundredths, the same moment on a 12-hour and a 24-hour clock, intervals that run past the hour in two hops, a duration written as a decimal, and clocks compared across time zones.

5 stepsStart Statistics

Data and Chance

A question worth surveying, a tally turned into a frequency table, a bar chart set against a dot plot, the mode and the median, what a chart does and does not prove, and a coin thrown ten times and a thousand.

7 stepsStart Calculation

Calculating with Wholes and Parts

Calculations rearranged by the laws of arithmetic, multiplication and division up to a thousand, fractions added once the pieces agree, a unit fraction of a whole number, and the decimal point kept where it belongs.

7 stepsStart Practice

Check What You Know

Thirty-six quick questions across the whole unit, twenty-nine things to take away, and the Cambridge mapping that says which stage every step belongs to.
