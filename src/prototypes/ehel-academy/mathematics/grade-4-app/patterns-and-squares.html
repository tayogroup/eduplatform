Patterns and Square Numbers

Warm up

1

Odd, even, and what happens

Pair the counters. One left over means odd. Now add two numbers and watch what the leftovers do.

2

Taking away, odd and even

Taking away follows a rule too, and it is the same rule: what matters is only whether each number has one left over when you pair it up.

3

The shape that hides a number

The shape stands for a number nobody has told you. The rest of the sum tells you what it has to be.

4

When the shape comes first

The shape does not always sit in the middle. When it comes first in a take-away, it is the whole amount that is hidden — so you have to put the two known parts back together.

5

The same shape twice

A shape stands for the same number everywhere it appears. So two of them make equal parts of the total, and a different shape is a different number.

6

What comes next?

Look at the step from each term to the next. Same step every time is linear. Changing step is non-linear.

7

Same step, or changing step?

The steps are not written in this time. Work them out yourself, then say whether they stay the same — linear — or change — non-linear.

8

Say the rule

Finding the next term gets you one more number. Saying the rule gets you all of them. A term-to-term rule says how to get from any term to the one after it.

9

Numbers that make squares

A square number is one whose dots make a perfect square. Watch what has to be added each time.

10

Between one square and the next

To turn one square into the next you add an L along two edges — a side, another side, and the one dot in the corner. Count it before you count the whole square.
