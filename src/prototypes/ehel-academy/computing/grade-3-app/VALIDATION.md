# Grade 3 Computing (computing/grade-3-v2) validation

Reviewed 2026-09-11; its fixes are committed and NOT yet deployed. Reviewer: Claude (automated measurement + judgement); no human teacher review yet.  
Against the 27-area framework in 'computing-grades validation.docx'. Scale: 1 Unsatisfactory; 2 Needs major improvement; 3 Acceptable with revisions; 4 Strong; 5 Excellent.  
What was reviewed: the standalone lesson build that ehel-comp-g03 launches (computing/grade-3-app, served as app/computing/grade-3-v2), not the Word-pack course under computing/grade-3, which the shell serves when no override applies and which declares a different framework.  

The Word report beside the owner's other validation reports (OneDrive: `computing-grade-3-v2 validation report.docx`) carries the same content; both are written from one script.

## Summary

The build teaches all 36 Cambridge Primary Computing 0059 Stage 3 objectives across fourteen lessons, more deeply than any other grade. A check by hand of every objective against the steps that claim it found each one taught through something the child does and nothing from Stage 4. One pair is only partly met: 3MD.02 and 3MD.03 name discrete and categorical data, and the lessons record and chart only a count (how many pets each child has); categorical data is defined and asked about, but no child records it or sees it drawn. 96 of the 102 teaching steps can tell a child “not quite”, each of the 144 questions has one key and a reason, and every lesson was played to the end in a browser after this review's fixes, with no errors and nothing wider than a phone screen.

Grade 3 already carries the Grade 1 validation's shared fixes, deployed on 2026-09-11. The one that mattered most was checked again here on the live pages: all fourteen lessons store every step, are recorded complete, and reopen where the learner left them.

Reading every learner-facing line found eleven kinds of error, all corrected. Lesson 3 taught that toast comes before butter “because butter melts on hot toast”, a reason that does not explain the order, in three places including a quiz key; it now says butter in a toaster would melt and burn. Its cereal activity marked “Boil the milk” as a step nobody needs, which many families take. Lesson 7's home hunt counts a doorbell among machines with a computer inside, when lesson 14 teaches that an old doorbell has none. Lesson 11 called a laptop's network cable “a cable to the wall socket”, which a child reads as the charger, in a sort and a quiz. Lesson 9 showed a child the objective code “3MD.03” in a quiz reason and answered a shoe-size question with “Count the feet”. The rest were smaller: a machine said to take “a WORD in” whose second round takes a number, a filter rule stated as true of one table only, a surprise “sent to your dad” that was for him, a padlock the most-used browser replaced in 2023, a reason that read “Cost is a cost.”, “programme” for program, and a plug “on a phone timer”.

Four home projects changed for safety. One asked a child, with no grown-up named, to change the inputs of a kettle and a toaster and see what the output did (it is now a torch, a light switch, a TV remote and a washing machine, with a grown-up); one had a grown-up follow the child's toast algorithm “silly mistakes and all” (it is now packing a school bag); a recipe to change and try, and a hunt for inputs along the street, named no grown-up (both now do).

The measurements found three things the reading could not. The Stage 3 machines had never been measured, and 64 of their controls were under 44 px on a phone: the cipher keys, the spreadsheet's column letters and choice chips, and the number chips inside program blocks; two label styles were 11 and 12 px. Eight pictures needed 2020 or 2021 devices, in 20 places, and the kit drew the shake input with a 2022 face that an older tablet shows as an empty box. All three are fixed, the last two in the shared kit. Contrast passes everywhere it was measured.

What is left is content work and work no measurement can do. Categorical data needs recording and a chart of its own. There is no recap of the lesson before and no warm-up, which Grades 1 and 2 now have. Seven activities repeat Grades 1 and 2: handwashing, planting a seed, getting dressed, the jam sandwich twice and a debug goal from Grade 1, tea and bedtime from Grade 2. A teacher needs to read the content and the question keys, somebody should listen to the cipher lesson's letter names and watch children use a lesson, and one should be walked with a screen reader. This review's fixes are committed and not deployed: the live Grade 3 pages carry the errors above until Grade 3 is redeployed.

Change status at 2026-09-11, fixes committed and not deployed: 10 items need no change or are done, 6 have their fix committed with a redeploy, a human check or an owner decision still open, 11 are open and not started.

Average score 4.1 / 5 across 27 areas; lowest 3 (Assessment Balance, Multimedia Quality, Duplication / overlap); 6 areas at 5.

## Before this review

Grade 3 was redeployed on 2026-09-11 with the Grade 1 validation's kit fixes: a finished lesson is stored complete and a reopened lesson opens where it was left (checked on all fourteen Grade 3 lessons on the live pages in this review), the blocks, the wifi toggle and the home cards' buttons are 44 px, the network map no longer cuts off its labels, and the Sort race game asks each sort step's own question. The Grade 2 validation's later kit change touched only drawings Grade 3 does not show. This review is about what is Grade 3's own, and about the Stage 3 machines (cipher, spreadsheet, filter, input machine, number chips) that the earlier reviews never measured.

## What was fixed during this review

Committed as `282c40c60` on 2026-09-11 and pushed; NOT deployed. Grades 1, 2 and 4 were rebuilt on the fixed kit: Grades 1 and 2's lesson data is unchanged, and Grade 4's differs by one picture (the shake sensor in lesson 7).

- Reasons and facts: toast comes before butter because butter in a toaster would melt and burn, not because “butter melts on hot toast” (lesson 3, three places including a quiz key and a game); a TV, not a doorbell, is among the machines with a computer inside (lesson 7), since lesson 14 teaches that an old doorbell has none; “age is more than 7” selects the rows where the age is “8 or more”, not “8 or 9” (lesson 10); a birthday surprise is “for your dad, sent to your mum” (lesson 12); the word machine takes a word in, “and then one that takes a number” (lesson 4).
- Words a child would misread: “a laptop with a cable to the wall socket” is now “a network cable in the wall” (lesson 11: a sort, its game and a quiz); “a plug on a phone timer” is “a plug you switch on from your phone” (lesson 14); “programme” is “program” (lesson 14).
- Feedback: “Count the feet” (ordering size-1 shoes) is now “Ask each child their shoe size, then count the size 1s”; a quiz reason that named the objective code 3MD.03 to the child, on a stem that restated its own answer, is a new question (lesson 9); “Cost is a cost.” now gives the reason (lesson 11).
- Activities: the cereal algorithm's unneeded step “Boil the milk”, which many families take, is now “Put on your wellies” (lesson 3); the padlock home project says some browsers show a different icon there, and no longer asks for a page without one (lesson 12); the factory-line home project lists paper, scissors, glue and crayons instead of cards (lesson 14).
- Safety: “Spot the input” asked the child to change the inputs of a kettle and a toaster, with no grown-up named; it now uses a torch, a light switch, a TV remote and a washing machine, with a grown-up (lesson 4). The “Follow it exactly” home project, where a grown-up follows the child's steps “silly mistakes and all”, uses packing a school bag instead of making toast (lesson 1). “Predict, then try” (changing a recipe and trying it, lesson 3) and the input hunt along the street (lesson 13) name a grown-up.
- Emoji: eight 2020–2021 pictures in 20 places became older ones that say the same thing (a lift became a building, bubble tea a hot drink, a mirror a pair of eyes, a bucket a shower head for the watering can, a toothbrush a tooth, a pot plant a pot or a seedling, a slide a cartwheel, a wheel a car); the kit's “Shake it” face (Emoji 15.0, 2022) became a vibrating phone, in the kit and in the lesson 7 of Grades 3 and 4.
- Tap targets and labels (shared kit): cipher keys from 38 × 40 to 44 × 44; spreadsheet column letters and choice chips to at least 44 px; number chips in program blocks to 44 px without making the block taller; the code strip's numbers from 11 to 13 px and the machines' INPUT and OUTPUT from 12 to 13 px. Only Grades 3 and 4 draw these controls. The kit README now records the size rule for the Stage 3 controls and a rule that pictures are Emoji 12.0 (2019) or older.

## Verdict on Grade 3 Computing

Nothing found stops the children already using Grade 3. Its fourteen lessons were played to the end on the live pages before this review changed anything, and every record was complete. The errors this review found are corrected in the repository and wait for a redeploy; none of them costs a learner their progress. The build teaches all 36 Stage 3 objectives through activities, more deeply than any other grade, and holds the Stage 4 line.

Categorical data is the one place the objectives ask for more than the lessons do. That, a recap and a warm-up, and new tasks for the seven activities borrowed from Grades 1 and 2 are the content work left. Before it is called validated rather than measured, a teacher should read the content and the question keys, somebody should hear the cipher lesson and watch a child use a lesson, and one should be walked with a screen reader.
